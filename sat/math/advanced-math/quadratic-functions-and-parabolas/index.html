Quadratic Functions and Parabolas

  • Advanced Math
  • Nonlinear functions
←PreviousAll Math lessonsNext→

The graph of a quadratic function is a parabola. Decide first which feature the question wants, because each of the three forms displays a different one.

Key takeaways

  • Standard form y=ax2+bx+cy = ax^2 + bx + cy=ax2+bx+c displays the y-intercept as ccc.
  • Factored form y=a(x−p)(x−q)y = a(x - p)(x - q)y=a(x−p)(x−q) displays the two zeros as ppp and qqq.
  • Vertex form y=a(x−h)2+ky = a(x - h)^2 + ky=a(x−h)2+k displays the vertex as (h,k)(h, k)(h,k).
  • The sign of aaa says which way the parabola opens, and its size sets how narrow the curve is.
  • From standard form the axis of symmetry is x=−b2ax = \frac{-b}{2a}x=2a−b​, and where there are two zeros it runs midway between them.
  • The vertex sits on the axis of symmetry, so its height is the function at that input.
  • Two inputs the same distance from the axis give the same output.

What the SAT may ask

  • Find the vertex of a parabola from any of its three forms.
  • Give the equation of the axis of symmetry.
  • Read the zeros or the y-intercept off an equation or a graph.
  • Match a graph of a parabola to its equation.
  • Find the greatest or least value a quadratic model reaches.
  • Say which form displays a stated feature as a constant.

The rule

A quadratic function is one that can be written ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c with aaa not zero, once its terms are collected. Its graph is a parabola: a symmetric curve with one turning point, called the vertex. The vertical line through the vertex is the axis of symmetry.

Standard and vertex form exist for every quadratic; a real factored form needs real zeros. Each form displays a different feature. All three equations below draw the parabola in the figure, which crosses the axis twice; y=x2+1y = x^2 + 1y=x2+1 has a vertex form and no factored form over the real numbers.

y=−2x2+4x+6y = -2x^2 + 4x + 6y=−2x2+4x+6 y=−2(x+1)(x−3)y = -2(x + 1)(x - 3)y=−2(x+1)(x−3) y=−2(x−1)2+8y = -2(x - 1)^2 + 8y=−2(x−1)2+8
Form What it displays
Standard, y=ax2+bx+cy = ax^2 + bx + cy=ax2+bx+c The y-intercept, ccc

Vertex form subtracts hhh, so read the sign carefully. In −2(x−1)2+8-2(x - 1)^2 + 8−2(x−1)2+8 the vertex is at x=1x = 1x=1. A plus inside the parentheses would put it on the negative side instead.

The leading coefficient aaa decides the direction. Positive opens upward, so the vertex is the lowest point and kkk is a minimum. Negative opens downward, so the vertex is the highest point and kkk is a maximum. A larger ∣a∣|a|∣a∣ makes the curve narrower.

From standard form, the axis of symmetry is x=−b2ax = \frac{-b}{2a}x=2a−b​. For the curve above that is −4−4\frac{-4}{-4}−4−4​, which is 111. Put that input back into the function to get the vertex's height.

From factored form, the axis runs midway between the zeros. The zeros above are −1-1−1 and 333, and their midpoint is 111.

Setting x=0x = 0x=0 gives the y-intercept from any form. In standard form that is just the constant term.

A closed range has two extremes and the vertex can hold only one. The vertex is the maximum when aaa is negative and the minimum when aaa is positive, and only while its input is in range. The other extreme always sits at an endpoint. So compare both endpoints with the vertex whenever the range admits it.

A change written outside the squared part moves the output and one written inside moves the input, so f(x−h)+kf(x - h) + kf(x−h)+k shifts the graph hhh right and kkk up. Polynomials and Function Notation carries that rule for functions in general.

Why the rule exists

A parabola is symmetric because the squared term treats equal distances alike. Two inputs the same distance from the axis, one on each side, square to the same number, so they produce the same output. The turning point is the one input with no partner.

That symmetry is why the axis is midway between the zeros. Both zeros give an output of zero, so they are the same distance from the axis on opposite sides.

The formula x=−b2ax = \frac{-b}{2a}x=2a−b​ is the same fact written for standard form. It is also why a quadratic has one greatest or least value over all inputs: the curve turns once, then moves away from the turn in both directions. Over a restricted range the turn may sit outside it, which is why the endpoints have to be compared there.

Worked examples

1. Find the vertex from standard form

The function fff is defined by f(x)=2x2−8x+1f(x) = 2x^2 - 8x + 1f(x)=2x2−8x+1. What are the coordinates of the vertex of the graph of y=f(x)y = f(x)y=f(x)?

Question asks: the vertex, as a point.

First move: the function is in standard form, so the axis of symmetry comes from −b2a\frac{-b}{2a}2a−b​, with aaa as 222 and bbb as −8-8−8.

x=−(−8)2(2)x = \frac{-(-8)}{2(2)}x=2(2)−(−8)​ x=2x = 2x=2

The vertex sits on that line, so substitute the input back into the function.

f(2)=2(4)−16+1f(2) = 2(4) - 16 + 1f(2)=2(4)−16+1 f(2)=−7f(2) = -7f(2)=−7

Answer: (2,−7)(2, -7)(2,−7)

Check: symmetry did the work, so test two inputs the same distance from 222.

f(1)=2−8+1=−5f(1) = 2 - 8 + 1 = -5f(1)=2−8+1=−5 f(3)=18−24+1=−5f(3) = 18 - 24 + 1 = -5f(3)=18−24+1=−5

Common mistake: answering 222. That is where the vertex sits, not how high it is.

3. Build the equation from two zeros and one more point

In the xyxyxy-plane, a parabola crosses the xxx-axis at x=−4x = -4x=−4 and x=2x = 2x=2, and it passes through the point (4,32)(4, 32)(4,32). What equation defines the parabola?

Question asks: an equation for the parabola.

First move: two zeros are given, so build factored form and leave the leading coefficient unknown. A factor is zero at the opposite sign, so a zero at −4-4−4 comes from the factor x+4x + 4x+4.

y=a(x+4)(x−2)y = a(x + 4)(x - 2)y=a(x+4)(x−2)

The given point lies on the curve, so substituting it has to make the equation true.

32=a(8)(2)32 = a(8)(2)32=a(8)(2) 32=16a32 = 16a32=16a a=2a = 2a=2

Answer: y=2(x+4)(x−2)y = 2(x + 4)(x - 2)y=2(x+4)(x−2)

Check: a point fixed the constant, so put that point back into the finished equation.

2(8)(2)=322(8)(2) = 322(8)(2)=32

Common mistake: writing y=2(x−4)(x+2)y = 2(x - 4)(x + 2)y=2(x−4)(x+2). That curve crosses the xxx-axis at 444 and at −2-2−2, which are the two zeros with their signs flipped.

Special cases

Parabolas with one zero or none, and what changing the leading coefficient leaves alone

A parabola does not have to cross the xxx-axis, and the vertex says whether it does.

An upward parabola whose vertex is above the xxx-axis has no zeros at all. Its lowest point is already above the axis, so nothing on the curve reaches it. A downward parabola whose vertex is below the axis is the same case upside down. Such a function has no factored form over the real numbers.

A vertex sitting on the xxx-axis gives one zero rather than two. The curve touches the axis and turns back, and the two factors are the same factor twice.

Changing aaa moves less than it looks. In vertex form, changing aaa stretches the curve and leaves the vertex where it is. In factored form, changing aaa leaves both zeros where they are. In neither case does the axis of symmetry move.

Common traps

Reading a number out of the wrong slot, or with the wrong sign

Two things go wrong when a number is read off a form: the slot and the sign. Each row below is a number that is correct where it stands and wrong where it was used.

Trap Quick check
Reading hhh as the number printed inside the parentheses The form subtracts hhh, so x+6x + 6x+6 puts the vertex at −6-6−6
Taking the constant of a factor for a zero The factor x+5x + 5x+5 is zero at −5-5−5
Answering with the axis when the ask is a greatest or least value The axis is an input; a greatest or least value is an output
Reading the standard-form constant as the vertex's height That constant is where the graph meets the yyy-axis

The method: Ask, form, read or move, check

  1. Name the ask. The vertex, the axis of symmetry, a zero, the y-intercept, which way the curve opens, a greatest or least value, or an equation. Write it down first, because each one is displayed by a different form.
  2. Name the form in front of you. Standard, factored, vertex, or a graph. The sign of aaa gives the direction from any of the three written forms, and the picture gives it directly.
  3. Read it off, or move to the form that shows it. Vertex form hands over the vertex. Standard form gives the axis through −b2a\frac{-b}{2a}2a−b​, and that input returns the vertex's height. Factored form gives the zeros, with the axis midway between them. From a graph, write vertex form and let a second point fix aaa.
  4. Check, then answer the ask. Three situations call for one. Symmetry doing the work: test two inputs the same distance from the axis, which must give the same output. A point fixing a constant: put a point back. A closed range: compare both endpoints with the vertex when its input is in range. Then answer the ask, with its units.

Read a feature off the graph

A graphing calculator is built into the testing app and is allowed on every Math question.

Type the equation as it stands and look at the curve. The calculator marks the turning point and the intercepts, so a question that asks for one of them becomes a matter of reading a point off the screen.

By hand is faster when the equation is already in the form that displays what the question wants.
