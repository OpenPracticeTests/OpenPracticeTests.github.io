Quadratic Equations

  • Advanced Math
  • Nonlinear equations in one variable and systems of equations in two variables
←PreviousAll Math lessonsNext→

A quadratic equation is one whose highest power is 222 once every term is moved to one side and collected. Four routes solve it, and the first decision is which route the equation in front of you already fits.

Key takeaways

  • Write the equation as ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0 unless one side is a square and the other a number.
  • A product equals zero only when one of its factors equals zero.
  • A square equal to a positive number gives two solutions, to zero one, and to a negative none.
  • The formula x=−b±b2−4ac2ax = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}x=2a−b±b2−4ac​​ solves every quadratic.
  • The discriminant b2−4acb^2 - 4acb2−4ac counts real solutions: positive gives two, zero gives one, negative gives none.
  • A line and a parabola meet where their two expressions for yyy are equal.
  • A situation can rule out a solution the equation allows.

What the SAT may ask

  • Solve a quadratic that factors with whole numbers.
  • Solve a quadratic with no simple integer factor pair.
  • Say how many real solutions an equation has.
  • Find a constant from a solution you are given.
  • Find where a line crosses a parabola.

The rule

A quadratic equation is any equation that can be written

ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0

with aaa not zero. Read the degree after moving every term to one side, not before: each side of x2+3=x2+xx^2 + 3 = x^2 + xx2+3=x2+x carries a square, and collecting leaves 3−x=03 - x = 03−x=0, which is linear.

Getting it to that form is the first move for three of the four routes. The exception is a side that is already a square, with a number on the other.

Factoring. When the equation sits at zero and the factors are whole numbers, each factor gives a solution. From x2+6x+8=0x^2 + 6x + 8 = 0x2+6x+8=0:

(x+2)(x+4)=0(x + 2)(x + 4) = 0(x+2)(x+4)=0

The solutions are −2-2−2 and −4-4−4. A product is zero only when one of its factors is zero, which is why the right side has to be zero first.

When the coefficient of x2x^2x2 is not 111, split the middle term first. Find two numbers that multiply to the first coefficient times the constant and add to the middle one. Use them to write the middle term as two terms, then group the four in pairs and pull out the binomial both pairs leave.

Taking the square root. When one side is a square and the other is a number, undo the square. Keep both signs. From (x−2)2=25(x - 2)^2 = 25(x−2)2=25:

x−2=±5x - 2 = \pm 5x−2=±5

The solutions are 777 and −3-3−3. The ±\pm± is yours, not the radical's. The square root symbol asks for the nonnegative root, and the second branch appears because a squared quantity loses the sign of what was squared.

What the number on the right decides. A positive number gives two solutions, zero gives one, and a negative gives none, since no real square is negative.

Completing the square. This route needs the coefficient of x2x^2x2 to be 111, so divide every term by it first if it is not. Move the constant to the other side, then halve the coefficient of xxx, square that, and add it to both sides, which leaves the left side a square.

The quadratic formula. This one always works.

x=−b±b2−4ac2ax = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}x=2a−b±b2−4ac​​

Read aaa, bbb and ccc off the equation only once it is at zero.

The discriminant. The part under the radical, b2−4acb^2 - 4acb2−4ac, counts the real solutions on its own.

Discriminant Real solutions
Positive Two
Zero One
Negative None

The same three cases are what the graph shows: the curve meets the horizontal axis twice, touches it once, or misses it.

Where ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0 has two real solutions, they add to −ba\frac{-b}{a}a−b​ and multiply to ca\frac{c}{a}ac​. Both divide by aaa, so on 3x2+8x−5=03x^2 + 8x - 5 = 03x2+8x−5=0 they come to −83\frac{-8}{3}3−8​ and −53\frac{-5}{3}3−5​ rather than −8-8−8 and −5-5−5. Neither needs the solutions themselves, so either one checks a pair you have already found.

A line and a parabola. Two graphs meet where their yyy values agree, so set the two expressions equal. What comes out is one quadratic, its solutions are the xxx-coordinates of the crossings, and its discriminant counts them.

Why the rule exists

Factoring rests on one fact about numbers: a product is zero only when a factor is zero. That is what turns one equation into two, and it is why factoring is worth the search for a whole-number pair.

The square root route rests on a second fact: a positive number has two square roots, and they differ only in sign. Drop one and a real solution goes with it.

The formula is completing the square done once, in general, on ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0. That is why it never fails. Completing the square there leaves b2−4acb^2 - 4acb2−4ac over 4a24a^24a2 on the right. A square denominator cannot change a sign, so the discriminant alone decides how many roots there are.

Worked examples

1. Move every term to one side before factoring

Solve x2+7x=18x^2 + 7x = 18x2+7x=18.

Question asks: both solutions.

First move: the equation is not at zero, so the factors say nothing yet. Subtract 181818 from both sides.

x2+7x−18=0x^2 + 7x - 18 = 0x2+7x−18=0

Now look for two numbers that multiply to −18-18−18 and add to 777. Those are 999 and −2-2−2.

(x+9)(x−2)=0(x + 9)(x - 2) = 0(x+9)(x−2)=0

Set each factor equal to zero.

x+9=0x + 9 = 0x+9=0 x=−9x = -9x=−9 x−2=0x - 2 = 0x−2=0 x=2x = 2x=2

Answer: −9-9−9 and 222

Check: the equation was not set to zero when it arrived, so put each solution back into it as it was written.

81−63=1881 - 63 = 1881−63=18 4+14=184 + 14 = 184+14=18

Common mistake: reading the solutions off the factors as 999 and −2-2−2. A factor of x+9x + 9x+9 is zero when xxx is −9-9−9, not when it is 999.

2. Build the square when no whole-number pair works

Solve x2+10x−4=0x^2 + 10x - 4 = 0x2+10x−4=0.

Question asks: both solutions.

First move: no whole numbers multiply to −4-4−4 and add to 101010, so build a square instead. Move the constant to the right.

x2+10x=4x^2 + 10x = 4x2+10x=4

Halve the coefficient of xxx, square it, and add that to both sides. Half of 101010 is 555, and 555 squared is 252525.

x2+10x+25=29x^2 + 10x + 25 = 29x2+10x+25=29

The left side is now a square.

(x+5)2=29(x + 5)^2 = 29(x+5)2=29

Take the square root of both sides, keeping both signs.

x+5=±29x + 5 = \pm \sqrt{29}x+5=±29​ x=−5±29x = -5 \pm \sqrt{29}x=−5±29​

Answer: −5+29-5 + \sqrt{29}−5+29​ and −5−29-5 - \sqrt{29}−5−29​

Check: the pair is irrational, so test it against the coefficients rather than substituting a radical twice. The two add to −10-10−10, which is −ba\frac{-b}{a}a−b​, and multiply to 25−2925 - 2925−29, which is −4-4−4 and is ca\frac{c}{a}ac​. That checks the rearrangement as well as the root.

Common mistake: adding 252525 to the left side only. The equation stays true only when both sides receive it.

3. Build the equation from an area, then drop the impossible solution

A rectangular rug is 444 feet longer than it is wide. Its area is 212121 square feet. How wide is the rug, in feet?

Question asks: the width, in feet.

First move: name the unknown and write the area. Let www be the width, so the length is w+4w + 4w+4.

w(w+4)=21w(w + 4) = 21w(w+4)=21

Expand, then move every term to one side.

w2+4w=21w^2 + 4w = 21w2+4w=21 w2+4w−21=0w^2 + 4w - 21 = 0w2+4w−21=0

Two numbers multiply to −21-21−21 and add to 444: they are 777 and −3-3−3.

(w+7)(w−3)=0(w + 7)(w - 3) = 0(w+7)(w−3)=0

The two solutions are −7-7−7 and 333. A rug cannot have a negative width, so −7-7−7 is out.

Answer: 333 feet

Check: the equation was built rather than handed over at zero, so test the surviving solution against the situation. A width of 333 gives a length of 777.

3×7=213 \times 7 = 213×7=21

Common mistake: answering 777. That is the length, and the question asked for the width.

Special cases

Solutions the equation allows and the problem does not

A situation can rule one out. A width, a count of people, and a length of time cannot be negative. A negative solution is discarded on that ground rather than on an algebraic one, so say why you dropped it.

Dividing by the variable throws one away. Given x2=5xx^2 = 5xx2=5x, dividing both sides by xxx leaves a single answer and hides the solution 000. Move every term to one side and factor out xxx instead, and both solutions survive.

A repeated factor means one solution, not two. When the discriminant is zero the two branches of the square root land on the same number.

A constant inside the equation moves the count. In x2−6x+k=0x^2 - 6x + k = 0x2−6x+k=0 the discriminant is 36−4k36 - 4k36−4k, so there are two solutions while kkk is below 999, one at 999, and none above it. Where a constant sits in the leading coefficient instead, the value that makes that coefficient zero is a separate case: the equation is no longer quadratic there.

Common traps

Correct work stopped one step early

A single root, a factor's constant, and the number you were about to divide all look like finished answers.

Trap Quick check
Keeping only the positive square root One square, two branches
Reading solutions straight off the factors Each factor is zero at the opposite sign
Using the zero-product rule on a product equal to something else Move every term to one side first
Dividing only the first term of the formula's numerator The whole numerator is divided by 2a2a2a

The method: Ask, arrange, route, check

  1. Name the ask. One solution, both solutions, how many solutions, a constant, or a point. Write it down before you start, because a count comes off the discriminant and a constant is usually found by substituting rather than by solving for xxx.
  2. Arrange the equation. Move every term to one side so that it reads ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0, unless one side is already a square and the other is a number. A product equal to anything other than zero tells you nothing about its factors until you have done this.
  3. Choose the route and run it. A square equal to a number: take the square root and keep both signs. A whole-number factor pair: set each factor equal to zero. Neither: complete the square, or use the formula. A count of solutions: compute b2−4acb^2 - 4acb2−4ac and stop there.
  4. Check, then answer the ask. Four things call for one. They are an equation you solved that arrived not at zero, a square root on both sides, an equation you built, and irrational solutions. Put every solution you keep back in, or, for an irrational pair, test it against −ba\frac{-b}{a}a−b​ and ca\frac{c}{a}ac​. Drop what the situation rules out.

Graph to check a solution

A graphing calculator is built into the testing app and is allowed on every Math question.

Move every term to one side, graph that expression, and read where the curve meets the horizontal axis, crossing it or touching it. Those meetings are the real solutions, and their count is what the discriminant would have told you.

A window that shows none does not establish none, since the curve may meet the axis outside it. By hand is faster when the equation already sits at zero and the factor pair is small.

Practice

Work each item with the method, under a rough clock: about a minute for a bare equation, and up to about two minutes once there is a context to translate or a graph or table to read first. The Math section gives you about 95 seconds a question on average, so that range is test-day pace rather than a drill speed. Press Show solution to see the steps, one to a line.

What are all the solutions to (x−6)2=4(x - 6)^2 = 4(x−6)2=4?

Correct. Taking the square root of both sides gives two branches, and adding 666 to each returns 444 and 888.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is every solution, so both branches of the root are part of the answer.

Step 2. One side is already a square and the other is a number, so nothing has to move.

Step 3. Take the square root of both sides, keeping both signs.

x−6=±2x - 6 = \pm 2x−6=±2

Add 666 to each branch.

x=6+2x = 6 + 2x=6+2 x=6−2x = 6 - 2x=6−2

The two solutions are 888 and 444.

Step 4. You took the square root of both sides, so put each solution back. Both (8−6)2(8 - 6)^2(8−6)2 and (4−6)2(4 - 6)^2(4−6)2 come to 444, so the solutions are 444 and 888.

One solution to the equation below is x=2x = 2x=2, where kkk is a constant. What is the value of kkk?

x2+kx−12=0x^2 + kx - 12 = 0x2+kx−12=0
Correct. Substituting the stated solution leaves 2k=82k = 82k=8, and kkk is 444.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is the constant kkk, not a solution. A solution is given.

Step 2. The equation is already at zero, so substitute the stated solution into it as it stands.

Step 3. Put 222 in place of xxx.

4+2k−12=04 + 2k - 12 = 04+2k−12=0 2k−8=02k - 8 = 02k−8=0 2k=82k = 82k=8 k=4k = 4k=4

Step 4. The equation arrived at zero and a solution was handed over, so the substitution is the whole of the work. The value of kkk is 444.

What is the lesser of the two solutions to the equation below?

2x2−7x+3=02x^2 - 7x + 3 = 02x2−7x+3=0
Correct. The factor 2x−12x - 12x−1 gives the lesser solution, 12\frac{1}{2}21​.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is the lesser solution, so both have to be found before either can be reported.

Step 2. The equation is already at zero.

Step 3. The leading coefficient is 222, so one factor carries it. Two numbers multiply to 666 and add to −7-7−7: they are −1-1−1 and −6-6−6. Split the middle term with them.

2x2−x−6x+3=02x^2 - x - 6x + 3 = 02x2−x−6x+3=0

Take xxx out of the first pair and −3-3−3 out of the second.

(2x−1)(x−3)=0(2x - 1)(x - 3) = 0(2x−1)(x−3)=0

Set each factor equal to zero.

2x−1=02x - 1 = 02x−1=0 x=12x = \frac{1}{2}x=21​ x−3=0x - 3 = 0x−3=0 x=3x = 3x=3

Step 4. Both factors gave a solution, and the ask is the smaller of the two. The lesser solution is 12\frac{1}{2}21​.

Which of the following equations has no real solutions?

Correct. Its discriminant is 9−169 - 169−16, which is negative, so no real number satisfies it.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a count of real solutions, so nothing has to be solved.

Step 2. Three of the four equations already sit at zero. The fourth does not, so move its constant across before reading any coefficient off it.

x2+4x−6=0x^2 + 4x - 6 = 0x2+4x−6=0

Step 3. Compute b2−4acb^2 - 4acb2−4ac for each equation. Where the constant is negative the two parts add.

9+16=259 + 16 = 259+16=25

The rearranged equation adds as well.

16+24=4016 + 24 = 4016+24=40

In one of them the two parts are equal.

16−16=016 - 16 = 016−16=0

That leaves one equation, and its discriminant is negative.

9−16=−79 - 16 = -79−16=−7

Step 4. Nothing was solved for, so the count is the answer. Only x2+3x+4=0x^2 + 3x + 4 = 0x2+3x+4=0 has a negative discriminant, so it is the equation with no real solutions.

In the xyxyxy-plane, the graphs of the two equations below cross at two points. What are the coordinates of the crossing with the greater xxx-coordinate?

y=x2+x−2y = x^2 + x - 2y=x2+x−2 y=6x+4y = 6x + 4y=6x+4
Correct. The greater solution of the reduced quadratic is 666, and the line puts yyy at 404040.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a point, so an xxx and a yyy are both needed.

Step 2. Neither equation is at zero. The graphs cross where the two expressions for yyy agree, so set them equal and move every term to one side.

x2+x−2=6x+4x^2 + x - 2 = 6x + 4x2+x−2=6x+4 x2−5x−6=0x^2 - 5x - 6 = 0x2−5x−6=0

Step 3. Two numbers multiply to −6-6−6 and add to −5-5−5: they are −6-6−6 and 111.

(x−6)(x+1)=0(x - 6)(x + 1) = 0(x−6)(x+1)=0

The two xxx-coordinates are 666 and −1-1−1, and the greater is 666. Put it into the line.

y=6(6)+4y = 6(6) + 4y=6(6)+4 y=40y = 40y=40

Step 4. Neither equation arrived at zero, so check the point in the equation that did not produce it. The parabola at 666 gives 36+6−236 + 6 - 236+6−2, which is 404040, so the crossing is (6,40)(6, 40)(6,40).
