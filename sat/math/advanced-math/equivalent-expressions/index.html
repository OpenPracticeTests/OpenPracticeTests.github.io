Equivalent Expressions

  • Advanced Math
  • Equivalent expressions
←PreviousAll Math lessonsNext→

Two expressions are equivalent when they give the same value for every input the variable is allowed to take. Decide first which form the question wants: factored, expanded, one fraction, or one power.

Key takeaways

  • Rewriting changes the form, never the value, and never restores an input the original barred.
  • Factoring and expanding are one move run in opposite directions.
  • Multiplying powers of one base adds the exponents; a power of a power multiplies them.
  • A negative exponent means a reciprocal; it does not put a minus on the value.
  • Only a factor of the whole top and the whole bottom cancels, never a single term.
  • Two fractions add or subtract only over one common denominator.
  • Check a rewrite by running the move backward, or by testing one allowed input.

What the SAT may ask

  • Choose the expression equivalent to a given product or quotient.
  • Factor a polynomial completely.
  • Write a sum or difference of two rational expressions as a single fraction.
  • Rewrite a power that carries a negative or fractional exponent.
  • Find the constant that makes two forms equivalent.

The rule

Two expressions are equivalent when they produce the same value for every allowed input. Every rule below preserves the value and changes only the appearance.

Expanding and factoring are one move run in two directions. Expanding multiplies every term of one factor by every term of the other.

(a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd(a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd(a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd

Squaring a binomial keeps a middle term, which is that line with both factors the same.

(a±b)2=a2±2ab+b2(a \pm b)^2 = a^2 \pm 2ab + b^2(a±b)2=a2±2ab+b2

Factoring runs the expansion backward, through one of three patterns.

Pattern Factored form
A common monomial 15x3+10x=5x(3x2+2)15x^3 + 10x = 5x(3x^2 + 2)15x3+10x=5x(3x2+2)
A difference of squares x2−81=(x+9)(x−9)x^2 - 81 = (x + 9)(x - 9)x2−81=(x+9)(x−9)
A trinomial x2+9x+14=(x+2)(x+7)x^2 + 9x + 14 = (x + 2)(x + 7)x2+9x+14=(x+2)(x+7)

With four terms and nothing common to all four, try grouping. Pull a common factor out of the first pair and out of the second pair. It works when the same binomial is left over, and not every four-term expression obliges.

x3+3x2+2x+6=x2(x+3)+2(x+3)x^3 + 3x^2 + 2x + 6 = x^2(x + 3) + 2(x + 3)x3+3x2+2x+6=x2(x+3)+2(x+3) x2(x+3)+2(x+3)=(x+3)(x2+2)x^2(x + 3) + 2(x + 3) = (x + 3)(x^2 + 2)x2(x+3)+2(x+3)=(x+3)(x2+2)

The exponent rules count copies of the base.

Rule What it does
x5⋅x3=x8x^5 \cdot x^3 = x^8x5⋅x3=x8 A product of like bases adds the exponents
x8x5=x3\dfrac{x^8}{x^5} = x^3x5x8​=x3 A quotient of like bases subtracts them
(x2)5=x10(x^2)^5 = x^{10}(x2)5=x10 A power of a power multiplies them
x−4=1x4x^{-4} = \dfrac{1}{x^4}x−4=x41​ A negative exponent sends the power across the bar
x0=1x^0 = 1x0=1 for x≠0x \ne 0x=0 Any nonzero base to the zero power is 111

A fractional exponent is a root, with the index underneath: x3/4x^{3/4}x3/4 is x34\sqrt[4]{x^3}4x3​. A root reaches every factor inside it, so 8x63\sqrt[3]{8x^6}38x6​ is 2x22x^22x2.

A negative exponent moves the power across the fraction bar and changes no sign. It asks for a reciprocal, and the value underneath can still be negative: (−2)−3(-2)^{-3}(−2)−3 is −18\frac{-1}{8}8−1​.

Some of these rules carry a restriction. A base under a negative power is nonzero, and an even root needs a nonnegative radicand to be real. The fractional-exponent identities are written for a nonnegative base, which is why x2\sqrt{x^2}x2​ is ∣x∣|x|∣x∣ rather than xxx. The whole-number exponent rules hold for any nonzero base, negative ones included.

Two fractions combine only over one common denominator. Multiplying the denominators always reaches one; when they share a factor the least common denominator is smaller and the scaling is lighter. Scale each numerator by whatever its own denominator was missing, then add or subtract the numerators.

ab+cd=ad+bcbd\frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad + bc}{bd}ba​+dc​=bdad+bc​

One fraction reduces only by canceling a factor of the whole top and the whole bottom. Factor both first, because a term is not a factor.

Canceling does not give an input back. Write down the values that make the original denominator zero before you cancel: the two forms agree only where both are defined, so those values stay barred. Reducing x(x+7)(x−4)(x+7)\frac{x(x + 7)}{(x - 4)(x + 7)}(x−4)(x+7)x(x+7)​ to xx−4\frac{x}{x - 4}x−4x​ leaves 444 and −7-7−7 both excluded, though only 444 still shows.

Undoing a power while SOLVING can hand back a barred value the same way, which Radical, Rational, and Absolute Value Equations checks for.

A question can ask for a rewrite that puts one quantity in view. Splitting a numerator into a multiple of the denominator plus what is left over turns a fraction into a constant plus a smaller fraction. That constant is then readable.

Why the rule exists

The distributive law is the first of two facts behind every rule here: multiplying a sum means multiplying each part of it. Expanding runs it forward and factoring runs it backward, which is why any factored form can be checked by multiplying out.

The second is what a power counts. x6x^6x6 is six copies of xxx multiplied together, so a product stacks the copies and a quotient cancels them in pairs.

A negative exponent records copies canceled past zero, which is why the power lands in the denominator, and why the sign of the base is untouched by it.

A fractional exponent records a root, because a power of a power multiplies the exponents. With xxx at or above zero, (x1/2)2(x^{1/2})^2(x1/2)2 is x(1/2)⋅2x^{(1/2) \cdot 2}x(1/2)⋅2, which is xxx, so x1/2x^{1/2}x1/2 is the nonnegative number whose square is xxx. Taking a square root twice is a different move and gives x1/4x^{1/4}x1/4.

Worked examples

1. Expand a product, then subtract a second polynomial

Which expression is equivalent to (4x+3)(x−2)−(2x2−5)(4x + 3)(x - 2) - (2x^2 - 5)(4x+3)(x−2)−(2x2−5)?

Question asks: one expanded polynomial.

First move: expand the product before touching the subtraction, because the subtraction applies to the whole second grouping.

(4x+3)(x−2)=4x2−8x+3x−6(4x + 3)(x - 2) = 4x^2 - 8x + 3x - 6(4x+3)(x−2)=4x2−8x+3x−6 4x2−8x+3x−6=4x2−5x−64x^2 - 8x + 3x - 6 = 4x^2 - 5x - 64x2−8x+3x−6=4x2−5x−6

Now subtract. The minus reaches both terms inside the second grouping, so the −5-5−5 becomes +5+5+5.

4x2−5x−6−2x2+5=2x2−5x−14x^2 - 5x - 6 - 2x^2 + 5 = 2x^2 - 5x - 14x2−5x−6−2x2+5=2x2−5x−1

Answer: 2x2−5x−12x^2 - 5x - 12x2−5x−1

Check: an expansion runs backward only by factoring, which is harder than the expansion was, so test one input instead. At 222 the product is 111111 times 000, and taking away 333 leaves −3-3−3; the answer gives 8−10−18 - 10 - 18−10−1, which is −3-3−3 as well.

Common mistake: leaving the −5-5−5 negative gives 2x2−5x−112x^2 - 5x - 112x2−5x−11, which is the minus applied to the first term of the grouping only.

2. Pull out a common monomial, then factor what is left

Factor 50x3−18x50x^3 - 18x50x3−18x completely.

Question asks: a complete factoring, so nothing factorable may be left inside.

First move: take out what both terms share. Both coefficients are even, and both terms carry an xxx.

50x3−18x=2x(25x2−9)50x^3 - 18x = 2x(25x^2 - 9)50x3−18x=2x(25x2−9)

What remains is a difference of two squares, since 25x225x^225x2 is the square of 5x5x5x and 999 is the square of 333.

2x(25x2−9)=2x(5x−3)(5x+3)2x(25x^2 - 9) = 2x(5x - 3)(5x + 3)2x(25x2−9)=2x(5x−3)(5x+3)

Answer: 2x(5x−3)(5x+3)2x(5x - 3)(5x + 3)2x(5x−3)(5x+3)

Check: the move was factoring, so run it backward. The two binomials multiply to 25x2−925x^2 - 925x2−9, and 2x2x2x times that returns the expression you started with.

Common mistake: stopping at 2x(25x2−9)2x(25x^2 - 9)2x(25x2−9) leaves a difference of squares inside, so the factoring is not complete.

3. Split a numerator to reveal a constant

Write 4x+15x+2\dfrac{4x + 15}{x + 2}x+24x+15​, where x≠−2x \ne -2x=−2, as a constant plus a fraction whose numerator is a number.

Question asks: a rewritten form, not a value of xxx.

First move: ask how many whole copies of the denominator fit inside the numerator. The numerator leads with 4x4x4x and the denominator leads with xxx, so try four copies.

4(x+2)=4x+84(x + 2) = 4x + 84(x+2)=4x+8

That is 777 short of the numerator, so write the numerator as those four copies plus the leftover.

4x+15=4(x+2)+74x + 15 = 4(x + 2) + 74x+15=4(x+2)+7

Divide each piece by the denominator. The first piece cancels completely.

4(x+2)+7x+2=4+7x+2\frac{4(x + 2) + 7}{x + 2} = 4 + \frac{7}{x + 2}x+24(x+2)+7​=4+x+27​

Answer: 4+7x+24 + \dfrac{7}{x + 2}4+x+27​

Check: the move went from one fraction to a constant plus a fraction, so run it backward. Putting 444 over the common denominator gives 4x+84x + 84x+8 on top, and adding 777 returns the numerator you started with.

Special cases

When the leading coefficient is not 1

A trinomial such as x2+9x+14x^2 + 9x + 14x2+9x+14 factors by finding two numbers that multiply to the constant and add to the middle coefficient. That shortcut fails once the leading coefficient is not 1, because the two numbers no longer land in the binomials untouched.

Split the middle term instead, then group. Multiply the leading coefficient by the constant. Find two numbers that multiply to that product and add to the middle coefficient, then use them to split the middle term into two.

For 5x2+13x+65x^2 + 13x + 65x2+13x+6, the product is 303030 and the pair is 101010 and 333.

5x2+13x+6=5x2+10x+3x+65x^2 + 13x + 6 = 5x^2 + 10x + 3x + 65x2+13x+6=5x2+10x+3x+6 5x2+10x+3x+6=5x(x+2)+3(x+2)5x^2 + 10x + 3x + 6 = 5x(x + 2) + 3(x + 2)5x2+10x+3x+6=5x(x+2)+3(x+2) 5x(x+2)+3(x+2)=(x+2)(5x+3)5x(x + 2) + 3(x + 2) = (x + 2)(5x + 3)5x(x+2)+3(x+2)=(x+2)(5x+3)

Common traps

Changing the form must not change the value

Name the move you are about to make, then check it against the rules above.

Trap Quick check
Canceling a term instead of a factor Only a factor of the whole top and the whole bottom cancels
Dropping the middle term when squaring a sum Squaring a binomial produces three terms, not two
Giving the minus to the first term of a grouping only Every term inside meets the minus
Adding denominators when adding fractions One common denominator, then scale each numerator
Reading a negative exponent as a negative number It moves the power across the bar and changes no sign

The method: Ask, choose, rewrite, check

  1. Name the form the question wants. Factored, expanded, one fraction, a fraction in lowest terms, a power with no negative exponent, or the value of a constant. Write it down first, because every choice is an expression and a wrong form still looks like an answer.
  2. Choose the move that produces that form. Terms with something in common point to factoring, and four terms point to grouping. A product points to expanding. Powers of one base point to the exponent rules. Two fractions point to a common denominator, and one fraction points to factoring the top and the bottom.
  3. Rewrite one line at a time. Every term inside a grouping meets the multiplier, and every term of a numerator meets the divisor. Keep each sign with the number it belongs to.
  4. Check, then answer the ask. Factoring, canceling, combining fractions, taking a root and applying an exponent rule all reverse in a line, so undo the move and the original has to return. An expansion reverses only by factoring, so test one allowed input instead: a disagreement disproves the rewrite and agreement proves nothing. Then give the form the question named.

Test one value to compare two forms

A graphing calculator is built into the testing app and is allowed on every Math question.

Equivalent expressions agree at every allowed input, so one input where two forms disagree proves they are not equivalent. Pick a small number that makes no denominator zero, such as 222, and evaluate the original and the choice. Avoid 000 and 111, which hide too many errors.

By hand is faster when the rewrite is a single factoring or exponent step.

Practice

Work each item with the method, under a rough clock: about a minute for a bare equation, and up to about two minutes once there is a context to translate or a graph or table to read first. The Math section gives you about 95 seconds a question on average, so that range is test-day pace rather than a drill speed. Press Show solution to see the steps, one to a line.

For x>0x > 0x>0, which expression is equivalent to x6x−3\dfrac{x^6}{x^{-3}}x−3x6​?

Correct. Subtracting the exponent −3-3−3 from 666 adds 333, leaving x9x^9x9.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a single power of xxx, so the answer is one base with one exponent.

Step 2. Both parts are powers of the same base, so the quotient rule applies: subtract the bottom exponent from the top one.

Step 3. Subtracting a negative exponent adds its size.

x6−(−3)=x9x^{6 - (-3)} = x^9x6−(−3)=x9

Step 4. An exponent rule reverses in a line, so run it backward. Multiplying x9x^9x9 by x−3x^{-3}x−3 adds the exponents to 666, which is the numerator you started with. The equivalent expression is x9x^9x9.

Which expression is equivalent to (3x−2)(x+5)(3x - 2)(x + 5)(3x−2)(x+5)?

Correct. The four products collect to 3x2+13x−103x^2 + 13x - 103x2+13x−10.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is an expanded form, so the answer is a polynomial rather than a product.

Step 2. The move is expanding: every term of the first factor meets every term of the second, which is four products.

Step 3. Multiply, keeping the minus with the 222.

(3x−2)(x+5)=3x2+15x−2x−10(3x - 2)(x + 5) = 3x^2 + 15x - 2x - 10(3x−2)(x+5)=3x2+15x−2x−10 3x2+15x−2x−10=3x2+13x−103x^2 + 15x - 2x - 10 = 3x^2 + 13x - 103x2+15x−2x−10=3x2+13x−10

Step 4. The move was an expansion, so test one allowed input. At 222 the product is 444 times 777, which is 282828, and 3x2+13x−103x^2 + 13x - 103x2+13x−10 gives 12+26−1012 + 26 - 1012+26−10, also 282828. The expanded form is 3x2+13x−103x^2 + 13x - 103x2+13x−10.

For x>0x > 0x>0, which expression is equivalent to 3x−2x+4\dfrac{3}{x} - \dfrac{2}{x + 4}x3​−x+42​?

Correct. Over the common denominator x(x+4)x(x + 4)x(x+4) the numerators leave x+12x + 12x+12.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is one fraction, so the two have to sit over one denominator.

Step 2. The two denominators share no factor, so the common denominator is their product.

Step 3. Scale each numerator by whatever its own denominator was missing, then subtract.

3x−2x+4=3(x+4)−2xx(x+4)\frac{3}{x} - \frac{2}{x + 4} = \frac{3(x + 4) - 2x}{x(x + 4)}x3​−x+42​=x(x+4)3(x+4)−2x​ 3(x+4)−2xx(x+4)=3x+12−2xx(x+4)\frac{3(x + 4) - 2x}{x(x + 4)} = \frac{3x + 12 - 2x}{x(x + 4)}x(x+4)3(x+4)−2x​=x(x+4)3x+12−2x​ 3x+12−2xx(x+4)=x+12x(x+4)\frac{3x + 12 - 2x}{x(x + 4)} = \frac{x + 12}{x(x + 4)}x(x+4)3x+12−2x​=x(x+4)x+12​

Step 4. Two fractions became one, so run it backward: the numerator x+12x + 12x+12 came from 3(x+4)−2x3(x + 4) - 2x3(x+4)−2x, and splitting those pieces back over x(x+4)x(x + 4)x(x+4) returns the two fractions. The single fraction is x+12x(x+4)\dfrac{x + 12}{x(x + 4)}x(x+4)x+12​.

For x>0x > 0x>0, which expression is equivalent to 64x243\sqrt[3]{64x^{24}}364x24​?

Correct. The cube root of 646464 is 444, and dividing the exponent by the index leaves 4x84x^84x8.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is an expression with no radical sign, so the root has to be taken rather than rewritten.

Step 2. A cube root reaches every factor inside it, so take the root of the coefficient and of the power separately.

Step 3. The coefficient is a perfect cube, and the index divides the exponent.

64x243=4x8\sqrt[3]{64x^{24}} = 4x^8364x24​=4x8

Step 4. A root was taken, so run it backward by cubing. 444 cubed is 646464 and x8x^8x8 cubed is x24x^{24}x24, which returns what was inside the radical. The equivalent expression is 4x84x^84x8.

Which expression is equivalent to 3x3−6x2+5x−103x^3 - 6x^2 + 5x - 103x3−6x2+5x−10?

Correct. Both pairs leave the binomial x−2x - 2x−2, so the factored form is (x−2)(3x2+5)(x - 2)(3x^2 + 5)(x−2)(3x2+5).

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a factored form, so the answer is a product.

Step 2. Nothing is common to all four terms, so group them in pairs and pull from each pair.

Step 3. The first pair shares 3x23x^23x2 and the second pair shares 555.

3x3−6x2+5x−10=3x2(x−2)+5(x−2)3x^3 - 6x^2 + 5x - 10 = 3x^2(x - 2) + 5(x - 2)3x3−6x2+5x−10=3x2(x−2)+5(x−2)

The binomial x−2x - 2x−2 now appears twice, so pull it out.

3x2(x−2)+5(x−2)=(x−2)(3x2+5)3x^2(x - 2) + 5(x - 2) = (x - 2)(3x^2 + 5)3x2(x−2)+5(x−2)=(x−2)(3x2+5)

Step 4. The move was factoring, so run it backward: multiplying the two factors returns all four terms. The factored form is (x−2)(3x2+5)(x - 2)(3x^2 + 5)(x−2)(3x2+5).

For x>6x > 6x>6, which expression is equivalent to x2+6xx2−36\dfrac{x^2 + 6x}{x^2 - 36}x2−36x2+6x​?

Correct. Canceling the shared factor x+6x + 6x+6 leaves xx−6\dfrac{x}{x - 6}x−6x​.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a fraction in lowest terms, so something has to cancel. The denominator you were given is zero at 666 and at −6-6−6, so both stay barred whatever cancels.

Step 2. Only a factor cancels, so factor the top and the bottom before looking. The top has a common monomial, and the bottom is a difference of squares.

Step 3. Factor both.

x2+6xx2−36=x(x+6)(x−6)(x+6)\frac{x^2 + 6x}{x^2 - 36} = \frac{x(x + 6)}{(x - 6)(x + 6)}x2−36x2+6x​=(x−6)(x+6)x(x+6)​

The factor x+6x + 6x+6 sits in both, so cancel it.

x(x+6)(x−6)(x+6)=xx−6\frac{x(x + 6)}{(x - 6)(x + 6)} = \frac{x}{x - 6}(x−6)(x+6)x(x+6)​=x−6x​

Step 4. A factor was canceled, so run it backward: putting x+6x + 6x+6 back on the top and the bottom returns the fraction you started with. The reduced form still bars −6-6−6, which it no longer shows, and the stem's condition keeps every input clear of both barred values. The reduced form is xx−6\dfrac{x}{x - 6}x−6x​.

The expression 12x2−5x−312x^2 - 5x - 312x2−5x−3 can be written as the product of 4x−34x - 34x−3 and one other binomial. What is the other binomial?

Correct. The two binomials have to multiply to 12x212x^212x2 at the front and to −3-3−3 at the back, which leaves 3x+13x + 13x+1.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is the second factor, so the answer is a binomial rather than a value of xxx.

Step 2. One factor is given, so nothing has to be guessed: each end of the product fixes one part of the missing binomial.

Step 3. The two leading terms multiply to 12x212x^212x2, and 4x4x4x times 3x3x3x gives that.

4x⋅3x=12x24x \cdot 3x = 12x^24x⋅3x=12x2

The two constants multiply to −3-3−3, and −3-3−3 times 111 gives that.

−3⋅1=−3-3 \cdot 1 = -3−3⋅1=−3

Step 4. The move was factoring, so run it backward: the middle term comes out 4x−9x4x - 9x4x−9x, which is −5x-5x−5x, so the other binomial is 3x+13x + 13x+1.
