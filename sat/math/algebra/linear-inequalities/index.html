Linear Inequalities

  • Algebra
  • Linear inequalities in one or two variables
←PreviousAll Math lessonsNext→

An inequality is solved like an equation, but its answer is a set of numbers. Decide first what the question wants from that set: the set itself, one end of it, an integer inside it, or a constant that produces it.

Key takeaways

  • Add or subtract anything on both sides, and the relation is unchanged.
  • Multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative reverses the relation.
  • Swapping the two sides reverses it as well.
  • A strict symbol leaves the set's edge out; an at-most or at-least keeps it.
  • A strict bound is never reached; a whole-number variable stops at the integer just inside it.
  • In two variables the solutions are a region, and a test point picks the side.
  • Check the direction with a number well inside the set, and the edge with the edge itself.

What the SAT may ask

  • Solve an inequality and choose its solution set.
  • Find the greatest or least value a variable can take.
  • Find the greatest or least integer in a solution set.
  • Decide whether a point is a solution of a two-variable inequality.
  • Write the inequality that models a stated limit.
  • Find a constant that produces a stated solution set.

The rule

Most moves treat an inequality exactly as they treat an equation. Add or subtract anything on both sides, and multiply or divide both sides by a positive number, and the relation is unchanged.

Of the moves that solve a linear inequality, exactly two reverse the relation. Multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number is the first.

−4x>20-4x > 20−4x>20 x<−5x < -5x<−5

Swapping the two sides is the second. A result reached as 9>x9 > x9>x is written x<9x < 9x<9, not x>9x > 9x>9. The symbol points at the smaller side, so moving the sides moves the point.

In one variable the answer is a set of numbers, and the printed symbol says whether the edge belongs to it. An at-most or an at-least keeps the edge; a strict symbol leaves it out. On a number line that is a filled circle against an open one.

In two variables the answer is a region. Draw the boundary line first, dashed for a strict symbol and solid otherwise. The boundary of 4x+5y>204x + 5y > 204x+5y>20 runs through (0,4)(0, 4)(0,4) and (5,0)(5, 0)(5,0), and it is dashed.

Then substitute one point that is not on the line: shade its side if the statement is true, the other side if it is not. The origin gives 000, which is not greater than 202020, so the region is the other side.

4(0)+5(0)=04(0) + 5(0) = 04(0)+5(0)=0

The symbol alone names no side, because the sign of the coefficient on yyy decides which way the region falls.

Two inequalities at once are two conditions, and a solution has to meet both. Each one shades a region, and the solutions are where the two shaded regions overlap. A point that passes one and fails the other is not a solution.

Test a point against every inequality in the set, not just the first. (1,5)(1, 5)(1,5) satisfies y>xy > xy>x, and it fails x+y≤4x + y \le 4x+y≤4, so it lies outside the overlap.

Why the rule exists

Multiplying by a negative reverses the relation because it reflects the number line through zero. Start with 3<83 < 83<8. Multiply both sides by −1-1−1 and the two numbers become −3-3−3 and −8-8−8, and −3-3−3 is now the larger of them. Reflection reverses which side of zero everything sits on, so the order has to turn around.

Adding a negative does nothing of the kind. Subtract 101010 from both sides of 3<83 < 83<8 and you get −7<−2-7 < -2−7<−2, with the order intact, because subtracting slides the whole line without reflecting it.

Worked examples

1. Collect the variable first, then divide

Solve 6x−5>9x+76x - 5 > 9x + 76x−5>9x+7.

Question asks: the solution set.

First move: collect the variable on one side.

Subtract 9x9x9x from both sides. Moving a term does nothing to the relation.

−3x−5>7-3x - 5 > 7−3x−5>7

Add 555 to both sides.

−3x>12-3x > 12−3x>12

Divide both sides by −3-3−3. That is the one move here that reverses the relation.

x<−4x < -4x<−4

Answer: x<−4x < -4x<−4

Check: the relation turned, so take a number well inside the set. At x=−6x = -6x=−6 the left side comes to −41-41−41 and the right side to −47-47−47.

6(−6)−5=−416(-6) - 5 = -416(−6)−5=−41

The left side is the greater of the two, so the original holds.

Then check the edge, which no interior point can settle.

6(−4)−5=−296(-4) - 5 = -296(−4)−5=−29 9(−4)+7=−299(-4) + 7 = -299(−4)+7=−29

The two sides are equal there, and the printed symbol is strict, so the edge is left out of the set.

A second route never divides by a negative. Collecting the variable on the right instead reaches −4>x-4 > x−4>x, and writing that the usual way round swaps the sides. Each route reverses exactly once.

2. Solve for y to see which side is shaded

Describe the graph of 4x−3y≥184x - 3y \ge 184x−3y≥18: its boundary line, and which side of that line is shaded.

Question asks: the boundary line and the shaded side.

First move: solve for yyy, which puts the boundary into a form you can graph.

Subtract 4x4x4x from both sides.

−3y≥−4x+18-3y \ge -4x + 18−3y≥−4x+18

Divide every term by −3-3−3. The divisor is negative, so the relation reverses.

y≤43x−6y \le \frac{4}{3}x - 6y≤34​x−6

The boundary is the line y=43x−6y = \frac{4}{3}x - 6y=34​x−6, and it is solid, because the printed symbol is an at-least and the points on the line satisfy it.

Answer: the region on and below that line.

Check: the relation turned, so take a point well inside the region. The point (0,−9)(0, -9)(0,−9) sits below the line, since the line is at −6-6−6 when xxx is zero.

4(0)−3(−9)=274(0) - 3(-9) = 274(0)−3(−9)=27

That is at least 181818, so the region below the line is the right one. The origin, which sits above the line, gives 000 and fails, which says the same thing from outside.

Then check the edge, which the inside point cannot settle. Take (0,−6)(0, -6)(0,−6), which sits on the line.

4(0)−3(−6)=184(0) - 3(-6) = 184(0)−3(−6)=18

That is at least 181818, so the boundary belongs to the region and is drawn solid.

3. When the bound is not a whole number

If 8x+3y≤508x + 3y \le 508x+3y≤50 and y=4y = 4y=4, what is the greatest integer value of xxx?

Question asks: the greatest integer in the set, which is not the same as the set's upper end.

First move: substitute 444 for yyy and simplify the term it lands in.

8x+12≤508x + 12 \le 508x+12≤50

Subtract 121212 from both sides, then divide both sides by 888. Subtracting never touches the relation, and the divisor is positive, so it holds.

8x≤388x \le 388x≤38 x≤4.75x \le 4.75x≤4.75

Answer: 444

The upper end is 4.754.754.75, and no integer lies between 444 and it. Step down to the integer inside the set.

Check: you rounded, so test both sides of the step. At x=4x = 4x=4 the left side comes to 444444, which is at most 505050, and an input of 555 would make it 525252, which is not.

8(4)+3(4)=448(4) + 3(4) = 448(4)+3(4)=44

Common mistake: rounding 4.754.754.75 up to 555 reports a value the set does not contain.

Special cases

When the variable terms cancel

Sometimes the same variable term ends up on both sides and disappears. What is left is a statement about numbers alone, and it is either true or false.

5x+9>5x+25x + 9 > 5x + 25x+9>5x+2

Subtracting 5x5x5x from both sides leaves 9>29 > 29>2, which is true whatever xxx was, so every real number is a solution.

7x−3≥7x+17x - 3 \ge 7x + 17x−3≥7x+1

Here subtracting 7x7x7x leaves −3≥1-3 \ge 1−3≥1, which is false, so there is no solution.

The close case is two equal constants, where the symbol decides alone.

6x+5≤6x+56x + 5 \le 6x + 56x+5≤6x+5

That leaves 5≤55 \le 55≤5, which is true, so every real number is a solution. Under a strict less-than the same two sides leave 5<55 < 55<5, which is false, so there is no solution.

Common traps

Turn the symbol, and then read the edge

Every number in a solution can be right while the answer is wrong, because what fails is the relation rather than any term in it. Among the moves that solve a linear inequality the reversal has two causes: a negative divisor or multiplier, and a swap of the two sides. A negative appearing anywhere else in the working is neither.

Trap Quick check
Dividing by a negative and leaving the symbol alone The divisor's sign is what triggers a reversal
Writing 9>x9 > x9>x as x>9x > 9x>9 Swapping the sides reverses the symbol
Reading a strict edge as reachable The printed symbol decides the edge, and no move changes it
Rounding a bound outward to an integer Step to the integer that is inside the set

A reversal changes the direction of a set and never its edge, so a set is pinned down only once you have read both.

The method: Ask, substitute, solve, check

  1. Name the ask. The set itself, one end of it, an integer inside it, whether a point belongs, a model for a limit, or a constant producing a stated set. These are different answers to the same solve, so write down which one you want before you start.
  2. Take in what you were given. A value for one variable turns a two-variable inequality into a one-variable one; a point is two values at once. Read a graph the same way: edge first, then direction. From a described situation, name the fixed amount, the rate, and whether the limit is allowed.
  3. Solve with the same moves an equation takes, carrying the symbol on every line. Collect the variable on one side, clear any grouping or fraction, and divide out the coefficient. Adding and subtracting never touch the relation. Turn the symbol if, and only if, you divided or multiplied both sides by a negative, or swapped the two sides.
  4. Answer the ask. Check first if the relation turned, if you rounded, or if you solved for a constant. A number well inside the set checks the direction, because a set and its reversal treat the edge alike; the edge itself checks whether the edge belongs. After rounding, test the integer you kept and the one you dropped.

When graphing is the faster route

A graphing calculator is built into the testing app and is allowed on every Math question.

Graph each side of a one-variable inequality as its own line. Where one line sits above the other is the solution set.

Graph the boundary line of a two-variable inequality to see where a given point sits relative to it.

One substitution is faster by hand, and so is one division.

If 6x+y4≤336x + \frac{y}{4} \le 336x+4y​≤33 and x=3x = 3x=3, what is the greatest possible value of yyy?

Correct. Substituting 333, subtracting the 181818 that lands and multiplying by 444 gives y≤60y \le 60y≤60.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is the greatest value yyy may take, which is the upper end of the solution set.

Step 2. Substitute 333 for xxx and simplify the term it lands in.

18+y4≤3318 + \frac{y}{4} \le 3318+4y​≤33

Step 3. Subtract 181818 from both sides, then multiply both sides by 444 to clear the denominator. Subtracting never touches the relation, and the multiplier is positive, so it holds.

y4≤15\frac{y}{4} \le 154y​≤15 y≤60y \le 60y≤60

Step 4. The printed symbol is an at-most, so 606060 itself belongs to the set. The answer is 606060.

3y−30≥123y - 30 \ge 123y−30≥12

Practice 3

  • Algebra
  • Linear inequalities in one or two variables
  • Standard

The solution to one of the following inequalities is graphed on the number line shown. Which inequality is it?

Correct. Dividing by −3-3−3 reverses the relation, and the set that comes out is the one graphed, x≥−2x \ge -2x≥−2.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is which of the four inequalities has the graphed set as its solution.

Step 2. The figure is what you were given, so read the set off it. The circle sits at −2-2−2 and is filled, so the edge belongs, and the shading runs right.

x≥−2x \ge -2x≥−2

Step 3. That set is an at-least, and two shapes produce one: an at-least over a positive coefficient, or an at-most over a negative one, where dividing reverses the relation. The symbol alone does not settle it, so use the edge: the graph's edge is −2-2−2, and one candidate solves to it.

−3x≤6-3x \le 6−3x≤6

Divide both sides by −3-3−3. The divisor is negative, so the relation reverses.

x≥−2x \ge -2x≥−2

Step 4. The relation turned, so check with a number well inside the graphed set. An input of 000 makes the left side 000, which is at most 666.

−3(0)=0-3(0) = 0−3(0)=0

An input of −4-4−4 sits outside the graphed set and makes the left side 121212, which is not at most 666. Then the edge itself: at −2-2−2 the left side comes to 666.

−3(−2)=6-3(-2) = 6−3(−2)=6

That is at most 666, so −2-2−2 belongs, which is what the filled circle shows, so the answer is −3x≤6-3x \le 6−3x≤6.

If 9y−6x≥279y - 6x \ge 279y−6x≥27 and y=5y = 5y=5, what is the greatest possible value of xxx?

Correct. Substituting 555 leaves a negative coefficient on xxx, so the division reverses the relation and gives x≤3x \le 3x≤3.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is the greatest value xxx may take.

Step 2. Substitute 555 for yyy and simplify the term it lands in.

45−6x≥2745 - 6x \ge 2745−6x≥27

Step 3. Subtract 454545 from both sides. The coefficient left on xxx is negative, which is the thing to notice before dividing.

−6x≥−18-6x \ge -18−6x≥−18

Divide both sides by −6-6−6 and reverse the relation.

x≤3x \le 3x≤3

Step 4. The relation turned, so check with a number well inside the set. At x=0x = 0x=0 the left side comes to 454545, which is at least 272727, and an input of 666 would make it 999, which is not.

9(5)−6(0)=459(5) - 6(0) = 459(5)−6(0)=45

Then the edge itself: at x=3x = 3x=3 the left side comes to 272727.

9(5)−6(3)=279(5) - 6(3) = 279(5)−6(3)=27

That is at least 272727, so 333 belongs, and the answer is 333.

Practice 5

  • Algebra
  • Linear inequalities in one or two variables
  • Standard

A club is stocking a display case. The case itself costs 151515 dollars, and each badge placed inside it costs 666 dollars. The club will spend at most 757575 dollars in total. Which inequality gives the possible numbers of badges, bbb?

Correct. The case is a one-off 151515, each badge adds 666, and an at-most lets the total equal 757575.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is an inequality, not a number of badges.

Step 2. Build it from what the question says. The case is bought once, so its cost is a fixed 151515. Each badge adds 666, so bbb badges add 6b6b6b.

15+6b15 + 6b15+6b

The limit is at most 757575 dollars, and an at-most lets the total equal the limit.

15+6b≤7515 + 6b \le 7515+6b≤75

Step 3. Nothing is left to solve, because the ask is the model rather than the number of badges it allows.

Step 4. The answer is 15+6b≤7515 + 6b \le 7515+6b≤75.

A badge is a thing you count, so bbb is a whole number and cannot be negative. Solving would give b≤10b \le 10b≤10, which leaves the club 000 through 101010 badges.

Which of the following points is a solution of 5x−2y<145x - 2y < 145x−2y<14?

Correct. This point makes the left side 111111, which is less than 141414.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is which point satisfies the inequality. A point is a solution when substituting it makes the statement true.

Step 2. A point is two values at once, so substitute both coordinates, the first for xxx and the second for yyy.

5(3)−2(2)=115(3) - 2(2) = 115(3)−2(2)=11

Step 3. Nothing is left to solve. The statement is true or false as it stands, and 111111 is less than 141414.

Step 4. The symbol is strict, so a point making the two sides equal lies on the boundary line and is not in the region. This one does not, and the answer is (3,2)(3, 2)(3,2).

Practice 7

  • Algebra
  • Linear inequalities in one or two variables
  • Advanced

The solution to kx−4≥20kx - 4 \ge 20kx−4≥20 is x≤−8x \le -8x≤−8, where kkk is a constant. What is the value of kkk?

Correct. The stated set points the other way from the printed symbol, so the division reversed the relation and matching the two edges gives k=−3k = -3k=−3.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is kkk. The solution set is given rather than wanted, so this runs backwards from an answer to the constant that produced it.

Step 2. Nothing is given to substitute. What the question hands over instead is the direction of the stated set.

Step 3. Add 444 to both sides of the printed inequality.

kx≥24kx \ge 24kx≥24

Dividing by kkk is what produces the solution set. The printed symbol is an at-least and the stated solution is an at-most, so that division reversed the relation, which happens only when the divisor is negative. The edge of the set is 24k\frac{24}{k}k24​.

24k=−8\frac{24}{k} = -8k24​=−8 k=−3k = -3k=−3

Step 4. You solved for a constant, so put it back.

−3x−4≥20-3x - 4 \ge 20−3x−4≥20

Adding 444 and then dividing by −3-3−3, which reverses the relation, returns the stated set.

x≤−8x \le -8x≤−8

An input of −10-10−10 is well inside that set and makes the left side 262626, which is at least 202020. Then the edge itself: at −8-8−8 the left side comes to 202020.

−3(−8)−4=20-3(-8) - 4 = 20−3(−8)−4=20

That is at least 202020, so −8-8−8 belongs. The ask was kkk, so the answer is −3-3−3.

Correct. Both sides come to 4x+34x + 34x+3, so the statement left over is true whatever xxx is.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is the whole solution set, described in words or as an inequality.

Step 2. Nothing is given to substitute.

Step 3. Divide every term of the numerator by 222.

4x+3≤4x+34x + 3 \le 4x + 34x+3≤4x+3

Subtract 4x4x4x from both sides. The variable terms cancel, and what is left has no variable in it.

3≤33 \le 33≤3

Step 4. That statement is true, and it stayed true without any help from xxx, so every real number satisfies the original. The at-most symbol is what makes it true: 333 is not less than 333, but it is at most 333. The answer is that all real numbers are solutions.
