Systems of Two Linear Equations

  • Algebra
  • Systems of two linear equations in two variables
←PreviousAll Math lessonsNext→

A system asks for the pair of values that satisfies two equations at once. Decide first whether the question wants that pair, one of its numbers, the number of solutions, or the system itself.

Key takeaways

  • A solution is an ordered pair that makes both equations true, not just one.
  • Substitution is fastest when a variable is already alone, or one step from it.
  • Elimination is fastest when the two equations line up on a variable; scale one or both until they do.
  • Two lines with different slopes cross once, so the system has exactly one solution.
  • Same slope and different yyy-intercepts means parallel lines and no solution.
  • Same slope and the same yyy-intercept means one line drawn twice and infinitely many solutions.
  • Check a pair in the equation that did not produce the second number.

What the SAT may ask

  • Solve a system for one variable or for the pair.
  • Choose the system that models a described situation.
  • Decide how many solutions a system has.
  • Find a constant that makes a stated pair the solution.
  • Match a graph of two lines to the system it defines.

The rule

A system of two linear equations is two equations in the same two unknowns. Its solution is the ordered pair that makes both true. On a graph, that pair is the point both lines pass through.

Substitution. Get one variable alone in one equation, then put that expression into the other. From y=4−xy = 4 - xy=4−x and 3x+2y=153x + 2y = 153x+2y=15:

3x+2(4−x)=153x + 2(4 - x) = 153x+2(4−x)=15 x=7x = 7x=7

Elimination. Line the two equations up on one variable, then add or subtract so that variable disappears. When neither variable lines up, multiply one equation, or both, until one does. Multiplying an equation by a nonzero number changes nothing about its solutions, because every term on both sides is multiplied. Multiplying by zero throws the equation away.

Sometimes the ask is a combination rather than a variable. Adding or subtracting the two equations as they stand can land on x+yx + yx+y or x−yx - yx−y directly, with neither variable ever found alone. Look for that before solving the whole system.

Neither route is always faster. Pick by what the equations already look like: a variable standing alone points to substitution, and matching or easily matched coefficients point to elimination.

Two equations do not always have one solution, and the slopes decide. Put both in the form y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b and compare.

What you see How many solutions
Different slopes Exactly one
Same slope, different yyy-intercepts None
Same slope, same yyy-intercept Infinitely many

In standard form the same test reads as a ratio. If every term of one equation is the same multiple of the other's, the two describe one line. If the coefficients follow that multiple and the constants do not, nothing satisfies both.

A vertical line has no slope, so the table cannot reach it. Two equations of the form x=ax = ax=a are one line when the two numbers agree and parallel when they differ. A vertical line crosses every line that is not vertical exactly once.

Why the rule exists

One equation in two unknowns has endless solutions, which is why its graph is a whole line rather than a point. A second equation cuts that line down to the part of it that also satisfies the second condition, and two different straight lines share at most one point.

Both routes work for the same reason. Substitution replaces a variable with something equal to it, and replacing a quantity with its equal cannot change what is true. Elimination adds equal things to equal things, which is the same move as adding a number to both sides, done with an equation instead of a number.

Worked examples

1. Build a system from two purchases and eliminate

A workshop pays 232323 dollars for 333 clamps and 222 mallets, and 303030 dollars for 222 clamps and 555 mallets. What does one mallet cost?

Question asks: the price of one mallet.

First move: name the two unknowns and write one equation per purchase. Let ccc be the price of a clamp and mmm the price of a mallet.

3c+2m=233c + 2m = 233c+2m=23 2c+5m=302c + 5m = 302c+5m=30

Neither variable lines up, so scale both. Multiply the first by 222 and the second by 333, which makes the ccc terms match.

6c+4m=466c + 4m = 466c+4m=46 6c+15m=906c + 15m = 906c+15m=90

Subtract the first from the second. The ccc terms disappear.

11m=4411m = 4411m=44 m=4m = 4m=4

Answer: 444 dollars

Check: you solved for a second number, so finish the pair and test it in the equation the elimination did not produce it from. Putting the mallet price back into the first purchase gives the clamp price.

3c+8=233c + 8 = 233c+8=23 c=5c = 5c=5

The second purchase then reads:

2(5)+5(4)=302(5) + 5(4) = 302(5)+5(4)=30

Common mistake: answering 555 reports the clamp, which is the other half of the same pair.

2. Compare two equations written in different forms

How many solutions does the system of y=34x−5y = \frac{3}{4}x - 5y=43​x−5 and 3x−4y=203x - 4y = 203x−4y=20 have?

Question asks: the number of solutions, so nothing has to be solved.

First move: put both equations in the same form. The first is already solved for yyy, so work on the second.

−4y=−3x+20-4y = -3x + 20−4y=−3x+20

Divide every term by −4-4−4.

y=34x−5y = \frac{3}{4}x - 5y=43​x−5

Answer: infinitely many.

The two equations describe one line, and every point on it satisfies both. Matching slopes alone would not have settled it. Had the second equation ended in a different constant, the lines would be parallel and there would be no solution. Compare the yyy-intercepts as well as the slopes.

Common mistake: stopping at −4y=−3x+20-4y = -3x + 20−4y=−3x+20 and reading its numbers against the first equation's. Convert first, or compare the two standard forms as a ratio; what you may not do is match a coefficient in one form against a coefficient in another.

3. Isolate a variable, then substitute

Solve the system of 3x+y=143x + y = 143x+y=14 and 5x−2y=55x - 2y = 55x−2y=5.

Question asks: the pair.

First move: nothing is isolated, so isolate the cheapest thing. The yyy in the first equation has a coefficient of 111, so one subtraction frees it.

y=14−3xy = 14 - 3xy=14−3x

Substitute that into the second equation.

5x−2(14−3x)=55x - 2(14 - 3x) = 55x−2(14−3x)=5

Distribute the −2-2−2 across both terms in the grouping.

5x−28+6x=55x - 28 + 6x = 55x−28+6x=5 11x=3311x = 3311x=33 x=3x = 3x=3

Put that back into the isolated equation.

y=14−3(3)y = 14 - 3(3)y=14−3(3) y=5y = 5y=5

Answer: (3,5)(3, 5)(3,5)

Check: you solved for a second number, so test the pair in the equation that did not produce it.

5(3)−2(5)=55(3) - 2(5) = 55(3)−2(5)=5

Special cases

When the variables all disappear

Solving a system can remove both variables at once, and what is left decides the answer.

If the leftover statement is false, such as 0=70 = 70=7, nothing satisfies both equations. The lines are parallel and the system has no solution.

If the leftover statement is true, such as 0=00 = 00=0, every pair that satisfies one equation satisfies the other. The lines are the same line and the system has infinitely many solutions.

Neither outcome is a mistake in the working. A system in which one equation is a multiple of the other carries no more information than one equation alone, so it cannot pin a single point.

A question can hand you the count and ask for the parameter. Take 2x+3y=72x + 3y = 72x+3y=7 beside 4x+ky=154x + ky = 154x+ky=15. Doubling the first gives 4x+6y=144x + 6y = 144x+6y=14, so k=6k = 6k=6 makes the two left sides match while the right sides differ: no solution.

Put 141414 in place of 151515 and k=6k = 6k=6 makes the two equations one equation, so the system has infinitely many solutions. Any other kkk leaves the coefficient ratios unequal, and the system has exactly one solution.

Common traps

One equation is not the system

A pair that satisfies one equation is not yet a solution. It may still be one; the equation it passed cannot say. The right value of the wrong variable, and a pair checked only in the equation it came from, both leave the other half of the test unrun.

Trap Quick check
Answering with the variable you eliminated last Read the ask again before writing the number down
Distributing across only the first term of a grouping Every term inside the grouping meets the multiplier
Scaling one side of an equation and not the other Multiply every term, on both sides
Reading matching slopes as no solution Matching yyy-intercepts as well means one line, not none

The method: Ask, choose, carry out, check

  1. Name the ask. The pair, one of its two numbers, the number of solutions, a constant that makes a stated pair work, or the system itself. Write down which one before you start, because the ask and the equations together decide the route.
  2. Choose the route, or the comparison. For a pair, substitution suits a variable that is alone or one step from it, and elimination suits coefficients that match or nearly do. To count solutions, put both equations in one form and compare them. To build a system, name the two facts it gives. To fit a constant, take the stated pair.
  3. Carry it out. Solving: eliminate or substitute, then put the result back for the second number. Comparing: read the slopes, then the yyy-intercepts, and if both variables disappear read the leftover statement, false for no solution and true for infinitely many. Building: write the total, then the relationship. Fitting a constant: substitute both coordinates.
  4. Answer the ask. If you solved for a second number, check the pair in the equation that did not produce it. A pair can satisfy the equation it came from and fail the other. Then give the quantity the question named, with its units when it has any.

Graph both equations to see the outcome

A graphing calculator is built into the testing app and is allowed on every Math question.

Enter both equations and look at the picture. Lines that cross give the solution as a point you can read off. Lines that lie on top of each other mean infinitely many solutions.

No crossing on the screen is not proof of no solution: the crossing may sit outside the window. Widen the window, or settle it from the equations.

By hand is faster when a variable is already alone, or when the coefficients already match.

Practice

Work each item with the method, under a rough clock: about a minute for a bare equation, and up to about two minutes once there is a context to translate or a graph or table to read first. The Math section gives you about 95 seconds a question on average, so that range is test-day pace rather than a drill speed. Press Show solution to see the steps, one to a line.

In the system of equations below, what is the value of xxx?

x=2y−5x = 2y - 5x=2y−5 3x+4y=253x + 4y = 253x+4y=25
Correct. Substituting and collecting gives y=4y = 4y=4, and the isolated equation then puts xxx at 333.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is xxx, so yyy is on the way rather than the answer.

Step 2. The first equation already has xxx alone, so substitution costs nothing.

Step 3. Put 2y−52y - 52y−5 in place of xxx in the second equation, distributing the 333 across both terms.

3(2y−5)+4y=253(2y - 5) + 4y = 253(2y−5)+4y=25 6y−15+4y=256y - 15 + 4y = 256y−15+4y=25 10y=4010y = 4010y=40 y=4y = 4y=4

Put that back into the isolated equation.

x=2(4)−5x = 2(4) - 5x=2(4)−5 x=3x = 3x=3

Step 4. You solved for a second number, so check (3,4)(3, 4)(3,4) in the equation that did not produce it: 3(3)+4(4)3(3) + 4(4)3(3)+4(4) is 252525. The ask was xxx, so the answer is 333.

How many solutions does the system of equations below have?

4x−6y=104x - 6y = 104x−6y=10 −6x+9y=−15-6x + 9y = -15−6x+9y=−15
Correct. Multiplying every term of the first equation by −32\frac{-3}{2}2−3​ gives the second.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a count, so nothing has to be solved.

Step 2. Both equations are already in standard form, so compare them term by term rather than solving.

Step 3. Look for one multiplier that turns the first equation into the second. The xxx terms need −32\frac{-3}{2}2−3​, since 444 times −32\frac{-3}{2}2−3​ is −6-6−6.

−6y×−32=9y-6y \times \frac{-3}{2} = 9y−6y×2−3​=9y 10×−32=−1510 \times \frac{-3}{2} = -1510×2−3​=−15

Every term follows the same multiplier, constants included.

Step 4. Nothing was solved, so no check applies. The two equations describe one line, so there are infinitely many solutions.

In the system of equations below, what is the value of yyy?

2x+3y=192x + 3y = 192x+3y=19 5x−4y=135x - 4y = 135x−4y=13
Correct. Scaling both equations to match the xxx terms and subtracting leaves 23y=6923y = 6923y=69, so yyy is 333.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is yyy, so xxx is the variable to get rid of.

Step 2. Nothing is isolated and no coefficients match, so elimination needs both equations scaled. Match the xxx terms, not the cheaper pair: eliminating xxx leaves yyy, and eliminating yyy would leave xxx and a second stage to run.

Step 3. Multiply the first equation by 555 and the second by 222.

10x+15y=9510x + 15y = 9510x+15y=95 10x−8y=2610x - 8y = 2610x−8y=26

Subtract the second from the first, and the xxx terms disappear.

23y=6923y = 6923y=69 y=3y = 3y=3

Step 4. One elimination answered the ask, so check by finding the other number. Substitute y=3y = 3y=3 into the first equation.

2x+9=192x + 9 = 192x+9=19 x=5x = 5x=5

Practice 5

  • Algebra
  • Systems of two linear equations in two variables
  • Standard

A club collects 555 dollars from every member and takes in 155155155 dollars in all. Its members split into two teams, and the larger team has 444 more than twice as many members as the smaller one. If sss is the number on the smaller team and lll is the number on the larger, which system of equations represents this situation?

Correct. Every member pays 555 dollars, and the larger team is twice the smaller plus 444.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is the system itself, so nothing is solved.

Step 2. Two facts are given, so there are two equations. The first is the money collected; the second is the relationship between the two counts.

Step 3. Every member is on one team or the other, so the membership is s+ls + ls+l, and each of them pays 555 dollars.

5(s+l)=1555(s + l) = 1555(s+l)=155

Then read the relationship in the order it is written: twice the smaller, and then 444 more.

l=2s+4l = 2s + 4l=2s+4

Step 4. Nothing was solved, so no check applies. The system is the one whose first equation charges every member and whose second doubles the smaller count and then adds four.
