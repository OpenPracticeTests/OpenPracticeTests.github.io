Ratios, Rates, and Units

  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
  • Ratios, rates, proportional relationships, and units
←PreviousAll Math lessonsNext→

A ratio compares two quantities, and a rate is a ratio whose two quantities carry different units. Decide first which unit the answer is measured in, because that unit decides which quantity goes on top.

Key takeaways

  • A ratio of aaa to bbb between the only two parts of a whole splits it into a+ba + ba+b equal parts.
  • Write both sides of a proportion with the same unit on top.
  • A unit rate is the amount for one unit, so divide the top quantity by the bottom one.
  • Scale a rate by multiplying, never by adding the difference.
  • Convert by multiplying by a fraction equal to 111, placed so the unit you are removing cancels.
  • The unit after the word per goes on the bottom of the fraction.
  • A larger unit makes a measurement's number smaller, and a larger unit after per makes a rate's number bigger.

What the SAT may ask

  • Find the missing term in a proportion.
  • Find one part of a ratio from the other part, or from the total.
  • Find a unit rate and use it on a new amount.
  • Convert a measurement, or a rate, into different units.
  • Compare two rates that are given in different units.
  • Find an average speed for a whole trip.

The rule

A ratio compares two quantities. A ratio of 555 to 444 between two separate parts means five of the first for every four of the second. The two together make nine equal parts, so the first quantity is 59\frac{5}{9}95​ of them and 54\frac{5}{4}45​ of the second.

Add the terms only when the two parts make up the whole. In a class of boys and girls only, boys to girls of 555 to 444 makes nine equal parts, and the boys are 59\frac{5}{9}95​.

Two readings break that. Boys to all students of 555 to 999 already names the share, so there is nothing to add. And where a third group exists, two terms count parts of the whole rather than all of it.

A rate is a ratio of two quantities measured in different units, such as grams per cubic centimeter. The unit after the word per goes on the bottom. A unit rate is the amount for one unit of that bottom quantity, so divide the top quantity by the bottom one.

Average speed over a whole trip is the total distance divided by the total time, so each stretch counts for the time it takes. Averaging the two speeds is right only when the two stretches take equally long, which two different speeds never do over equal distances. There the slower stretch takes longer, so it counts for more.

Two quantities are proportional when one is a fixed multiple of the other, y=kxy = kxy=kx. Their quotient yx\frac{y}{x}xy​ is that same kkk at every input other than zero. The graph is a straight line through the origin.

A rate charged on top of a starting fee draws a straight line and is not proportional, because that quotient changes. Straight-line models with a starting value belong to the Linear Functions lesson.

A proportion sets two ratios equal. It is true only when both sides are written in the same order: the same quantity on top of each side, in the same unit. Multiply along the two diagonals to solve it.

47=x91\frac{4}{7} = \frac{x}{91}74​=91x​ 7x=3647x = 3647x=364 x=52x = 52x=52

To change a unit, multiply by a fraction that equals 111, arranged so the unit you are removing cancels. Write that unit on the opposite side of the line from where it sits now. A unit on top goes underneath; a unit underneath, as after the word per, goes on top.

4500 m×1 km1000 m=4.5 km4500 \text{ m} \times \frac{1 \text{ km}}{1000 \text{ m}} = 4.5 \text{ km}4500 m×1000 m1 km​=4.5 km

A percent is a ratio out of 100100100. Percent of a number, percent change, and working back to the original before a change belong to the Percentages lesson.

Why the rule exists

Cross multiplication is not a separate rule. Multiplying both sides of a proportion by both denominators clears them and leaves the two diagonal products equal. The shortcut is two ordinary multiplications already done for you.

A conversion fraction works because its top and its bottom are one quantity written two ways. One meter and one hundred centimeters are the same length, so their quotient is 111. Multiplying by 111 changes nothing except the unit the number is counted in.

Worked examples

1. Build a proportion from a scale statement

On a park map, 666 centimeters represents 151515 kilometers. Two shelters are 222222 centimeters apart on the map. How far apart are the shelters, in kilometers?

Question asks: a real distance in kilometers.

First move: write both sides with centimeters on top and kilometers underneath, so the two fractions compare the same things in the same order.

615=22d\frac{6}{15} = \frac{22}{d}156​=d22​

Multiply along the diagonals.

6d=3306d = 3306d=330 d=55d = 55d=55

Answer: 555555 kilometers

Common mistake: adding 161616 kilometers because the map distance rose by 161616 centimeters, which gives 313131. A proportion scales by multiplying: 222222 centimeters is 113\frac{11}{3}311​ of 666 centimeters, so the real distance is 113\frac{11}{3}311​ of 151515 kilometers.

2. Convert a rate whose units change on top and on the bottom

A sorting belt carries trays past an oven door at 454545 centimeters per minute. What is the belt's speed in meters per hour?

Question asks: the same speed in meters per hour.

First move: change one unit at a time. Start underneath, since the answer needs hours there.

45 cm1 min×60 min1 h=2700 cm1 h\frac{45 \text{ cm}}{1 \text{ min}} \times \frac{60 \text{ min}}{1 \text{ h}} = \frac{2700 \text{ cm}}{1 \text{ h}}1 min45 cm​×1 h60 min​=1 h2700 cm​

The minutes cancel. Now change the centimeters on top to meters.

2700 cm1 h×1 m100 cm=27 m1 h\frac{2700 \text{ cm}}{1 \text{ h}} \times \frac{1 \text{ m}}{100 \text{ cm}} = \frac{27 \text{ m}}{1 \text{ h}}1 h2700 cm​×100 cm1 m​=1 h27 m​

Answer: 272727 meters per hour

Check: you rewrote the rate in different units, so check each direction. An hour is longer than a minute, so the number on top grew. A meter is longer than a centimeter, so it takes fewer of them.

Common mistake: multiplying by 100100100 rather than dividing when the centimeters become meters, which gives 270000270000270000 meters an hour.

3. Decide which quantity goes on top

A metal bar has a mass of 108010801080 grams and a volume of 120120120 cubic centimeters. A second bar of the same metal has a mass of 630630630 grams. What is the volume of the second bar?

Question asks: a volume in cubic centimeters.

First move: find the density. It is named in grams per cubic centimeter, so grams go on top.

1080120=9\frac{1080}{120} = 91201080​=9

Every cubic centimeter of this metal has a mass of 999 grams. The second bar's volume is therefore its mass divided by that density.

6309=70\frac{630}{9} = 709630​=70

Answer: 707070 cubic centimeters

Common mistake: multiplying 630630630 by 999, which gives 567056705670 and makes the lighter bar the larger one. The unit the density is named in tells you the grams are being divided.

Special cases

Squared and cubed units use squared and cubed factors

A length converts by one factor. An area converts by that factor squared, and a volume by it cubed, because the factor applies once along each direction.

One meter is one hundred centimeters, so square that hundred for an area.

1 m2=(100 cm)2=10000 cm21 \text{ m}^2 = (100 \text{ cm})^2 = 10000 \text{ cm}^21 m2=(100 cm)2=10000 cm2

Cube it for a volume, which makes one cubic meter a million cubic centimeters.

Using the plain hundred on an area gives 100100100 square centimeters and is off by a factor of one hundred.

Common traps

The fraction built upside down

Two numbers make two different fractions, and only one of them answers the question. The unit named in the ask is what settles which, so read the ask before you divide.

Trap Quick check
Building a proportion with different units on top Read the two tops: they must measure the same thing
Using a rate given for several units as though it were per one Divide down to one unit first
Adding the difference instead of multiplying by the ratio Find how many times bigger the new amount is, then multiply
Leaving a length in meters when the rate is per centimeter Convert before you multiply
Reading one part of a ratio as the whole Add the two terms only when they make up the whole
Averaging two speeds to get an average speed Divide total distance by total time; each stretch counts for the time it takes

The method: Name, set up, convert, answer

  1. Name the ask and its unit. Write down what the answer is measured in. That unit decides which quantity goes on top.
  2. Set the comparison up. For a part of a whole, add the terms only when the two parts make up that whole. For a rate, divide it down to one unit. For a proportion, put the same quantity on top of both sides. When two ratios share a quantity, work through that shared quantity one ratio at a time.
  3. Convert, then compute. Multiply by a fraction equal to one, arranged so the unit you are removing cancels. That unit goes underneath when it is on top, and on top when it is underneath. Convert one unit at a time. Then do the multiplication or division your setup calls for.
  4. Answer the ask. If you rewrote a quantity in a different unit, check the direction. A measurement counted in a larger unit gives a smaller number. A rate goes the other way after per: the same rate written per hour carries a bigger number than written per minute. Then give the quantity the question named, with its unit.

Set up by hand, divide with the calculator

A graphing calculator is built into the testing app and is allowed on every Math question.

Write the fraction with its units first, then type it and read the value. The calculator cannot tell you which quantity belongs on top.

A brass sample contains copper and zinc in the ratio 777 to 333 by mass. The sample contains 210210210 grams of copper. How many grams of zinc does the sample contain?

Correct. Each of the ten parts is 303030 grams, so the three zinc parts come to 909090 grams.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a mass of zinc in grams.

Step 2. The terms 777 and 333 make ten equal parts, and the seven copper parts weigh 210210210 grams, so one part is what to find first.

Step 3. Nothing needs converting. Divide to get one part, then take three of them.

2107=30\frac{210}{7} = 307210​=30 3×30=903 \times 30 = 903×30=90

Step 4. No unit was rewritten, so the direction check does not apply. The sample holds 909090 grams of zinc.

A braid uses 888 grams of yarn for every 202020 centimeters of its length. How many grams of yarn does a braid 333 meters long use?

Correct. Three meters holds fifteen twenty-centimeter lengths at eight grams each, so the braid uses 120120120 grams.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a mass of yarn in grams.

Step 2. The rate pairs eight grams with twenty centimeters, so grams go on top and centimeters underneath.

Step 3. The braid is measured in meters, so rewrite its length in centimeters, then count the twenty-centimeter lengths and multiply.

3×100=3003 \times 100 = 3003×100=300 30020=15\frac{300}{20} = 1520300​=15 15×8=12015 \times 8 = 12015×8=120

Step 4. You rewrote the length in a different unit: a centimeter is shorter than a meter, so the number grew. The braid uses 120120120 grams of yarn.

A delivery van drives 120120120 kilometers to a depot at 303030 kilometers per hour, then returns along the same 120120120 kilometers at 606060 kilometers per hour. What is the van's average speed for the whole trip, in kilometers per hour?

Correct. The whole trip covers 240240240 kilometers in 666 hours, which is 404040 kilometers per hour.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a speed in kilometers per hour, so kilometers go on top and hours underneath.

Step 2. An average speed divides the whole trip's distance by the whole trip's time, so build both totals. Each leg's time is its distance divided by its speed.

Step 3. Nothing needs converting. Take each leg's time, then divide the whole distance by the whole time.

12030=4\frac{120}{30} = 430120​=4 12060=2\frac{120}{60} = 260120​=2 120+1204+2=40\frac{120 + 120}{4 + 2} = 404+2120+120​=40

Step 4. No unit was rewritten, so the direction check does not apply. The van averages 404040 kilometers per hour.

A supplier sells canvas in two rolls: an 181818-meter roll for 545454 dollars and a 252525-meter roll for 505050 dollars. In dollars, what does one meter of canvas cost from the roll that costs less per meter?

Correct. The 252525-meter roll is the cheaper one per meter, at 222 dollars a meter.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a price for one meter, so dollars go on top and meters underneath.

Step 2. Two rolls means two unit prices, so divide each roll's price by its length and compare the results.

Step 3. Nothing needs converting.

5418=3\frac{54}{18} = 31854​=3 5025=2\frac{50}{25} = 22550​=2

Step 4. No unit was rewritten, so the direction check does not apply. The 252525-meter roll costs less per meter, at 222 dollars a meter.

A cutting machine uses 505050 milliliters of coolant every 666 minutes. If 111 liter is 100010001000 milliliters, how many liters of coolant does the machine use in 444 hours?

Correct. Four hours holds forty six-minute stretches at fifty milliliters each, which comes to 222 liters.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a volume in liters.

Step 2. The rate covers six minutes at a time and the run is given in hours, so the two have to meet before anything is multiplied.

Step 3. Rewrite the four hours in minutes, count the six-minute stretches, multiply by the coolant each one uses, then rewrite the total in liters.

4×60=2404 \times 60 = 2404×60=240 2406=40\frac{240}{6} = 406240​=40 40×50=200040 \times 50 = 200040×50=2000 20001000=2\frac{2000}{1000} = 210002000​=2

Step 4. You rewrote two quantities in different units. A minute is shorter than an hour, so that number grew; a liter is larger than a milliliter, so that number shrank. The machine uses 222 liters.

In a hardware stockroom, the ratio of bolts to nuts is 222 to 333, and the ratio of nuts to washers is 555 to 888. The stockroom holds 160160160 bolts. How many washers does it hold?

Correct. Scaling the bolts up through the nuts and then on to the washers gives 384384384.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a number of washers.

Step 2. The two ratios share the nuts, but they count the nuts differently, as 333 in one and 555 in the other. Scale each ratio until both show the same number of nuts.

2:3=10:152 : 3 = 10 : 152:3=10:15 5:8=15:245 : 8 = 15 : 245:8=15:24

Bolts, nuts and washers are then in the ratio 10:15:2410 : 15 : 2410:15:24.

Step 3. Nothing needs converting. The 160160160 bolts are 161616 times the 101010, so multiply every term by 161616.

15×16=24015 \times 16 = 24015×16=240 24×16=38424 \times 16 = 38424×16=384

Step 4. These are counts, with no unit to rewrite, so the direction check does not apply. The stockroom holds 384384384 washers.

Practice 8

  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
  • Ratios, rates, proportional relationships, and units
  • Advanced

One irrigation line delivers 181818 liters of water per hour. A second line delivers 222 liters every 555 minutes. How many more liters per hour does the second line deliver than the first?

Correct. Rewriting the second line's rate for a whole hour puts it 666 liters ahead of the first.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a number of liters per hour, so both lines have to be counted per hour.

Step 2. The first line is already stated for an hour. The second is stated for five minutes, so rewrite it for sixty.

Step 3. Five minutes goes into an hour twelve times, so multiply the second line's amount by twelve, then subtract.

2×12=242 \times 12 = 242×12=24 24−18=624 - 18 = 624−18=6

Step 4. You rewrote the second line's rate in a different unit: an hour is longer than five minutes, so its number grew. The second line delivers 666 more liters an hour.
