Percentages

  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
  • Percentages
←PreviousAll Math lessonsNext→

A percent is a number of hundredths, and every percent question turns on which number it is taken of. Decide first whether the question wants a part, a percent, the whole, or the value before or after a change.

Key takeaways

  • A percent is hundredths: divide it by 100100100 before using it.
  • The base is the number the percent is taken of; name it first.
  • A rise of rrr multiplies by 1+r1 + r1+r, and a fall of rrr multiplies by 1−r1 - r1−r.
  • To find the value before a change, divide by the multiplier.
  • Percent change is 100100100 times the change divided by the earlier value.
  • Changes on a changing base multiply; percents add only when both are taken of the same amount.
  • A gap in percentage points is not a percent change.

What the SAT may ask

  • Find a percent of a number.
  • Find what percent one number is of another.
  • Find the whole from a part and its percent.
  • Find the value before or after a percent change.
  • Choose the expression that models two percent changes in a row.
  • Compare percent changes across the rows of a table.

The rule

A percent is a number of hundredths. 37%37\%37% means 37100\frac{37}{100}10037​, which is 0.370.370.37. Write ppp for the percent's own number and rrr for the decimal it stands for, so r=p100r = \frac{p}{100}r=100p​. Every formula below runs on rrr.

Three quantities are in play: the part, the whole (the number the percent is taken of, also called the base), and the percent itself. Any two of them give the third.

part=r×whole\text{part} = r \times \text{whole}part=r×whole whole=partr\text{whole} = \frac{\text{part}}{r}whole=rpart​ p=100×partwholep = 100 \times \frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}}p=100×wholepart​

Of and greater than are different words. AAA is 150%150\%150% of BBB means A=1.5BA = 1.5BA=1.5B.

AAA is 150%150\%150% greater than BBB means AAA is BBB plus 150%150\%150% of BBB, so A=2.5BA = 2.5BA=2.5B. Less than reads the same way: 45%45\%45% less than BBB is 0.55B0.55B0.55B.

A change is one multiplication. A rise of rrr multiplies the base by 1+r1 + r1+r, and a fall of rrr multiplies it by 1−r1 - r1−r. A rise of 9%9\%9% multiplies by 1.091.091.09, and a fall of 9%9\%9% multiplies by 0.910.910.91.

To reach the value before a change, divide by that same multiplier. After a rise,

before=after1+r\text{before} = \frac{\text{after}}{1 + r}before=1+rafter​

and after a fall,

before=after1−r\text{before} = \frac{\text{after}}{1 - r}before=1−rafter​

That second division needs 1−r1 - r1−r above zero, since a fall of 100%100\%100% leaves nothing to work back from. Adding the percent back onto the later value undoes neither change, because the percent was taken of the earlier value.

Percent change measures the change against the earlier value, and its sign gives the direction. The earlier value cannot be zero.

percent change=100×new−oldold\text{percent change} = 100 \times \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}}percent change=100×oldnew−old​

Reversing a comparison changes the base, so it changes the percent. The gap between the two quantities stays the same and is then measured against the other one.

Two changes in a row multiply. A rise of r1r_1r1​ followed by a fall of r2r_2r2​ leaves

(1+r1)(1−r2)(1 + r_1)(1 - r_2)(1+r1​)(1−r2​)

times the original amount, because the second change is taken of what the first one produced.

Percents add only when both are taken of the same amount. A 10%10\%10% tax and a 20%20\%20% tip charged on a bill of 100100100 dollars come to 130130130 dollars. A 20%20\%20% tip charged on the amount after tax comes to 132132132.

Why the rule exists

A percent is a share written over a fixed denominator. Putting every share over 100100100 makes two shares comparable at a glance, which two raw counts are not.

The multiplier follows from that. Take a number bbb and add rrr of it back on, and what you have is b+rbb + rbb+rb, which factors as b(1+r)b(1 + r)b(1+r). One multiplication then does the work of a percent-of step and an addition.

Division is what undoes a multiplication, so the value before a change is recovered by dividing. Adding the percent back cannot recover it, because the percent belongs to the number you are looking for rather than to the number you were handed.

Worked examples

1. Apply a percent increase with a multiplier

A storage locker rents for 175175175 dollars a month. The rent rises by 4%4\%4%. What is the new monthly rent?

Question asks: the rent after the rise.

First move: the base is the rent before the rise, so build the multiplier for a rise of 4%4\%4%.

1+0.04=1.041 + 0.04 = 1.041+0.04=1.04

Multiply the base by it.

175×1.04=182175 \times 1.04 = 182175×1.04=182

Answer: 182182182 dollars

Check: a change multiplier was used, and the change is a rise, so the answer must sit above 175175175. It does.

Common mistake: answering 777 gives the size of the rise rather than the new rent.

2. Find the value before a percent decrease

A desk lamp is marked down by 8%8\%8% and now sells for 464646 dollars. What did it sell for before the markdown?

Question asks: the price before the markdown.

First move: the base is that earlier price, and it is the unknown. Build the multiplier for a fall of 8%8\%8%.

1−0.08=0.921 - 0.08 = 0.921−0.08=0.92

The multiplier acts on the earlier price and produces the price on the tag, so call the earlier price ppp.

0.92p=460.92p = 460.92p=46

Divide both sides by 0.920.920.92.

p=50p = 50p=50

Answer: 505050 dollars

Check: a change multiplier was divided out, so put the answer back through the change.

0.92×50=460.92 \times 50 = 460.92×50=46

Common mistake: adding 8%8\%8% of the new price back on, as 46+0.08(46)46 + 0.08(46)46+0.08(46), gives 49.6849.6849.68 and not the earlier price. The 8%8\%8% was taken of the earlier price.

3. Take a percent of a percent

In a hiking group, 45%45\%45% of the members joined this year. Of the members who joined this year, 80%80\%80% signed up online. What percent of all the members joined this year and signed up online?

Question asks: a percent of all the members.

First move: find the base of each percent. The first is taken of the whole group, and the second is taken of the members who joined this year.

Write both percents as decimals and multiply.

0.45×0.80=0.360.45 \times 0.80 = 0.360.45×0.80=0.36

Answer: 36%36\%36%

Check: no change was crossed, so there is nothing to put back through one. Both percents are shares of a group rather than changes to it.

Common mistake: adding the two percents gives 125%125\%125%, which is more members than the group has.

Special cases

Percentage points, and two changes that do not cancel

A gap between two percents is measured in percentage points. A rate that moves from 32%32\%32% to 36%36\%36% has gained 444 percentage points. Measured against the rate itself, that gain is 432\frac{4}{32}324​, a rise of 12.5%12.5\%12.5%.

A fall and a rise of the same percent do not return to the start. Fall 20%20\%20%, then rise 20%20\%20%, and the two multipliers give

0.80×1.20=0.960.80 \times 1.20 = 0.960.80×1.20=0.96

which is 96%96\%96% of the starting amount. The fall is taken of the larger number and the rise of the smaller one, so the amount added is smaller than the amount removed.

Repeating one change over many periods is an exponential model, and the Advanced Math lesson on exponential functions covers it. This page stops at one change and at a pair of them.

Common traps

The wrong base

A percent means nothing until you know what it is a percent of. Taken of the wrong number, it gives a value that is right about the arithmetic and wrong about the question.

Trap Quick check
Taking the percent of the value after the change The base is the value before it
Adding the percent back to undo a fall Divide by the multiplier instead
Adding two percent changes together Multiply the multipliers when the second acts on the first's result
Reading a gap in percentage points as a percent change Divide the gap by the earlier rate, then multiply by 100100100
Reporting the size of a change where a percent was asked Read the last line of the question again

The method: Base, ask, form, check

  1. Name the base. Find the number the percent is taken of: the number that follows the word "of", the value before a change, or the group a share is measured against. Write it down before any arithmetic, because every other step depends on it.
  2. Name the ask. A part, a percent, the whole, the value after a change, the value before one, or the size of the change itself.
  3. Choose the form and carry it out. Multiply the base by rrr for a part. Divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100100100 for a percent. Divide the part by rrr for the whole. Use 1+r1 + r1+r or 1−r1 - r1−r to cross a change, and divide by it to cross back. For two changes in a row, multiply both multipliers.

  4. Check any change multiplier, then answer the ask. A change multiplier is the 1+r1 + r1+r or 1−r1 - r1−r that carries a value across a rise or a fall.

    If you multiplied by one, a rise must land above the base and a fall below it. If you divided by one, multiply your answer by it and check that the number the question gave you comes back. Nothing needs checking where no change was crossed.

Write the multiplier down first

A graphing calculator is built into the testing app and is allowed on every Math question.

Percent work is one multiplication or one division once the multiplier is on the page, and the calculator cannot tell you which number the percent is taken of.

Use it when the multiplier is awkward, such as dividing by 0.830.830.83 to undo a fall of 17%17\%17%. Then read the result back against the number the question gave you.

Practice

Work each item with the method, under a rough clock: about a minute for a bare equation, and up to about two minutes once there is a context to translate or a graph or table to read first. The Math section gives you about 95 seconds a question on average, so that range is test-day pace rather than a drill speed. Press Show solution to see the steps, one to a line.

181818 is 25%25\%25% of what number?

Correct. Dividing 181818 by 0.250.250.25 gives the whole, 727272.

The method, applied

Step 1. The base is the number the 25%25\%25% is taken of, and that number is the unknown.

Step 2. The ask is the whole, with the part and the percent both given.

Step 3. Divide the part by the percent written as a decimal.

180.25=72\frac{18}{0.25} = 720.2518​=72

Step 4. No change was crossed, so nothing goes back through one. The number is 727272.

A community center has 280280280 folding chairs. During a meeting, 70%70\%70% of the chairs are in use. How many of the chairs are not in use?

Correct. The chairs not in use are 30%30\%30% of 280280280, which is 848484.

The method, applied

Step 1. The base is the 280280280 chairs, since the percent is taken of them.

Step 2. The ask is a count, and the percent the stem states belongs to the chairs in use rather than to the ones the question wants.

Step 3. Take the complement of the stated share first.

1−0.70=0.301 - 0.70 = 0.301−0.70=0.30

Then multiply the base by it.

0.30×280=840.30 \times 280 = 840.30×280=84

Step 4. The 30%30\%30% names a share of the chairs rather than a change to them, so no change was crossed. The answer is 848484 chairs.

A farm stand sold 20%20\%20% fewer crates of apples on Friday than on Saturday. Saturday's total was what percent more than Friday's total?

Correct. The gap between the two days measured against Friday's smaller total is 25%25\%25%.

The method, applied

Step 1. The base is Friday's total, because the ask measures Saturday against it. The stem's own 20%20\%20% has the other base, Saturday's total.

Step 2. The ask is a percent more, so the gap between the two days goes over Friday's total.

Step 3. No count is given, so take Saturday as 100100100 crates. A fall of 20%20\%20% leaves Friday with 808080.

100−80=20100 - 80 = 20100−80=20

Divide the gap by Friday's total and multiply by 100100100.

100×2080=25100 \times \frac{20}{80} = 25100×8020​=25

Step 4. A fall was crossed, so Friday's total has to sit below Saturday's, and 808080 does. Saturday's total is 25%25\%25% more than Friday's.

A membership costs ddd dollars. The price rises by 15%15\%15% in the spring, and later in the year the raised price is cut by 25%25\%25%. Which expression gives the price after both changes?

Correct. The rise multiplies by 1.151.151.15 and the cut multiplies that result by 0.750.750.75, so the price is (1.15)(0.75)d(1.15)(0.75)d(1.15)(0.75)d.

The method, applied

Step 1. There are two bases. The first change is taken of ddd, and the second is taken of the price the first change produced.

Step 2. The ask is an expression for the price after both changes, so no number is computed.

Step 3. Write a multiplier for each change and apply them in order. A rise of 15%15\%15% gives 1.151.151.15, and a cut of 25%25\%25% gives 0.750.750.75.

(1.15)(0.75)d(1.15)(0.75)d(1.15)(0.75)d

Step 4. No change was carried out on a number, so nothing goes back through one. The expression is the product of the two multipliers and ddd.

A town recycled 15%15\%15% of its household waste in 2021 and 24%24\%24% of its household waste in 2024. The 2024 rate is what percent greater than the 2021 rate?

Correct. The gap of 999 percentage points measured against the 2021 rate of 151515 is 60%60\%60%.

The method, applied

Step 1. The base is the 2021 rate of 15%15\%15%, because the question measures the later rate against it.

Step 2. The ask is a percent change, not a gap in percentage points.

Step 3. Subtract to get the change.

24−15=924 - 15 = 924−15=9

Then divide the change by the base and multiply by 100100100.

100×915=60100 \times \frac{9}{15} = 60100×159​=60

Step 4. No change was crossed, so nothing goes back through one. The 2024 rate is 60%60\%60% greater than the 2021 rate.

A monthly water bill rose by 16%16\%16%, and the new bill is 878787 dollars. By how many dollars did the bill rise?

Correct. The bill before the rise was 757575 dollars, and 878787 less 757575 is 121212.

The method, applied

Step 1. The base is the bill before the rise, and the question hands over the bill after it.

Step 2. The ask is the size of the rise, so the earlier bill is on the way rather than the answer.

Step 3. A rise of 16%16\%16% multiplies by 1.161.161.16, so divide the new bill by that multiplier.

871.16=75\frac{87}{1.16} = 751.1687​=75

Then subtract to get the rise.

87−75=1287 - 75 = 1287−75=12

Step 4. A change multiplier was divided out, so put the earlier bill back through the rise.

75×1.16=8775 \times 1.16 = 8775×1.16=87

The bill rose by 121212 dollars.

In the first week, a crew resurfaced 30%30\%30% of a 484848-mile trail. In the second week, the crew resurfaced 25%25\%25% of the part that was still unresurfaced. How many miles of the trail were still unresurfaced at the end of the second week?

Correct. The second week leaves 75%75\%75% of the 33.633.633.6 unresurfaced miles, which is 25.225.225.2 miles.

The method, applied

Step 1. There are two bases. The first week is taken of the whole 484848 miles, and the second week is taken of the part still unresurfaced.

Step 2. The ask is a length still unresurfaced, so each stage leaves a remainder.

Step 3. Resurfacing 30%30\%30% leaves 70%70\%70% of the trail.

0.70×48=33.60.70 \times 48 = 33.60.70×48=33.6

Resurfacing 25%25\%25% of that leaves 75%75\%75% of it.

0.75×33.6=25.20.75 \times 33.6 = 25.20.75×33.6=25.2

Step 4. Two fall multipliers were used, so each result has to sit below the number it came from, and both do. The trail has 25.225.225.2 miles still unresurfaced.

The table shows the number of new library cards issued at four branches in two years.

Branch 2023 2024
Harbor 220 154
Ridge 400 436
Summit 130 169
Willow 60 90

Which branch had the greatest percent increase in new library cards from 2023 to 2024?

Correct. Willow gained 303030 cards on a 2023 count of 606060, an increase of 50%50\%50%.

The method, applied

Step 1. Each branch carries its own base, which is its own 2023 count.

Step 2. The ask is the greatest percent increase, so a gain in cards is not enough on its own.

Step 3. Harbor fell, so it is out. Divide each remaining gain by that branch's 2023 count: Ridge gained 363636 on 400400400, Summit gained 393939 on 130130130, and Willow gained 303030 on 606060.

100×3060=50100 \times \frac{30}{60} = 50100×6030​=50

Ridge comes to 9%9\%9% and Summit to 30%30\%30%, both below that.

Step 4. No change was crossed, so nothing goes back through one. Willow's increase of 50%50\%50% is the greatest.
