Probability

  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
  • Probability and conditional probability
←PreviousAll Math lessonsNext→

Decide first which group the pick is made from. That group's size is the number underneath, and naming a smaller group can change the answer.

Key takeaways

  • A probability is the favorable count divided by the size of the group drawn from.
  • Every probability lands between 000 and 111.
  • Wording like of those who names the group, so it sets the denominator.
  • In a two-way table, underneath goes the count of everyone the condition describes, and on top the cells the outcome covers.
  • With no group named, divide by the grand total.
  • The chance an outcome does not happen is 111 minus the chance it does.
  • The chance of AAA given BBB is not the same question as the chance of BBB given AAA.

What the SAT may ask

  • Find the probability of one outcome from a list of counts.
  • Find a probability for the whole group from a two-way table.
  • Find a probability inside a group the question names.
  • Find the probability that an outcome does not happen.
  • Fill in a missing count in a table, then take a probability.
  • Compare probabilities across the rows of a table.

The rule

A probability is a share of a group. Count the members the outcome describes, then divide by the size of the group the selection is made from.

P=favorable countsize of the group drawn fromP = \frac{\text{favorable count}}{\text{size of the group drawn from}}P=size of the group drawn fromfavorable count​

This rule needs every member of the group to be equally likely to be picked, which is what the words selected at random promise.

Every probability lands between 000 and 111. A value above 111 means the setup is wrong, so check both counts and which one went underneath.

The question names the group, and that group is the denominator.

A two-way table sorts one collection two ways at once. This one sorts 808080 bakery orders by what was ordered and by how it left the shop.

Order Collected Delivered Total
Bread 36 14 50
Pastry 8 22 30
Total 44 36 80

With no group named, divide by the grand total. For one of the 808080 orders selected at random, the chance it was delivered is:

P=3680=920P = \frac{36}{80} = \frac{9}{20}P=8036​=209​

A condition names a smaller group. One of the bread orders is selected at random draws from the 505050 bread orders, so 505050 goes underneath:

P=1450=725P = \frac{14}{50} = \frac{7}{25}P=5014​=257​

The order of the two groups matters. One of the delivered orders is selected at random draws from the 363636 delivered orders, which is a different question:

P=1436=718P = \frac{14}{36} = \frac{7}{18}P=3614​=187​

The same cell sits on top both times. What changed is the total under it.

The chance an outcome does not happen is 111 minus the chance it does. Inside a named group, take that difference inside the same group.

P(not delivered)=1−920P(\text{not delivered}) = 1 - \frac{9}{20}P(not delivered)=1−209​ P(not delivered)=1120P(\text{not delivered}) = \frac{11}{20}P(not delivered)=2011​

A fraction, a decimal, and a percent are all ways of writing one probability. A percent of a total is one only when the question makes a random pick from a named group.

A ratio can compare two parts or a part with the whole; a probability always compares the favorable count with the group drawn from. The bread and pastry orders stand in the ratio 505050 to 303030, and bread to all orders is 505050 to 808080. Only the second of those is the chance that a random order is bread.

Why the rule exists

A probability is one count compared with another, so it means nothing until you say which collection you counted in. Selected at random promises that every member of that collection is equally likely. Then the outcome's share of the collection is the chance of picking it.

That is why a condition can change the answer. One of the bread orders throws every pastry order out of the pool before the selection happens. The pool is smaller, and the shares inside it are different.

Every member either has the outcome or does not, so the two counts add to the size of the group and the two shares add to 111.

Worked examples

1. Read a probability off a frequency table

A chess club recorded how many matches each of its 404040 members played last month.

Matches played Members
0 6
1 11
2 14
3 7
4 2

One of the 404040 members is selected at random. What is the probability that the member played at least 222 matches?

Question asks: a probability for the whole club.

First move: no group is named beyond the club, so 404040 goes underneath. Then decide which rows count. At least 222 includes the row for 222.

14+7+2=2314 + 7 + 2 = 2314+7+2=23 P=2340P = \frac{23}{40}P=4023​

Answer: 2340\frac{23}{40}4023​

Common mistake: leaving out the row for 222. That reads more than 222 and gives 940\frac{9}{40}409​.

2. Use the total of the group the question names

The table shows the 100100100 students at a music school, by instrument and by whether they perform in the spring concert.

Instrument Performs Does not perform Total
Piano 30 12 42
Violin 22 13 35
Flute 15 8 23
Total 67 33 100

One of the students who plays the violin is selected at random. What is the probability that the student performs in the spring concert?

Question asks: a probability inside the violin group.

First move: one of the students who plays the violin names the group, so the violin row's total goes underneath. That total is 353535, not the 100100100 in the corner and not the 424242 or the 232323 beside it.

The count on top is the violin students who perform, which is the cell where that row meets the Performs column.

P=2235P = \frac{22}{35}P=3522​

Answer: 2235\frac{22}{35}3522​

Check: the question named a group, so check the denominator. The 353535 is the violin row's own total and the 222222 counts violin students, so both numbers come out of that one row.

Common mistake: dividing by the 676767 students who perform. That gives 2267\frac{22}{67}6722​, the chance that a performer plays the violin, which is the question asked backward.

3. Fill in a missing count before dividing

The table shows the 160160160 visitors to a museum on one Saturday, by ticket type and by whether they joined the guided tour. One entry is not shown.

Ticket Joined the tour Did not join Total
Adult 54 96
Student 34 30 64
Total 88 72 160

One of the visitors who did not join the tour is selected at random. What is the probability that the visitor bought an adult ticket?

Question asks: a probability inside the group who did not join the tour.

First move: find the missing entry, because it is the count the answer needs. Subtract along the adult row.

96−54=4296 - 54 = 4296−54=42

Its own column gives the same number, 72−3072 - 3072−30, which is a free check on it.

Now the group. One of the visitors who did not join the tour names it, so that column's total goes underneath.

P=4272=712P = \frac{42}{72} = \frac{7}{12}P=7242​=127​

Answer: 712\frac{7}{12}127​

Check: the 727272 is the Did not join column's own total and the 424242 counts visitors in that column, so both numbers come out of the same column.

Common mistake: dividing by the 969696 adult visitors. That gives 716\frac{7}{16}167​, the chance that an adult visitor stayed off the tour.

Special cases

Two picks in a row, and outcomes that overlap

Two picks multiply, never add. The chance of both is the chance of the first times the chance of the second given that the first has happened.

Put the first member back and the second pick meets the same group, so that second chance is the second outcome's own chance.

Keep the first one out and re-read both counts. The group is one smaller. The count on top drops only if the first pick was one of the members the second pick wants. A box holds 111111 counters, 666 of them blue. Take two without putting the first back, and the chance both are blue is:

611×510=311\frac{6}{11} \times \frac{5}{10} = \frac{3}{11}116​×105​=113​

Here the first pick was blue, so one blue counter has gone off the top and one counter off the bottom. Had the first not been blue, the top would still read 666.

Two outcomes that cannot both happen add. A drawn card is red or black, never both, so those chances add.

When they can both happen, add them and take the overlap off once. Of the 808080 bakery orders, 505050 are bread and 363636 were delivered, and 141414 are both. The chance a randomly selected order is bread or was delivered is:

P=50+36−1480=910P = \frac{50 + 36 - 14}{80} = \frac{9}{10}P=8050+36−14​=109​

Adding 505050 and 363636 alone counts those 141414 orders twice.

Common traps

The group you draw from

The number underneath is decided by the question, not by the table.

Trap Quick check
Dividing by the grand total when a group is named Underline the group in the question, then use its own size
Answering the reverse question Underneath goes the group the pick is made from, wherever the sentence names it
Putting one part over the other part Underneath goes the whole group, not the rest of it
Reporting the chance an outcome happens when the question asks that it does not Subtract from 111
Comparing counts across groups Divide inside each group first, then compare

The method: Ask, group, count, check

  1. Name the ask. A probability, a count, a group, or a statement. Write down which one, because everything after this step changes with it.
  2. Find the group the selection is made from. The question names it: given, of those who, one of the [group] is selected at random, or a randomly selected [member of the group]. With none of that wording, the group is everyone.
  3. Count the favorable members inside that group, and divide. Fill in a missing count first, by subtracting along its row or its column. Add the cells the outcome covers, then put that count over the number of members the whole condition describes.
  4. Answer the ask, and check the denominator whenever the question named a group and you put a count over a total. The number underneath has to be that group's own size, and the count on top has to come from inside it. Skip the check when the group is everyone, and when nothing was put over a total.

Divide to compare fractions

A graphing calculator is built into the testing app and is allowed on every Math question.

Use the calculator when several fractions have to be ranked. Fractions with different denominators are slow to rank by eye, so divide each one out and compare the decimals instead.

The Math section gives you about 95 seconds a question on average, so that range is test-day pace rather than a drill speed. Press Show solution to see the steps, one to a line.

A drawer holds 666 brass buttons, 999 wooden buttons, and 777 glass buttons. If one button is selected at random from the drawer, what is the probability that it is not a glass button?

Correct. The brass and wooden buttons are the ones that are not glass, so 151515 of the 222222 buttons in the drawer qualify, which is 1522\frac{15}{22}2215​.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a probability.

Step 2. No group is named beyond the drawer, so the group is every button in it.

6+9+7=226 + 9 + 7 = 226+9+7=22

Step 3. Not a glass button covers the brass buttons and the wooden ones.

6+9=156 + 9 = 156+9=15 P=1522P = \frac{15}{22}P=2215​

Step 4. The probability that the button is not glass is 1522\frac{15}{22}2215​.

The table shows how the 180180180 tickets sold for a school play were split by seating section and by performance night. No totals are shown.

Section Friday Saturday
Front 21 27
Middle 26 31
Rear 30 45

One of the 180180180 tickets is selected at random. What is the probability that it is a Front section ticket?

Correct. The Front section sold 484848 of the 180180180 tickets, which is 415\frac{4}{15}154​.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a probability.

Step 2. The question names no group beyond the 180180180 tickets, so 180180180 goes underneath.

Step 3. The table prints no row totals, so add the Front section's two nights.

21+27=4821 + 27 = 4821+27=48 P=48180=415P = \frac{48}{180} = \frac{4}{15}P=18048​=154​

Step 4. The probability that the ticket is a Front section ticket is 415\frac{4}{15}154​.

A drama club has 200200200 members. Of the 787878 members who signed up for the spring production, 545454 have attended every rehearsal. One of the members who signed up for the spring production is selected at random. What is the probability that the member has not attended every rehearsal?

Correct. Of the 787878 who signed up, 242424 have missed a rehearsal, which is 413\frac{4}{13}134​.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a probability.

Step 2. One of the members who signed up for the spring production names the group, so 787878 goes underneath rather than 200200200.

Step 3. Count the members of that group who have not attended every rehearsal.

78−54=2478 - 54 = 2478−54=24 P=2478=413P = \frac{24}{78} = \frac{4}{13}P=7824​=134​

Step 4. The question named a group and a count went over a total, so check the denominator. The 787878 is that group's own size and the 242424 counts members of it. The probability is 413\frac{4}{13}134​.

An orchard packed 200200200 crates of pears last week, and 969696 of those crates were graded premium. The probability that a randomly selected premium crate was packed on Tuesday is 38\frac{3}{8}83​. How many premium crates were packed on Tuesday?

Correct. Three eighths of the 969696 premium crates is 363636.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a count, not a probability.

Step 2. A randomly selected premium crate names the group, so the 38\frac{3}{8}83​ describes the 969696 premium crates rather than all 200200200.

Step 3. A probability times the size of its group gives the count.

96×38=3696 \times \frac{3}{8} = 3696×83​=36

Step 4. Nothing was put over a total, so no denominator check applies. The orchard packed 363636 premium crates on Tuesday.

A craft guild has four chapters. The table shows how many members of each chapter entered this year's annual show.

Chapter Entered Did not enter Total
Aspen 105 45 150
Birch 96 64 160
Cedar 45 80 125
Elm 60 15 75

For which chapter is the probability greatest that a randomly selected member of that chapter entered the annual show?

Correct. Elm's 606060 entries out of 757575 members is 0.80.80.8, the largest share in the table.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a chapter, not a number.

Step 2. A randomly selected member of that chapter names a different group in every row, so each chapter's own total goes underneath.

Step 3. Divide each chapter's entries by its own size and compare the decimals.

105150=0.7\frac{105}{150} = 0.7150105​=0.7 96160=0.6\frac{96}{160} = 0.616096​=0.6 45125=0.36\frac{45}{125} = 0.3612545​=0.36 6075=0.8\frac{60}{75} = 0.87560​=0.8

Step 4. The question named a group, so check the denominators. Every total used is its own chapter's and every count on top comes from that chapter's row. Elm is the greatest, at 0.80.80.8.

One of the swimmers who trains in the morning is selected at random. What is the probability that the swimmer is in the beginner group or the intermediate group?

A community workshop took 240240240 sign-ups. The table shows them by session and by whether the person had attended a workshop before.

Session Attended before First time Total
Morning 40 50 90
Afternoon 38 52 90
Evening 22 38 60
Total 100 140 240

One of the sign-ups who did not choose the evening session is selected at random. What is the probability that the person had attended a workshop before?

Correct. Setting the evening session aside leaves 180180180 sign-ups, 787878 of whom had attended before, which is 1330\frac{13}{30}3013​.

The method, applied

Step 1. The pick is made from the sign-ups who did not choose the evening session, so that group is the denominator. The table prints no total for it.

Step 2. Build it by taking the evening row off the bottom row.

240−60=180240 - 60 = 180240−60=180

Step 3. The count on top is the people in that same group who had attended before, which is the attended-before column less its evening entry.

100−22=78100 - 22 = 78100−22=78 P=78180P = \frac{78}{180}P=18078​ 78180=1330\frac{78}{180} = \frac{13}{30}18078​=3013​

Step 4. The question named a group and a count went over a total, so check the denominator. The 180180180 is the size of the group the pick is made from, and the 787878 counts people inside it. The probability is 1330\frac{13}{30}3013​.

The table shows the 250250250 people who attended a regional conference, by whether they came for the first time and by whether they attended a workshop.

Attendee Attended a workshop Did not attend a workshop Total
First time 44 16 60
Returning 101 89 190
Total 145 105 250

The fraction 4460\frac{44}{60}6044​ gives the probability of which of the following events?

Correct. The 606060 is the First time row's total, so the selection is from those attendees, and 444444 of them attended a workshop.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a statement, so nothing is computed.

Step 2. Find the group by reading the number underneath. The 606060 is the First time row's total, so the selection is from the attendees who came for the first time.

Step 3. The 444444 on top is the cell where that row meets the workshop column, so the outcome is attending a workshop.
