Two-Variable Data and Scatterplots

  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
  • Two-variable data: models and scatterplots
←PreviousAll Math lessonsNext→

A scatterplot shows what was recorded, and a line of best fit shows what a model predicts. Decide first whether the question wants a recorded value, a predicted value, the gap between them, or what a number in the model means.

Key takeaways

  • A plotted point is data. The line of best fit is a model, which may pass through every point, some, or none.
  • Predict by putting the input into the model, never by reading the nearest point.
  • Residual equals recorded minus predicted. A positive residual puts the point above the line, where the model predicted too low.
  • The slope of a fitted line is the change the model predicts for each one unit of input.
  • The constant is the model's prediction at an input of zero, which may sit outside the data.
  • Check the input against the range the data covers: inside it is interpolation, outside it is extrapolation.
  • A straight line is not the only model. One turn is a reason to consider a quadratic, and a constant ratio over equal steps an exponential.

What the SAT may ask

  • Predict a value from a line of best fit, or from its equation.
  • Find the residual, or say whether the model predicted too high or too low.
  • Say what the slope or the constant of a fitted model means, with units.
  • Choose the equation, or the shape of model, that fits a set of data.
  • Decide which claim a model and its data support.
  • Find the input at which the model predicts a stated value.

The rule

Two-variable data pairs two measurements of the same thing. Each paper airplane in the figure gives a wingspan and a glide distance, and each pair is one point.

Describe an association by its direction, its form and its strength. Direction is whether the points rise or fall to the right. Form is whether they follow a line or a curve. Strength is how closely they follow it.

Points that rise to the right are a positive association and points that fall to the right are a negative association. Points that climb and then fall have no single direction, and they can still be closely related. Say no association only when the points show no pattern at all.

A line of best fit is a model of the overall pattern. It is the one straight line that runs closest to the whole cloud, and it may pass through all of the points, some of them, or none. The line drawn beside this rule is:

y=0.5x−1y = 0.5x - 1y=0.5x−1

Here xxx is a wingspan in centimeters and yyy is a glide distance in meters.

To predict, put the input into the model. At a wingspan of 222222:

y=0.5(22)−1y = 0.5(22) - 1y=0.5(22)−1 y=10y = 10y=10

The plane with that wingspan actually glided 111111 meters.

The residual is the recorded value minus the predicted value.

residual=recorded−predicted\text{residual} = \text{recorded} - \text{predicted}residual=recorded−predicted

For that plane the residual is 11−1011 - 1011−10, which is 111. A positive residual puts the point above the line, so the model predicted too low. A negative residual puts the point below the line, so the model predicted too high.

The slope of a fitted line is a predicted rate. The 0.50.50.5 says the model predicts half a meter more glide for each extra centimeter of wingspan. It does not say that any one plane gained half a meter.

The constant is the model's prediction when the input is zero. For this line that is −1-1−1 meter.

Why the rule exists

A model is needed because one input can come with different outputs. Two planes with the same wingspan may glide different distances, and no single rule reaches both points. A line of best fit describes the pattern the whole cloud shares instead.

Where a good fit does miss, the misses stay small and fall on both sides of the line.

Worked examples

1. Find the gap between one board and the model

A lumber yard measured the length and the mass of each of several oak boards. The line of best fit is m=4L+9m = 4L + 9m=4L+9, where LLL is the length in feet and mmm is the mass in pounds. One board is 777 feet long and has a mass of 343434 pounds. What is the residual for that board?

Question asks: the gap between a recorded mass and the model's prediction.

First move: a residual needs the prediction, so put the length into the model first.

m=4(7)+9m = 4(7) + 9m=4(7)+9 m=37m = 37m=37

The model predicts 373737 pounds and the board recorded 343434. Subtract in that order: recorded minus predicted.

34−37=−334 - 37 = -334−37=−3

Answer: −3-3−3 pounds

The model only ran forwards here, so there is nothing to put back into it.

The sign carries the direction: a negative residual puts this board's point below the line.

Common mistake: subtracting the other way round and answering 333. That number is the size of the miss, and it hides which way the model went wrong.

2. Say what a fitted rate means

At each of several spots on a lake, a survey recorded the water depth and the water temperature. The line of best fit is T=24−0.8dT = 24 - 0.8dT=24−0.8d, where ddd is the depth in meters and TTT is the temperature in degrees Celsius. What does 0.80.80.8 mean in this model?

Question asks: the meaning of the rate, not a value.

First move: attach the units. The rate multiplies ddd, which is in meters, and the output is in degrees, so the rate is in degrees per meter.

The sign in front of it is a minus, so the model predicts a fall rather than a rise.

Answer: the model predicts a temperature 0.80.80.8 degrees lower for each additional meter of depth.

Common mistake: saying the temperature drops by exactly 0.80.80.8 degrees per meter. The spots are scattered around the line, so that rate belongs to the model and to no pair of spots.

3. Decide when no straight line fits

A meadow survey counted the open blossoms in one plot at the end of each week. Would a line of best fit model these counts well?

Week Open blossoms
111 666
222 191919
333 282828
444 272727
555 161616
666 444

Question asks: whether a straight line is the right shape.

First move: read every row before fitting anything. The counts climb from 666 to 282828, then fall back to 444, so the pattern turns once.

A straight line holds one direction. It rises everywhere, falls everywhere, or stays flat, so it cannot climb and then fall.

Answer: no. The counts turn once, which is a reason to consider a quadratic model.

Common mistake: fitting a line because the first three weeks rise steadily. The opening rows of a table do not settle its shape.

Special cases

The limits of a fitted line

A constant belongs to an input of zero, which can sit outside the data. No plane has a wingspan of zero, so report the fitted line's −1-1−1 meter there as the model's prediction, never as something measured.

A prediction inside the range the data covers is interpolation, and one outside it is extrapolation. That line was fitted to wingspans from 101010 to 262626 centimeters. At 181818 centimeters recorded points sit on both sides, and the prediction can still miss. At 606060 there are none, so nothing measured limits how far the line drifts.

Match the shape of the model to the shape of the data. A trend that climbs or falls at a steady rate calls for a line. A pattern that turns once is a reason to consider a quadratic. Values that change by about the same factor over equal input steps are a reason to consider an exponential.

On a table with equal input steps, equal first differences say linear, equal and nonzero second differences say quadratic, and equal positive ratios say exponential. Recorded data may follow one of these exactly or only come close.

An association is not a cause. A rising cloud says the two measurements move together, and nothing more. Whether a cause can be read into it depends on how the study was designed, not on the plot.

Common traps

The point that is not the prediction

At any one input the plot carries two numbers, and either can look like the answer.

Trap Quick check
Reading the nearest plotted point as the prediction Read up to the line, not to the dot
Subtracting the recorded value from the prediction Residual is recorded minus predicted
Calling a curved cloud no association Look for a curve before ruling out a pattern
Reading a fitted rate as every point's exact change The rate belongs to the model, not to one point
Trusting a prediction far outside the data Compare the input with the range that was measured

The method: Name the ask, split data from model, work, answer

  1. Name the ask. A predicted value, a recorded value, the gap between them, an input, what a number in the model means, or how far the model can be trusted. Write it down first, because the ask alone decides the next move.
  2. Split the data from the model. A plotted point and a table row are recorded values. The line or curve of best fit and its equation are predictions.
  3. Do the one move the ask names. Substitute an input for a prediction. Subtract the prediction from the recorded value for a residual. Attach units to the rate and to the constant for an interpretation. Set the model equal to a value and solve for an input. Do one thing to a line.
  4. Answer the ask, then check inverse work or a chosen model. Substitute a solved input back into the model. Recompute the residual you turned into a recorded value. Test a chosen equation against two rows. Skip the check for anything the model produced going forwards, a reading, a count, a comparison, an interpretation, or a judgment about shape.

The scatterplot above shows the firmness score and the number of weeks aged for each of nine cheese wheels, with the line of best fit drawn through them. That line is y=2x+6y = 2x + 6y=2x+6, where xxx is the number of weeks aged. What firmness score does the line of best fit predict for a wheel aged 888 weeks?

Correct. Putting 888 into the fitted line gives a predicted score of 222222.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is the value the model predicts, not the value that was recorded.

Step 2. Split the two. The dot above 888 on the horizontal axis is data, and it sits below the line. The line is the model, and its equation is printed in the question.

Step 3. Put 888 into the equation of the line.

y=2(8)+6y = 2(8) + 6y=2(8)+6 y=22y = 22y=22

Step 4. The line of best fit predicts a firmness score of 222222 for a wheel aged 888 weeks.

A line of best fit is drawn for a set of two-variable data. At one recorded point, the recorded value is greater than the value the line predicts for that input. Where does that point lie?

Correct. The recorded value is the higher of the two at that input, so the point sits above the line.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is where a point sits, so nothing has to be computed.

Step 2. Split the two numbers at that input. The recorded value belongs to the point and the predicted value belongs to the line.

Step 3. The residual is the recorded value minus the predicted value, and the question says the first is larger, so the residual is positive.

Step 4. A positive residual puts the point above the line of best fit.

Practice 3

  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
  • Two-variable data: models and scatterplots
  • Standard

At each of several ponds, a survey counted the reed beds and the dragonflies. The line of best fit for the data is y=8x+7y = 8x + 7y=8x+7, where xxx is the number of reed beds and yyy is the number of dragonflies. At one pond with 666 reed beds, the residual is −7-7−7. How many dragonflies were counted at that pond?

Correct. The model predicts 555555 and the residual is −7-7−7, so the count recorded is 484848.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a recorded count, and the residual is what connects it to the model.

Step 2. Split the two. The equation gives the prediction, and the residual says how far the count sits from that prediction.

Step 3. Predict first.

y=8(6)+7y = 8(6) + 7y=8(6)+7 y=55y = 55y=55

A residual is recorded minus predicted, so the recorded count is the prediction plus the residual.

55+(−7)=4855 + (-7) = 4855+(−7)=48

Step 4. The model ran backwards, so check. Taking the prediction off 484848 gives −7-7−7, the residual the question states. That pond had 484848 dragonflies.

A syrup evaporator recorded five runs. The table shows the hours it ran and the quarts of syrup it drew off, and the line of best fit is q=3h+5q = 3h + 5q=3h+5.

Hours hhh Quarts qqq
6 27
12 33
18 65
24 73
30 97

For which number of hours is the number of quarts recorded furthest above what the model predicts?

Correct. The model predicts 595959 quarts at 181818 hours and 656565 were recorded, which is 666 above and the largest such gap on the table.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask names a gap between what was recorded and what the model predicts, so both sides are in play and one row has to win.

Step 2. Split the two. The quarts column is recorded data and q=3h+5q = 3h + 5q=3h+5 is the model, so predict at each row before comparing anything.

Step 3. The model gives 232323, 414141, 595959, 777777 and 959595 quarts. Take the recorded value less the predicted one at each row.

27−23=427 - 23 = 427−23=4 33−41=−833 - 41 = -833−41=−8 65−59=665 - 59 = 665−59=6 73−77=−473 - 77 = -473−77=−4 97−95=297 - 95 = 297−95=2

Step 4. Only 444, 666 and 222 sit above the model, and 666 is the largest of them, at 181818 hours.

Practice 5

  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis

  • Standard

Over five weeks a market stall recorded the price of a jar of jam and the number of jars sold. Which equation is the line of best fit for these data?

Price in dollars, xxx Jars sold, yyy
222 454545
444 383838
666 303030
888 262626
101010 212121
Correct. Sales fall about 333 jars for each dollar and the line reaches 505050 jars at a price of zero.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is which straight line models the data, so no single value is wanted.

Step 2. Split the two. The table is data and each choice is a candidate model, so the work is holding models up against rows.

Step 3. Read the direction first. Sales fall as the price rises, so any rising line is out. Now size the rate. Across the table the count drops from 454545 to 212121 while the price rises from 222 to 101010.

45−2110−2=3\frac{45 - 21}{10 - 2} = 310−245−21​=3

The model falls about 333 jars per dollar, which rules out the line falling 666. Two candidates are left, so test each constant against the first row.

45−3(2)=3945 - 3(2) = 3945−3(2)=39

That is 666 short of the 454545 recorded, and this line falls short at every other price too, so the whole table would sit above it.

50−3(2)=4450 - 3(2) = 4450−3(2)=44

That leaves the first row just above the line, and the third row just below it.

Step 4. An equation was chosen, so check. At six dollars it predicts 323232 against a recorded 303030, and at eight dollars it predicts 262626 against a recorded 262626. The line of best fit is y=50−3xy = 50 - 3xy=50−3x.

Practice 6

  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
  • Two-variable data: models and scatterplots
  • Advanced

A gardener recorded the length of each of several rectangular beds and the number of bulbs planted in it. The bed lengths ranged from 121212 to 303030 feet. The line of best fit is b=2L+9b = 2L + 9b=2L+9, where LLL is the length in feet and bbb is the number of bulbs. Which statement is best supported by these data and this model?

Correct. A length of 202020 feet sits inside the range measured, and the model gives 494949 bulbs there.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is which statement the data and the model support together, so two things have to hold: the arithmetic, and the range the data covers.

Step 2. Split the two. The lengths from 121212 to 303030 feet are what was measured. The equation is what the model predicts, and it returns a number for any length at all.

Step 3. Take the length that sits inside the measured range and put it into the model.

b=2(20)+9b = 2(20) + 9b=2(20)+9 b=49b = 49b=49

Step 4. The model predicts about 494949 bulbs for a bed 202020 feet long.

Practice 7

  • Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
  • Two-variable data: models and scatterplots
  • Advanced

A workshop recorded the mass of wax in each of several hand-dipped candles and how long the candle burned. The masses ranged from 404040 to 909090 grams. The line of best fit is b=3w+8b = 3w + 8b=3w+8, where www is the mass of wax in grams and bbb is the burn time in minutes. What is the best interpretation of 888 in this model?

Correct. Setting the mass of wax to zero leaves 888, which is the burn time the model predicts there.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is what a number means, so nothing is solved.

Step 2. Split the two numbers in the model. The 333 multiplies the mass and is a rate. The 888 stands alone and is what is left when the mass is zero.

Step 3. Set the mass of wax to zero.

b=3(0)+8b = 3(0) + 8b=3(0)+8 b=8b = 8b=8

The lightest candle recorded held 404040 grams, so a mass of zero sits outside the data. That makes 888 minutes a prediction of the model rather than anything measured.

Step 4. The model predicts a burn time of 888 minutes for a candle containing no wax.

A school ran five car wash fundraisers. The line of best fit for the data below is y=3x+14y = 3x + 14y=3x+14, where xxx is the number of volunteers and yyy is the number of cars washed. At how many of the five fundraisers does the model predict more cars than were washed?

Volunteers, xxx Cars washed, yyy
222 191919
444 242424
666 373737
888 383838
101010 424242
Correct. The model predicts more cars than were washed at 333 of the five fundraisers.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a count of rows, and the test on each row is a comparison rather than a value.

Step 2. Split the two columns. The table gives what was washed and the equation gives what the model predicts, so every row needs both.

Step 3. Predict at each row. At two volunteers:

y=3(2)+14y = 3(2) + 14y=3(2)+14 y=20y = 20y=20

The five predictions are 202020, 262626, 323232, 383838 and 444444. Set each against the count beside it. The model is above the count at two, four and ten volunteers, below it at six, and level with it at eight.

Step 4. The model predicts more cars than were washed at 333 of the five fundraisers.
