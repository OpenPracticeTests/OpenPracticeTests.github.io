Lines, Angles, and Triangles

  • Geometry and Trigonometry
  • Lines, angles, and triangles
←PreviousAll Math lessonsNext→

A question here gives you one measure and asks for another. Decide first whether the ask is an angle, a length, or a range of lengths, then find the relationship that connects what you were given to it.

Key takeaways

  • Angles on one side of a straight line add to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘, and angles around a point add to 360∘360^{\circ}360∘.
  • Where two lines cross, the two angles across from each other are equal.
  • The three angles of any triangle add to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘.
  • When a transversal cuts lines that are parallel, the eight angles take at most two sizes, and any two sizes add to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘.
  • In one triangle, two sides are equal exactly when the two angles opposite them are equal, and a larger angle faces a longer side.
  • Two matching angles make two triangles similar. That on its own does not make them congruent.
  • A third side is strictly between the difference and the sum of the other two.

What the SAT may ask

  • Find an angle where a transversal cuts two parallel lines.
  • Find an angle of a triangle from the other two, or from an exterior angle.
  • Find an angle in a triangle that has two equal sides.
  • Solve for a variable when two angle measures are written as expressions.
  • Find a side length from two similar triangles.
  • Say which lengths can be the third side of a triangle.

The rule

Angles that fill a whole add to what the whole measures. Angles on one side of a straight line add to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘. Angles around a point add to 360∘360^{\circ}360∘. Where two lines cross, the two angles across from each other are equal.

The three angles of any triangle add to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘. The reference sheet our practice tests carry hands you that fact. It carries none of the exterior-angle or transversal rules below.

An angle outside a triangle, formed by extending one side, equals the sum of the two interior angles at the other two vertices. It is also the supplement of the interior angle beside it, so either route reaches it.

When a transversal cuts two parallel lines, the eight angles it makes take at most two sizes. In the figure, angles 1, 3, 5 and 7 are all one size and angles 2, 4, 6 and 8 are all the other, and one from each group adds to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘. A transversal that meets the lines at a right angle makes all eight 90∘90^{\circ}90∘, which is one size.

Pair Where they sit Needs parallel lines
1 and 5, corresponding The same corner at each crossing Yes; equal
3 and 5, alternate interior Between the lines, opposite sides of the transversal Yes; equal
3 and 6, co-interior Between the lines, the same side of the transversal Yes; add to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘
1 and 3, vertical Across one crossing point No; equal either way
1 and 2, a linear pair Side by side on line mmm No; add to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘ either way

Only the first three rows need the lines to be parallel. The last two hold at any crossing, because neither of them looks at the second line.

In one triangle, two sides are equal exactly when the two angles opposite them are equal. In triangle ABCABCABC with AB=ACAB = ACAB=AC, those are the angles at BBB and at CCC.

The sides of one triangle run in the same order as the angles they face. A larger angle faces a longer side, and the longest side faces the largest angle.

Two triangles are similar when two angles of one match two angles of the other. The third pair then matches on its own, because the three angles add to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘.

Matching angles fix the shape and say nothing about the size, so on their own they establish similarity and not congruence. Similar triangles are congruent when the factor between them turns out to be 111.

Write the correspondence before you write the proportion. Reading △ABC∼△DEF\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF△ABC∼△DEF in order, AAA matches DDD, BBB matches EEE, and CCC matches FFF. Sides follow the vertices: ABABAB matches DEDEDE, BCBCBC matches EFEFEF, and ACACAC matches DFDFDF. Where the naming is not given, match sides by the angles they sit opposite.

Every length of a similar triangle is multiplied by the same factor kkk. So one matched pair gives kkk, and kkk then reaches every other pair.

DEAB=EFBC=k\frac{DE}{AB} = \frac{EF}{BC} = kABDE​=BCEF​=k

A perimeter is a sum of lengths, so it is multiplied by kkk too, which is a second way to find the factor.

Any two sides of a triangle add to more than the third. So a third side is strictly between the difference and the sum of the other two.

∣a−b∣<c<a+b|a - b| < c < a + b∣a−b∣<c<a+b

Why the rule exists

Take any triangle and draw a line through one vertex parallel to the side opposite it. That line and the two sides leaving the vertex make three angles that fill one side of a straight line, so they add to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘.

The outer two are alternate interior angles with the triangle's other two, so they are equal to them, and the middle one is the triangle's third angle. That is why a triangle's three angles add to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘.

The exterior angle rule falls out of the same sum. The interior angle beside it makes 180∘180^{\circ}180∘ with it, and the three interior angles also make 180∘180^{\circ}180∘, so what is left over is the other two.

Enlarging leaves the angles alone and multiplies every length by the same factor. That is why one matched pair settles the ratio for all of them, and why a perimeter carries the factor once.

k⋅a+k⋅b+k⋅c=k(a+b+c)k \cdot a + k \cdot b + k \cdot c = k(a + b + c)k⋅a+k⋅b+k⋅c=k(a+b+c)

Going from one end of side ccc to the other along the other two sides is a bend, and a bend is longer than the straight run. So a+b>ca + b > ca+b>c, and rearranging that for the other two sides gives the lower bound.

Worked examples

1. Set two alternate interior angles equal, then report the angle

Lines mmm and nnn are parallel and are cut by a transversal. One of the two alternate interior angles measures (4x+9)∘(4x + 9)^{\circ}(4x+9)∘, and the other measures (6x−21)∘(6x - 21)^{\circ}(6x−21)∘. What is the measure, in degrees, of each of those two angles?

Step 1. The ask is an angle measure in degrees. It is not xxx, and xxx has no unit here at all.

Step 2. The lines are parallel, and the two angles are alternate interior angles, so they are equal. That is the connection: two names for one measure.

Step 3. Set the two expressions equal and solve.

4x+9=6x−214x + 9 = 6x - 214x+9=6x−21 30=2x30 = 2x30=2x x=15x = 15x=15

Now turn xxx into the measure the ask names.

4(15)+9=694(15) + 9 = 694(15)+9=69

Step 4. Two expressions were set equal, so put the value into both and confirm they agree.

6(15)−21=696(15) - 21 = 696(15)−21=69

Each of the two angles measures 69∘69^{\circ}69∘.

2. Take the scale factor from two perimeters

Triangle ABCABCABC is similar to triangle DEFDEFDEF. The perimeter of triangle ABCABCABC is 242424 and the perimeter of triangle DEFDEFDEF is 363636. If AB=6AB = 6AB=6, what is the length of DEDEDE?

Step 1. The ask is one length, DEDEDE. Nothing is stated about any side of DEFDEFDEF, so the factor has to come from somewhere other than a matched pair of sides.

Step 2. Every length of DEFDEFDEF is the matching length of ABCABCABC multiplied by the same factor kkk. A perimeter is a sum of three lengths, so it carries that factor once as well. Two perimeters therefore give kkk.

Step 3. Write the factor, then apply it to ABABAB. Reading the similarity in order, AAA matches DDD and BBB matches EEE, so DEDEDE is the side that matches ABABAB.

k=3624k = \frac{36}{24}k=2436​ k=32k = \frac{3}{2}k=23​ DE=6⋅32DE = 6 \cdot \frac{3}{2}DE=6⋅23​ DE=9DE = 9DE=9

Step 4. The factor is larger than 111, so DEDEDE has to come out larger than ABABAB, and 999 is larger than 666. The length of DEDEDE is 999.

3. Establish the similarity first, then run the ratio

Segments ACACAC and BDBDBD cross at point PPP. Segment ABABAB is parallel to segment DCDCDC. If AP=14AP = 14AP=14, PC=21PC = 21PC=21, and AB=8AB = 8AB=8, what is the length of DCDCDC?

Step 1. The ask is the length DCDCDC. The figure has the two segments crossing at PPP and the chevrons marking ABABAB and DCDCDC as parallel. Nothing yet says the two triangles are similar, so that has to be established before any ratio is written.

Step 2. Two relationships do it. Angle APBAPBAPB and angle CPDCPDCPD are across the crossing point at PPP, so they are equal. And ABABAB is parallel to DCDCDC with ACACAC cutting both, so angle BAPBAPBAP and angle DCPDCPDCP are alternate interior angles and are equal too.

Two matching angles make triangle APBAPBAPB similar to triangle CPDCPDCPD, with AAA matching CCC, PPP matching PPP, and BBB matching DDD.

Step 3. The correspondence pairs APAPAP with CPCPCP and pairs ABABAB with CDCDCD. Write one ratio equal to the other.

DCAB=CPAP\frac{DC}{AB} = \frac{CP}{AP}ABDC​=APCP​ DC8=2114\frac{DC}{8} = \frac{21}{14}8DC​=1421​ DC8=32\frac{DC}{8} = \frac{3}{2}8DC​=23​ DC=12DC = 12DC=12

Step 4. The factor 32\frac{3}{2}23​ is larger than 111, and DCDCDC came out larger than the ABABAB it was scaled from. The length of DCDCDC is 121212.

Special cases

Cases the main rules do not settle

An equilateral triangle. Three equal sides give three equal angles, and three equal angles adding to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘ makes each 60∘60^{\circ}60∘. It runs backwards too.

A transversal across lines that are not parallel. The corresponding, alternate interior and co-interior pairs all fail. Angles on a straight line still add to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘ and angles across a crossing point are still equal, because neither of those needs a second line.

The stem can say two lines are parallel, and an arrow mark on the figure can. So can a stated angle relationship that only parallel lines give, such as two corresponding angles said to be equal.

Matching angles with no matching side. Two triangles with all three angles equal are similar and can be any two sizes at all. Nothing follows about a length until some pair of matching lengths is given, and that pair is what sets the factor. A perimeter is a sum of lengths, so two of those will do it.

What it takes to make two triangles congruent. Congruent means same shape and same size, so a length has to be involved. Four sets of facts are enough on their own.

Set What matches
SSS All three pairs of sides
SAS Two pairs of sides and the angle between them
ASA or AAS Two pairs of angles and any one pair of sides
Hypotenuse-leg In two right triangles, the hypotenuses and one pair of legs

Three matching angles are not on that list: they give similarity only. Neither is two sides with an angle that is not between them, which can leave two triangles that differ. Hypotenuse-leg is a case of that shape which does settle it, and the right angle is what makes it one.

Three lengths that make no triangle. When the two shorter lengths add to the longest or to less than it, the two short sides cannot meet. There is no triangle to find angles in, so check the three lengths before doing anything else with them.

Common traps

Reading a relationship off the picture instead of off the marks

Work from the stated measures and the marks on the drawing, and read any note that says the figure is not drawn to scale. How long a side looks and how open an angle looks are not facts.

Trap Quick check
Two lines treated as parallel because they look it It takes a stem, a mark, or a stated angle relationship
Sides matched by the order they appear in the stem Match them through the correspondence the similarity statement gives
The ratio used upside down Scaling up gives a longer side than the one you started from; scaling down gives a shorter one
The difference between two sides added on Similar figures scale by a factor, not by a shared increase
A bound treated as a length the third side can take The third side is strictly between the difference and the sum
A longer side stated opposite a smaller angle The sides run in the same order as the angles they face

The method: Mark, connect, solve, check

  1. Write the ask down, then mark everything the stem states. Arrows on the parallel lines, ticks on the equal sides, and every angle measure you are given, on the figure or on a sketch of your own. A fact you do not mark is a fact you will not use.

  2. Find the relationship that connects what you know to what you want. Angles on a line and around a point, angles across a crossing point, the triangle's 180∘180^{\circ}180∘, the exterior angle, and the transversal pairs where the lines are parallel.

    Equal sides give equal angles opposite them, and two matching angles make two triangles similar. If the angle you want sits on neither parallel line, draw a line through it parallel to both and use the transversal pairs twice.

  3. Write one equation and solve it. For an angle, that is a sum set to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘, two measures set equal to each other, or the pieces of a split angle added. For a length, pair each side with the side it corresponds to first, then set one ratio equal to another and solve.

  4. Answer what the ask named, then run the check that fits it. Put an angle you built from parts back into its parts. Where the parts are a triangle's angles, the three must reach 180∘180^{\circ}180∘; where a line splits one angle, the pieces must rebuild it.

    Two expressions you set equal must give the same measure when your value goes back into both. A length from similar triangles must come out longer than the side it was scaled from exactly when the factor is larger than 111.

    A length answering a range must sit inside it. And in any one triangle, the sides have to run in the same order as the angles they face.

The arithmetic is small and the setup is not

A graphing calculator is built into the testing app and is allowed on every Math question.

Nearly every step on this page is one subtraction from 180180180 or one multiplication by a ratio. Reach for the calculator only when a ratio is not a whole number.

Write the relationship and the correspondence down first. A ratio typed before the sides are paired can go in either way up, and the calculator returns the wrong one as readily as the right one.

In triangle ABCABCABC, AB=ACAB = ACAB=AC and angle BACBACBAC measures 34∘34^{\circ}34∘. What is the measure, in degrees, of angle ABCABCABC?

Correct. Angle ABCABCABC sits opposite ACACAC and angle ACBACBACB sits opposite ABABAB, and those two sides are equal, so the two angles are equal and each is half of 180−34180 - 34180−34, which is 737373.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is one angle, angle ABCABCABC, in degrees. Mark ABABAB and ACACAC as equal.

Step 2. Equal sides give equal angles opposite them. Angle ABCABCABC is opposite ACACAC and angle ACBACBACB is opposite ABABAB, so those two angles are equal. The third relationship is the triangle's 180∘180^{\circ}180∘.

Step 3. Call each of the two equal angles bbb and write one equation.

34+b+b=18034 + b + b = 18034+b+b=180 2b=1462b = 1462b=146 b=73b = 73b=73

Step 4. Put the parts back: 34+73+73=18034 + 73 + 73 = 18034+73+73=180. Angle ABCABCABC measures 737373 degrees.

Two sides of a triangle have lengths 888 and 151515. The third side has a length that is an integer. What is the greatest possible perimeter of the triangle?

Correct. The third side has to be less than 8+15=238 + 15 = 238+15=23, so the greatest integer it can be is 222222, and the three sides then add to 454545.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a perimeter, so the answer is the three side lengths added. Two of them are 888 and 151515; call the third ccc.

Step 2. The relationship is the one between three side lengths: ccc is strictly between the difference and the sum of the other two.

Step 3. Write that range, then take the largest integer inside it.

7<c<237 < c < 237<c<23 c=22c = 22c=22 8+15+22=458 + 15 + 22 = 458+15+22=45

Step 4. Check that 222222 sits strictly inside the range rather than on an end: it is greater than 777 and less than 232323. The greatest possible perimeter is 454545.

In triangle ABCABCABC and triangle DEFDEFDEF, angle AAA is equal to angle DDD and angle CCC is equal to angle FFF. If AB=9AB = 9AB=9, BC=12BC = 12BC=12, and DE=15DE = 15DE=15, what is the length of EFEFEF?

Correct. Two matching angles force the third pair to match too, so BBB matches EEE, and EFEFEF is 159\frac{15}{9}915​ of BCBCBC, which is 202020.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is the length EFEFEF. No angle has a measure here, and none is needed: what the stem gives is which angle matches which.

Step 2. Angle AAA matches angle DDD and angle CCC matches angle FFF, which is two matching pairs, so the triangles are similar. The third pair follows on its own, because the three angles of each add to 180∘180^{\circ}180∘, so angle BBB matches angle EEE.

Step 3. Sides follow the vertices that matched. ABABAB runs from AAA to BBB and DEDEDE runs from DDD to EEE, so those two are a pair; BCBCBC and EFEFEF pair the same way. Build the factor from the pair that is fully given, then apply it.
