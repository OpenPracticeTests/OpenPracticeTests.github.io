Circles

  • Geometry and Trigonometry
  • Circles
←PreviousAll Math lessons

A circle is fixed by its center and its radius, and every piece of it is a share of the whole. Decide first whether the question is about a piece of a circle, about an angle drawn in it, or about a circle written as an equation.

Key takeaways

  • A central angle's share of a whole turn is the arc's share of the circumference and the sector's share of the area.
  • One whole turn is 360360360 degrees and 2π2\pi2π radians. Match the angle to the turn.
  • A radius meets a tangent at a right angle, and an inscribed angle is half the central angle on the same arc.
  • Each bracket of (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2(x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2 holds a difference, so the center is (h,k)(h, k)(h,k) and each printed sign is the opposite.
  • The right side of that equation is r2r^2r2, never rrr.
  • A point is on the circle when it makes the two sides of the equation equal.
  • For x2+y2+Dx+Ey=Fx^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey = Fx2+y2+Dx+Ey=F, complete the square in xxx and in yyy first.

What the SAT may ask

  • Move between a central angle and the arc or sector it cuts, either way round.
  • Restate an angle in degrees as radians, or the other way round.
  • Use a tangent's right angle, or an inscribed angle, to find a missing measure.
  • Read a circle's center or its radius from its equation.
  • Write a circle's equation from a center and a radius, a center and a point on it, or the two ends of a diameter.
  • Complete the square to put a circle's equation in standard form.

The rule

A central angle's share of a whole turn is the same share of the circle. The arc it cuts is that share of the circumference, and the sector it cuts is that share of the area.

share=central anglewhole turn\text{share} = \frac{\text{central angle}}{\text{whole turn}}share=whole turncentral angle​

One whole turn is 360360360 degrees, and it is also 2π2\pi2π radians. Put a degree angle over 360360360 and a radian angle over 2π2\pi2π. A central angle of 404040 degrees is 19\frac{1}{9}91​ of a turn. Its arc is one ninth of the circumference, and its sector is one ninth of the area.

The reference sheet our practice tests carry hands you A=πr2A = \pi r^2A=πr2, C=2πrC = 2\pi rC=2πr, and both of those measures of a whole turn. It hands you no arc length, no sector area, and no equation of a circle.

Two formulas do the share in one step, and both need θ\thetaθ in radians. An arc is s=rθs = r\thetas=rθ and a sector is A=12r2θA = \frac{1}{2}r^2\thetaA=21​r2θ. In degrees, put θ360\frac{\theta}{360}360θ​ in front of 2πr2\pi r2πr or of πr2\pi r^2πr2 instead.

Three angle facts about a circle. A tangent touches a circle at one point, and the radius drawn to that point meets it at a right angle.

An inscribed angle has its vertex on the circle, and it is half the central angle standing on the same arc. Measure that central angle through the arc the inscribed angle cuts. An angle inscribed in a semicircle cuts half the circle, so it is a right angle.

A circle in the xyxyxy-plane has a center (h,k)(h, k)(h,k) and a radius rrr, and it is written this way.

(x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2(x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2

Each bracket prints the opposite of the center's coordinate, and the right side holds r2r^2r2. So a circle centered at (−8,3)(-8, 3)(−8,3) with a radius of 111111 is written like this.

(x+8)2+(y−3)2=121(x + 8)^2 + (y - 3)^2 = 121(x+8)2+(y−3)2=121

A point lies on the circle when its coordinates make the two sides equal. Substitute and compare.

When xxx and yyy also appear to the first power, build the two squares yourself. Take half the coefficient of xxx, square it, and add that number to both sides. Do the same with the coefficient of yyy.

The two numbers you halved go inside the brackets, so each one is the opposite of a center coordinate. The two you added join the constant on the right to make r2r^2r2.

Why the rule exists

A circle is every point at one fixed distance from one point, and the equation says exactly that.

The bracket x−hx - hx−h measures how far a point sits from the center across, and y−ky - ky−k measures how far it sits up or down. Square each, add them, and you have the square of the straight-line distance between the point and the center. On a circle that distance is rrr, so the sum is r2r^2r2.

That is why a bracket prints x−hx - hx−h when the center's coordinate is hhh: the bracket holds a difference, not the coordinate. It is also why the right side is r2r^2r2 rather than rrr. The distance was squared to get there and was never un-squared.

The share rule comes from the same place. A central angle opens the same fraction of the turn however far out from the center you look. That is why it takes the same fraction of the boundary and of the inside.

Worked examples

1. Find an arc's length from a diameter and a degree angle

A circle has a diameter of 404040 centimeters. A central angle of 363636 degrees intercepts an arc of the circle. What is the length of that arc, in centimeters?

Question asks: a length along the circle's edge.

First move: the formula wants a radius and the question states a diameter, so halve it first.

r=20r = 20r=20

Now the whole way around, then the angle's share of a turn.

C=2π(20)C = 2\pi (20)C=2π(20) C=40πC = 40\piC=40π 36360=110\frac{36}{360} = \frac{1}{10}36036​=101​

The arc is that share of the circumference.

110(40π)=4π\frac{1}{10}(40\pi) = 4\pi101​(40π)=4π

Answer: 4π4\pi4π centimeters

Check: a share of a turn was taken, so match the angle to it. The 363636 arrived in degrees and sits over 360360360, which is a whole turn in degrees.

Common mistake: using the stated 404040 as the radius. That doubles the circumference and gives an arc of 8π8\pi8π.

2. Write a circle's equation from its center and one point on it

A circle in the xyxyxy-plane has its center at (4,−3)(4, -3)(4,−3) and passes through the point (9,1)(9, 1)(9,1). Write an equation for the circle.

Question asks: the whole equation, not a radius.

First move: put the center into the two brackets, each coordinate with its sign flipped, and leave the right side unknown.

(x−4)2+(y+3)2=r2(x - 4)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = r^2(x−4)2+(y+3)2=r2

The stated point is on the circle, so its coordinates make the two sides equal. Substitute them.

(9−4)2+(1+3)2=r2(9 - 4)^2 + (1 + 3)^2 = r^2(9−4)2+(1+3)2=r2 25+16=r225 + 16 = r^225+16=r2 r2=41r^2 = 41r2=41

Answer: (x−4)2+(y+3)2=41(x - 4)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 41(x−4)2+(y+3)2=41

Check: step 1 asked for an equation, and this is one. No share of a turn was taken.

Common mistake: taking a square root at the end and writing 41\sqrt{41}41​ on the right. The equation holds the square of the radius, and 414141 is already it.

3. Complete the square in both variables to find the center

In the xyxyxy-plane, the equation x2+y2−18x+14y=95x^2 + y^2 - 18x + 14y = 95x2+y2−18x+14y=95 defines a circle. What is the center of the circle?

Question asks: the center, as a pair of coordinates.

First move: this is not standard form, so build the two squares. Group the xxx terms and the yyy terms.

x2−18x+y2+14y=95x^2 - 18x + y^2 + 14y = 95x2−18x+y2+14y=95

Half of −18-18−18 is −9-9−9, and −9-9−9 squared is 818181. Half of 141414 is 777, and 777 squared is 494949. Add both numbers to each side.

(x2−18x+81)+(y2+14y+49)=95+81+49(x^2 - 18x + 81) + (y^2 + 14y + 49) = 95 + 81 + 49(x2−18x+81)+(y2+14y+49)=95+81+49 (x−9)2+(y+7)2=225(x - 9)^2 + (y + 7)^2 = 225(x−9)2+(y+7)2=225

Each bracket prints the opposite of a center coordinate.

Answer: (9,−7)(9, -7)(9,−7)

Check: step 1 asked for a center, and this is one. The completed form hands over the radius too, since 225225225 is the square of the radius and its square root is 151515.

Common mistake: reading the two brackets without flipping their signs, which gives (−9,7)(-9, 7)(−9,7).

Special cases

Circles the rule does not finish

A circle centered at the origin. Both hhh and kkk are 000, so the equation reads x2+y2=r2x^2 + y^2 = r^2x2+y2=r2. Nothing is missing. The two subtractions are there and subtract nothing.

The unit circle is that circle with r=1r = 1r=1, so it is x2+y2=1x^2 + y^2 = 1x2+y2=1. Angles on it are measured from the positive xxx-axis. A positive angle turns counterclockwise and a negative one turns clockwise.

The point where an angle's ray crosses the circle is (cos⁡θ,sin⁡θ)(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)(cosθ,sinθ). So cos⁡θ\cos\thetacosθ carries the sign of xxx in the quadrant the ray lands in, and sin⁡θ\sin\thetasinθ the sign of yyy. Adding a whole turn to θ\thetaθ lands on the same point.

Squared terms with a coefficient. Divide every term by that coefficient first, so each square starts with a leading 111. Do not use the circle rule when the two squared coefficients differ, or when an xyxyxy term appears.

Equal coefficients alone do not promise a circle. Build both squares, then read the right side. A positive number is r2r^2r2 and the graph is a circle. Zero leaves one point, the center. A negative number leaves nothing to draw, since no sum of two squares is negative.

A parabola is completed differently. It has one square, and the expression has to keep its value, so the number is added and subtracted in the same place. It stays there, as part of the vertex.

Common traps

Reading the equation as though it printed what you want

Standard form prints the opposite of each center coordinate, and it prints r2r^2r2 rather than rrr. Read what a number measures before it goes anywhere.

Trap Quick check
Taking a bracket's number for a center coordinate Flip its sign
Taking the right side for the radius It holds r2r^2r2; take the square root
Writing a stated radius on the right side Square it first
Pairing a degree angle with 2π2\pi2π, or a radian angle with 360360360 A turn is 360360360 degrees or 2π2\pi2π radians, not both at once
Deciding the unit from whether the answer shows π\piπ The unit comes from the question and the turn you divided by. An angle of 111 radian shows no π\piπ at all
Answering with the whole circle when a sector or an arc was named The answer is a share of the whole

The method: Ask, sort, set up, check

  1. Name the ask and the unit it comes back in. An angle in degrees or in radians, a length, an area, a center, or a whole equation. Write that down before anything else.
  2. Sort the question into one of three families. An arc, a sector, or a bare angle runs on the share of a whole turn. A tangent or an inscribed angle runs on the angle facts. A circle in the xyxyxy-plane runs on the equation. One question can need two, and the equation goes first.
  3. Set the relation up in the form that shows what you want, then put the numbers in. For a share, write the part over the whole. For an equation that is not in standard form, complete the square in xxx and in yyy first. Then read the center out of the brackets and r2r^2r2 off the right side.
  4. Answer the ask, and match any angle to its turn. When a share of a turn was taken, check that a degree angle sat over 360360360 and a radian angle over 2π2\pi2π. Skip that check when no share was taken, and name the ask instead.

Square roots and squares, not the setup

A graphing calculator is built into the testing app and is allowed on every Math question.

On this topic the calculator saves time on one thing: a square root, or the square of a two-digit number.

Get the center and r2r^2r2 onto paper first. Graphing a finished equation is a fair check on a center you have already worked out, but the reading is what the algebra is for.

An angle measures 3π4\frac{3\pi}{4}43π​ radians. What is the measure of this angle, in degrees?

Correct. The angle is 38\frac{3}{8}83​ of a whole turn, and 38\frac{3}{8}83​ of 360360360 degrees is 135135135.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is an angle in degrees. The angle arrived in radians, so it has to be converted before anything is reported.

Step 2. There is no arc, no sector and no equation here. The angle stands on its own, and an angle on its own is a share of a whole turn.

Step 3. Write the angle over a whole turn in radians, then take that share of a whole turn in degrees.

3π4÷2π=38\frac{3\pi}{4} \div 2\pi = \frac{3}{8}43π​÷2π=83​ 38(360)=135\frac{3}{8}(360) = 13583​(360)=135

Step 4. A share of a turn was taken, so match the angle to it. The angle arrived in radians and sat over 2π2\pi2π; the answer is in degrees and came from 360360360. The angle measures 135135135 degrees.

A circle in the xyxyxy-plane has its center at (−10,6)(-10, 6)(−10,6) and a radius of 333. Which equation represents the circle?

Correct. Each center coordinate enters its bracket with the sign flipped and the radius is squared, which gives (x+10)2+(y−6)2=9(x + 10)^2 + (y - 6)^2 = 9(x+10)2+(y−6)2=9.

The method, applied

Step 1. The ask is a whole equation, so the answer is a form rather than a number.

Step 2. The circle sits in the xyxyxy-plane and no angle is mentioned, so this runs on the equation.

Step 3. Standard form already shows a center and a radius, so nothing has to be built. Put each coordinate in with its sign flipped, then square the radius.

(x−(−10))2+(y−6)2=32(x - (-10))^2 + (y - 6)^2 = 3^2(x−(−10))2+(y−6)2=32 (x+10)2+(y−6)2=9(x + 10)^2 + (y - 6)^2 = 9(x+10)2+(y−6)2=9

Step 4. Step 1 asked for an equation, and the circle is (x+10)2+(y−6)2=9(x + 10)^2 + (y - 6)^2 = 9(x+10)2+(y−6)2=9.
