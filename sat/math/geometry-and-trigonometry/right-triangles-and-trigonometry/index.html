Right Triangles and Trigonometry

  • Geometry and Trigonometry
  • Right triangles and trigonometry
←PreviousAll Math lessonsNext→

A right triangle has one angle of 909090 degrees, and the theorem and the three ratios on this page all need it. Decide first what the question hands you: two sides, a special angle set, an angle, or a ratio.

Key takeaways

  • In a right triangle, a2+b2=c2a^2 + b^2 = c^2a2+b2=c2, where ccc is the side opposite the right angle.
  • The hypotenuse sits opposite the right angle and is the longest of the three sides.
  • A 303030-606060-909090 triangle has sides xxx, x3x\sqrt{3}x3​, 2x2x2x, each opposite its own angle.
  • A 454545-454545-909090 triangle has sides sss, sss, s2s\sqrt{2}s2​, the hypotenuse last.
  • Sine is opposite over hypotenuse, cosine adjacent over hypotenuse, tangent opposite over adjacent.
  • Which leg is opposite and which is adjacent depends on which acute angle you read from.
  • The two acute angles add to 909090 degrees, and each one's sine is the other's cosine.

What the SAT may ask

  • Find a missing side from the other two sides.
  • Find a side of a 303030-606060-909090 or 454545-454545-909090 triangle from one given side.
  • Write the sine, cosine, or tangent of a named angle from the side lengths.
  • Turn one stated ratio into another, on the same angle or on the other acute angle.
  • Find a side from one angle and one side, using sine, cosine, or tangent.
  • Build a right triangle out of a rectangle, a diagonal, or two triangles that share a side.

The rule

The side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse, and it is the longest of the three. The other two sides are the legs.

The Pythagorean theorem: a2+b2=c2a^2 + b^2 = c^2a2+b2=c2, where aaa and bbb are the legs and ccc is the hypotenuse. It holds in a right triangle and in no other triangle, so establish the right angle before you use it.

A list of common formulas is available throughout the Math section. The sheet our practice tests carry gives you a2+b2=c2a^2 + b^2 = c^2a2+b2=c2 and both special right triangles below. It defines no sine, cosine, or tangent, so learn those three.

Two angle sets fix the ratios between all three sides.

Sine, cosine, and tangent are ratios of two sides, read from one acute angle.

Opposite and adjacent are named relative to the acute angle the question gives. In the figure the leg of 888 is opposite xxx and adjacent to yyy. So the sine of xxx and the cosine of yyy are the same ratio.

sin⁡x=817\sin x = \frac{8}{17}sinx=178​

For an acute angle, sine and cosine both come out between 000 and 111. Each puts a leg over the hypotenuse, and the hypotenuse is the longest side. Tangent puts one leg over the other, so it can be more than 111.

The two acute angles add to 909090 degrees, so the sine of either one is the cosine of the other. Both angles here are measured in degrees, which is what the 909090 counts.

sin⁡x=cos⁡(90∘−x)\sin x = \cos(90^\circ - x)sinx=cos(90∘−x)

Why the rule exists

Fix one acute angle of a right triangle and the whole shape is fixed. Scale that shape up or down and every ratio between two of its sides stays the same, so one number can stand for the whole family. That is what sine, cosine, and tangent record.

The two special triangles are that idea worked out once. Cut a square along its diagonal and you get a 454545-454545-909090 triangle whose two legs are equal.

c2=s2+s2c^2 = s^2 + s^2c2=s2+s2 c2=2s2c^2 = 2s^2c2=2s2 c=s2c = s\sqrt{2}c=s2​

Cut an equilateral triangle down the middle and you get a 303030-606060-909090 triangle. The cut halves one side, so the short leg is half the hypotenuse, which is why the hypotenuse is 2x2x2x.

The theorem itself needs the right angle. Hold the two legs at fixed lengths and open the angle between them: the third side gets longer. Close it and the third side gets shorter. At exactly one opening the three sides satisfy a2+b2=c2a^2 + b^2 = c^2a2+b2=c2, and that opening is the right angle.

Worked examples

1. Recover a leg from the hypotenuse and the other leg

In right triangle KLMKLMKLM, angle MMM is a right angle. The hypotenuse KLKLKL has length 292929 and the leg KMKMKM has length 202020. What is the length of LMLMLM?

Question asks: the second leg, not the hypotenuse.

First move: the right angle is at MMM, so KLKLKL sits opposite it and is the hypotenuse. The two legs are KMKMKM and LMLMLM, and one of them is the unknown.

202+LM2=29220^2 + LM^2 = 29^2202+LM2=292 LM2=841−400LM^2 = 841 - 400LM2=841−400 LM2=441LM^2 = 441LM2=441 LM=21LM = 21LM=21

Answer: 212121

Check: a length was produced, so the hypotenuse has to be the longest side. Both 212121 and 202020 come in under 292929.

Common mistake: subtracting the lengths rather than their squares. That gives 29−20=929 - 20 = 929−20=9, and the theorem is written about squares.

2. Run a 30-60-90 triangle backward from its long leg

In right triangle PQRPQRPQR, angle RRR is a right angle and angle PPP measures 303030 degrees. The side PRPRPR has length 939\sqrt{3}93​. What is the length of PQPQPQ?

Question asks: the hypotenuse.

First move: the third angle is 606060 degrees, so the sides run xxx, x3x\sqrt{3}x3​, 2x2x2x. Match the given side to its own angle: PRPRPR sits opposite QQQ, the 606060 degree angle, so it is the x3x\sqrt{3}x3​ side.

x3=93x\sqrt{3} = 9\sqrt{3}x3​=93​ x=9x = 9x=9

The hypotenuse sits opposite the right angle, so it is the 2x2x2x side.

PQ=18PQ = 18PQ=18

Answer: 181818

Check: a length was produced. 939\sqrt{3}93​ is about 15.615.615.6, so 181818 is the longest of the three sides.

Common mistake: doubling the side you were handed. That treats 939\sqrt{3}93​ as the short leg and gives 18318\sqrt{3}183​, which is about 31.231.231.2 and far too long.

3. Find a height from an angle of elevation

A flagpole stands at a right angle to level ground. From a point on the ground 202020 meters from the foot of the pole, the angle of elevation to the top of the pole measures 353535 degrees. How tall is the pole, to the nearest tenth of a meter?

Question asks: the height of the pole, in meters.

First move: the pole meets the ground at a right angle, so the pole, the ground and the line of sight make a right triangle. Read the sides relative to the 353535 degree angle: the height is opposite it and the 202020 is adjacent to it.

That is the pair tangent uses.

tan⁡35∘=h20\tan 35^\circ = \frac{h}{20}tan35∘=20h​ h=20tan⁡35∘h = 20\tan 35^\circh=20tan35∘

Set the calculator to degrees before you evaluate it.

h=14.0h = 14.0h=14.0

Answer: about 14.014.014.0 meters

Check: a length was produced. The third angle is 555555 degrees, and a larger acute angle faces a longer side, so the side opposite the 353535 has to be the shorter leg. 14.014.014.0 comes in under 202020.

Common mistake: leaving the calculator in radians. The same keystrokes then return about 0.470.470.47 for the tangent and a pole about 9.59.59.5 meters tall.

Special cases

Establishing the right angle, and angles outside a right triangle

Only those two angle sets are worth memorizing. Every acute angle fixes its own side ratios. For any other angle the ratio comes from somewhere else: stated in the question, built from two sides, or read off a calculator in degree mode.

Establish the right angle before you use the theorem to find a side, or use any of the three ratios. A stem can state it and a figure can mark it. Two perpendicular lines give it, so does the corner of a rectangle, and a line you draw yourself can create one.

The theorem establishes it too. If three side lengths satisfy a2+b2=c2a^2 + b^2 = c^2a2+b2=c2, the angle opposite the longest side is a right angle. So a question can hand you a right triangle without ever saying the words.

Sine, cosine and tangent are defined here as ratios of sides, which needs an acute angle in a right triangle. All three reach past that setting. Sine and cosine are defined on the unit circle, which the circles page carries along with angles measured in radians, and tangent is sine over cosine. Every angle here is in degrees.

Common traps

Naming opposite and adjacent from the wrong angle

Opposite and adjacent are not fixed sides. They are named relative to the acute angle the question gives. The hypotenuse is the same side whichever acute angle you read from.

Trap Quick check
Reading the longest side of any triangle as a hypotenuse Only a right triangle has one
Using the leg opposite the other acute angle Read the sides relative to the angle the question names
Multiplying by 2\sqrt{2}2​ or 3\sqrt{3}3​ where you have to divide A leg cannot come out longer than the hypotenuse
Matching a given side to the wrong angle of a 303030-606060-909090 Each side sits opposite its own angle
Stopping at c2c^2c2 The theorem gives the square, so take the root
Reporting a sine or a cosine larger than 111 For an acute angle both stay under 111; only tangent can pass it

The method: Mark, sort, relate, answer

  1. Mark the right angle and name the ask. The side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse and the other two are legs. Write down whether the question wants a length or a ratio.
  2. Sort what you are given. Two sides means the Pythagorean theorem. An angle set of 303030, 606060, 909090 or 454545, 454545, 909090 means the fixed side ratios. Any other angle, with a side or with a stated ratio, means sine, cosine, or tangent.

  3. Write the relation your numbers and the unknown share, and chain a second where one does not reach. For the theorem, put the two known sides in and keep ccc on the hypotenuse.

    For a special triangle, match the given side to its angle before you scale. For a ratio, read the sides relative to the named angle: the far leg is opposite and the touching leg adjacent. Two triangles usually need the shared side first.

  4. Answer the ask, then check what you produced. When a side length was produced, check it against what you know: the hypotenuse is longest, and a larger acute angle faces a longer side.

    When the answer is the sine or cosine of an acute angle, it has to come out under 111. A tangent has no bound like that, so neither check reaches one.

The root, the trig value, and degree mode

A graphing calculator is built into the testing app and is allowed on every Math question.

Two things here want a calculator: a square root, and the sine, cosine, or tangent of an angle that is not 303030, 454545, or 606060.

Set it to degrees before you evaluate a trig value. Every angle on this page is in degrees, and the same keystrokes in radian mode return a different number with no warning.

Write the relation and match each side to its letter first. Typing a sum of squares before that is done is how a hypotenuse goes in where a leg belongs.

In right triangle JKLJKLJKL, angle LLL is a right angle. The side JLJLJL measures 242424, the side KLKLKL measures 777, and the side JKJKJK measures 252525. What is the cosine of angle JJJ?

Correct. Cosine is the adjacent leg over the hypotenuse, and from angle JJJ that is 2425\frac{24}{25}2524​.

The method, applied

Step 1. The right angle is at LLL, so JKJKJK sits opposite it and is the hypotenuse. The ask is a ratio.

Step 2. All three sides are given and the question names an angle, so this is one of the three ratios.

Step 3. Read the sides relative to JJJ. The leg touching JJJ measures 242424 and the hypotenuse measures 252525. Cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse.

cos⁡J=2425\cos J = \frac{24}{25}cosJ=2524​

Step 4. The answer is the cosine of an acute angle, so it has to come out under 111, and 2425\frac{24}{25}2524​ does. The cosine of angle JJJ is 2425\frac{24}{25}2524​.

In right triangle DEFDEFDEF, angle FFF is a right angle and angle DDD measures 303030 degrees. The hypotenuse DEDEDE has length 161616. What is the length of DFDFDF?

Correct. Halving the hypotenuse gives x=8x = 8x=8, and the side opposite the 606060 degree angle is x3x\sqrt{3}x3​, so DFDFDF is 838\sqrt{3}83​.

The method, applied

Step 1. The right angle is at FFF, so DEDEDE is the hypotenuse. The ask is a length.

Step 2. Two angles are given and the third is 606060 degrees, so the sides run xxx, x3x\sqrt{3}x3​, 2x2x2x.

Step 3. Match the given side to its angle. The hypotenuse sits opposite the right angle, so it is the 2x2x2x side.

2x=162x = 162x=16 x=8x = 8x=8

The side DFDFDF sits opposite the 606060 degree angle, so it is the x3x\sqrt{3}x3​ side.

DF=83DF = 8\sqrt{3}DF=83​

In right triangle RSTRSTRST, angle TTT is a right angle, and the sine of angle RRR is 1213\frac{12}{13}1312​. What is the cosine of angle SSS?

Correct. The two acute angles add to 909090 degrees, so the cosine of one is the sine of the other, which makes the cosine of SSS equal to 1213\frac{12}{13}1312​.

The method, applied

Step 1. The right angle is at TTT, so RRR and SSS are the two acute angles. The ask is a ratio.

Step 2. A ratio is given and a ratio is wanted, and both are read on the same triangle, so no side length has to be found.

Step 3. Read the sides relative to SSS. The leg opposite RRR is the leg touching SSS, and the hypotenuse is the same side either way, so the sine of RRR and the cosine of SSS are built from the same two sides.

cos⁡S=sin⁡R\cos S = \sin RcosS=sinR cos⁡S=1213\cos S = \frac{12}{13}cosS=1312​

Step 4. The answer is the cosine of an acute angle, so it has to come out under 111, and 1213\frac{12}{13}1312​ does. The cosine of angle SSS is 1213\frac{12}{13}1312​.

In right triangle XYZXYZXYZ, angle ZZZ is a right angle. Which expression is equal to the tangent of angle XXX?

Correct. Tangent is the opposite leg over the adjacent leg, and from angle XXX that is YZXZ\frac{YZ}{XZ}XZYZ​.

The method, applied

Step 1. The right angle is at ZZZ, so XYXYXY sits opposite it and is the hypotenuse. The other two sides, XZXZXZ and YZYZYZ, are the legs. The ask is a ratio.

Step 2. No numbers are given, so the answer is the ratio written in letters. Nothing has to be computed.

Step 3. Read the sides relative to XXX. The leg across the triangle from XXX is YZYZYZ, and the leg touching XXX is XZXZXZ. Tangent is opposite over adjacent.

tan⁡X=YZXZ\tan X = \frac{YZ}{XZ}tanX=XZYZ​

Step 4. No length was measured and the answer is a tangent, which has no bound like a sine or a cosine, so neither check reaches this one. The tangent of angle XXX is YZXZ\frac{YZ}{XZ}XZYZ​.

Correct. The hypotenuse is the s2s\sqrt{2}s2​ side, so dividing 222222 by 2\sqrt{2}2​ leaves a leg of 11211\sqrt{2}112​.

Step 1. The right angle is at JJJ, so GHGHGH is the hypotenuse. The ask is a leg.

Step 2. The angle set is 454545, 454545, 909090, so the sides run sss, sss, s2s\sqrt{2}s2​.

Step 3. Match the given side to its own angle. The hypotenuse is the s2s\sqrt{2}s2​ side, so the root has to be divided out.

s2=22s\sqrt{2} = 22s2​=22 s=222s = \frac{22}{\sqrt{2}}s=2​22​ s=112s = 11\sqrt{2}s=112​

Step 4. A length was produced, so check it against the hypotenuse. 11211\sqrt{2}112​ is about 15.615.615.6, under 222222. The length of GJGJGJ is 11211\sqrt{2}112​.

In the figure, points PPP, RRR, and QQQ lie on one line, and segment RSRSRS meets that line at a right angle. The length of PRPRPR is 121212, the length of PSPSPS is 373737, and angle SQRSQRSQR measures 454545 degrees. What is the length of PQPQPQ?

Correct. The shared leg RSRSRS comes out at 353535, the 454545 degree angle makes RQRQRQ equal to it, and 121212 added to 353535 gives 474747.

The method, applied

Step 1. Segment RSRSRS meets the line at a right angle, so there are two right triangles, PRSPRSPRS and SRQSRQSRQ, and they share the leg RSRSRS. The ask is a length.

Step 2. Triangle PRSPRSPRS hands over two sides, so the theorem gives its third. Triangle SRQSRQSRQ hands over an angle set, so its side ratios are fixed.

Step 3. In triangle PRSPRSPRS the right angle is at RRR, so PSPSPS is the hypotenuse.

122+RS2=37212^2 + RS^2 = 37^2122+RS2=372 RS2=1369−144RS^2 = 1369 - 144RS2=1369−144 RS2=1225RS^2 = 1225RS2=1225 RS=35RS = 35RS=35

Triangle SRQSRQSRQ has a right angle at RRR and a 454545 degree angle at QQQ, so it is a 454545-454545-909090 triangle, and the two legs of one of those are equal.

RQ=35RQ = 35RQ=35 PQ=12+35PQ = 12 + 35PQ=12+35 PQ=47PQ = 47PQ=47

Step 4. A length was produced, so check it against a hypotenuse. In triangle PRSPRSPRS, the side PSPSPS measures 373737 and is longer than both 121212 and 353535. The length of PQPQPQ is 474747.

A rectangular plot of ground measures 999 meters by 404040 meters. A straight path runs from one corner of the plot to the opposite corner. Walking that path is shorter than walking along two sides of the plot. By how many meters is it shorter?

Correct. The path is the diagonal, 414141 meters, and the two sides come to 494949 meters, so the path saves 888 meters.

The method, applied

Step 1. The corners of a rectangle are right angles, so two sides and the path make a right triangle. The ask is a difference between two distances.

Step 2. The two legs are known, so the theorem gives the path.

Step 3. The path runs opposite the right angle, so it is the hypotenuse.

92+402=c29^2 + 40^2 = c^292+402=c2 c2=81+1600c^2 = 81 + 1600c2=81+1600 c2=1681c^2 = 1681c2=1681 c=41c = 41c=41

The walk along two sides covers 999 meters and then 404040 meters.

49−41=849 - 41 = 849−41=8

Step 4. A length was produced, so check it against the sides that made it. The path measures 414141, longer than both 404040 and 999. The path is shorter by 888 meters.

cos⁡M=4553\cos M = \frac{45}{53}cosM=5345​
