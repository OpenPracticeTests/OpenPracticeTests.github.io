Algebra to trigonometry

Math

Everything the Math section tests, one page per topic. Each page teaches one rule or method, works it through on original examples, names the trap the test wraps it in, and drills it with practice items tagged by difficulty tier, every wrong answer being the number one specific slip produces.

22 of 22 topics are written. They are organized by the section's four content domains, in blueprint order. The math questions inside the practice tests are all written too, and every one carries a worked explanation you can read in review.

Algebra

About 35% of the section.

Linear Equations in One Variable

Name what the question asks for before you touch the equation, then undo the operations in reverse order.

Linear Equations in Two Variables

Slope, intercepts, and the forms of a line, and how to move between an equation, a graph, and two points.

Linear Functions

Read a rate of change and a starting value out of a table, a graph, or a sentence, and say what each means in context.

Systems of Two Linear Equations

Substitution, elimination, and the coefficient test that tells you when a system has no solution or infinitely many.

Linear Inequalities

Solve and graph inequalities in one or two variables, flip the sign when you must, and read a boundary that is not part of the answer.

Advanced Math

About 35% of the section.

Equivalent Expressions

Factor, expand, and rewrite with exponent rules until two expressions are visibly the same one.

Quadratic Equations

Factoring, the quadratic formula, and the discriminant, plus the line-and-parabola system that reduces to a quadratic.

Radical, Rational, and Absolute Value Equations

Isolate, undo, and then check: the equations where a solution can appear that the original never allowed.

Quadratic Functions and Parabolas

Vertex, intercepts, and the form that shows each one, so a graph question becomes a rewriting question.

Exponential Functions

Growth and decay by a constant factor, the percent hidden in that factor, and how to tell exponential from linear in a table.

Polynomials and Function Notation

Zeros, factors, and remainders, composed functions, transformations, and reading a function off its graph or table.

Problem-Solving and Data Analysis

About 15% of the section.

Ratios, Rates, and Units

Set up the proportion, keep the units straight, and convert once rather than twice.

Percentages

Percent of, percent change, and the reverse problem: what the original was before the change.

One-Variable Data

Mean, median, mode, range, and standard deviation, and how each one moves when the data does.

Two-Variable Data and Scatterplots

Lines of best fit, what their slope and intercept mean, and the gap between a data point and the model's prediction.

Probability

Probabilities from a two-way table, conditional probability, and the denominator that changes when a condition is added.

Margin of Error and Inference

What a sample statistic lets you say about a population, and what a margin of error does and does not mean.

Evaluating Statistical Claims

Random selection against random assignment, and which conclusions each study design can and cannot support.

Geometry and Trigonometry

About 15% of the section.

Area and Volume

Composite figures, the formulas on the reference sheet, and what happens to area and volume when a dimension scales.

Lines, Angles, and Triangles

Parallel lines, angle sums, similar triangles, and the proportions that similarity hands you.

Right Triangles and Trigonometry

The Pythagorean theorem, special right triangles, and sine, cosine, and tangent as ratios of sides.

Circles

Arc length, sector area, the equation of a circle, and completing the square to find its center and radius.
