Test day

Test day checklist

You take the SAT on a laptop or tablet, in an app called Bluebook. Usually it is your own device. The device, the app, your admission ticket and your photo ID all have to be ready before you leave the house, and the doors close fifteen minutes after check-in opens.

This covers the standard weekend SAT. If you are taking the SAT on a school day, your school may give you the device, and gives you a sign-in ticket for Bluebook instead of your own account, so follow your school's instructions rather than this page.

These are College Board's rules, taken from several of their pages, one per subject below. For the morning itself, the room and the break, that page is What to Expect on Test Day, which we read on 2026-09-02. Where we are suggesting something rather than reporting a rule, it says so.

Rules about devices, times, and what you may carry change between test dates, and this page cannot change with them. So when two things disagree, go in this order:

  • What the staff at your test centre tell you on the day.
  • Your admission ticket and your Bluebook homepage.
  • College Board's current pages, starting at What to Expect on Test Day.
  • This page.

Get the device ready early

Bluebook is an app you install, not a website you open on the morning. Start at least 30 days before your test date.

  • Check that your device is one Bluebook runs on. It runs on Windows devices, Macs, iPads, and school-managed Chromebooks. It does not run on a personal Chromebook or on a phone.
  • Install Bluebook, open it, and run Test Your Device. That is the check that tells you what is wrong while there is still time to fix it.
  • Sign in with your College Board account and read your homepage.
  • Take a practice test in Bluebook.
  • Check the battery holds a charge for three to four hours.
  • Free up space: 1 GB on a Windows device, Mac, or Chromebook, and 250 MB on an iPad.

Practise on the device you are going to bring. A keyboard you do not know, a screen size you do not know, and a battery you have never run down are three problems you do not need on the morning.

Bluebook needs a minimum operating system version, and that version moves. Run Test Your Device rather than reading a version number off a page. It tells you about your device, on the day you ask it.

If you have no device and cannot borrow one, College Board will lend you one. The request closes 30 days before your test day.

Get the settings right before you test

College Board's rule is that Bluebook must be the only application running during the test. On a personal device, some settings have to be off and some have to be on.

Off:

  • Parental controls and screen time limits.
  • Content filters.
  • Any VPN.
  • Startup apps. College Board names writing assistants such as Grammarly, and messaging apps.
  • Guided Access, if you are testing on an iPad.

On:

  • Let Bluebook update itself.
  • Set your keyboard layout to English (U.S.).
  • Set the device to get its date and time automatically.

We suggest closing the programs in that first list rather than minimising them.

Exam setup, and your admission ticket

Registering for the test and setting the test up are two different steps. Exam setup happens inside Bluebook, and completing it is how you get your admission ticket.

College Board's instruction is to complete exam setup five days before test day. The one exception is a borrowed device, which you collect at the centre on the morning and set up there.

  • Open Bluebook, sign in, and complete exam setup.
  • Check that your name and details match your photo ID exactly.
  • Generate your admission ticket.
  • Print it. College Board says a printed ticket is preferred, and we suggest one too: your phone may be collected before you get to show anything on it.
  • Learn your College Board username and password by heart. You type them at the test centre.

Check the device again on test-day morning

Do this before you leave the house.

  • Connect to the internet.
  • Close Bluebook and open it again, so it can update.
  • Run Test Your Device.
  • Sign in.
  • Read your homepage for updates and messages about your test site.
  • Check the required settings again. College Board's own morning list names parental controls and content filters here.
  • Charge the battery to full.

What to bring

College Board splits its list in two. The first half is what you need in order to be allowed to test. We have moved one item: their list has a calculator here, and their calculator policy says a handheld is not required, so it sits below instead.

  • Your testing device, fully charged, with Bluebook installed and exam setup done.
  • Your up-to-date admission ticket from Bluebook.
  • An acceptable photo ID, a physical one.
  • Pencils or pens for scratch work.
  • Your College Board username and password.

The second half is allowed and worth having:

  • A charging cable or a portable charger. College Board says outlet availability is not guaranteed.
  • A watch with no audible alarm.
  • A bag or backpack.
  • A drink and a snack for the break.
  • A backup testing device.
  • A calculator you know, if you use one.

Staff collect the scratch paper at the end. Do not bring your own paper, notes, or a formula sheet: those are on the prohibited list.

An epinephrine auto-injector is allowed without an accommodation, in a clear bag under your desk.

We suggest packing all of it the night before and leaving it by the door.

Your photo ID

Your admission ticket on its own will not get you in. You need a physical photo ID as well, and it has to meet all of these:

  • Valid and not expired.
  • Issued by a government, or by the school you currently attend.
  • An original physical document. A photocopy is refused, and so is a digital ID on a phone.
  • Your full legal name, spelled the way it is spelled on your admission ticket.
  • A recent photo that looks like you on the day and matches the photo you gave College Board.
  • In good condition, with text that can be read.

A credit or debit card, a birth certificate, a Social Security card, and any temporary ID are not accepted. From age 21 a school ID no longer counts, and you need a government-issued one.

We suggest holding the ticket and the ID side by side this week and reading the two names against each other. A middle name on one and not the other is the kind of thing you want to find now.

The calculator

Bluebook has a Desmos calculator built in, so a handheld is optional. College Board recommends bringing a familiar approved model if you use one.

A calculator is not allowed if it does algebra for you. That rules out CAS models, and any calculator with an app, menu, or program that factors or solves algebraically. Also not allowed: a QWERTY keyboard, wireless connectivity, a camera, or paper tape. A tablet, a laptop, or a phone app does not count as a calculator.

In the room:

  • The cover stays under your desk.
  • You cannot share a calculator.
  • You cannot use a handheld during Reading and Writing. It goes under the desk for those two modules.

If you do not already have a calculator you are quick with, we suggest not buying one for test day. Learn Desmos instead. It is on the machine in front of you either way.

What to leave at home

College Board's prohibited list:

  • Phones, smartwatches, fitness trackers, smart glasses, and other wearable technology.
  • Audio players, Bluetooth devices including wireless earbuds, and any other electronic device.
  • Headphones, earbuds, and earplugs.
  • Cameras and recording devices.
  • Separate timers of any type.
  • Detachable privacy screens.
  • An external keyboard for a laptop or Chromebook.
  • A stylus for an iPad.
  • Notes, books, papers, and any other reference material.
  • Compasses, rulers, protractors, and cutting devices.
  • Prohibited calculators.
  • Weapons and firearms.

If a prohibited electronic device makes a noise, or you are seen with it at any time, including during the break, you may be dismissed on the spot, your scores may be cancelled, and the device may be taken.

You also cannot use a phone or any website during the test or the breaks, and College Board names AI tools such as ChatGPT in that rule. You cannot look at notes, talk to other students, copy or record questions, memorize questions to write down later, or share anything about them afterwards. And when you have finished, you wait to be dismissed rather than walking out. The penalties run from a stopped test to cancelled scores to a ban from future College Board tests.

Staff are required to collect phones and other prohibited electronics, or to have you put them in a backpack away from your desk, and that holds through the break. We suggest leaving yours at home if you can. If it comes with you, follow whichever process your centre uses, and do not go back to it until you are dismissed.

Getting there

For a standard weekend test:

  • Check-in is at 7:45 a.m.
  • The doors close at 8 a.m.
  • Testing starts between 8:15 and 8:30 a.m.
  • With standard time you finish around 10:45 to 11 a.m.

Follow your admission ticket if it says anything different. Once testing has started, nobody else is let in.

If College Board is lending you the device, check-in is at 7:15 a.m. You still have to sign in, complete exam setup, and get your admission ticket, and for you that happens at the centre rather than at home.

We suggest leaving earlier than you need to, and checking the address rather than trusting your memory of it.

In the room

Staff check you in and give you a seat. Have your admission ticket and your photo ID out. Then:

  • Connect your device to the test centre's Wi-Fi.
  • Open Bluebook and sign in.
  • Work through the check-in steps in the app.
  • Put away whatever the staff tell you to put away.
  • Enter the start code the proctor gives you.

Do not enter a start code until you are told to. Proctors answer questions about the procedure, not about the test.

Bluebook times each student separately, and start codes are entered at slightly different moments, so students move through the morning at slightly different points. Somebody else breaking or finishing before you tells you nothing about how you are doing.

The break

There is one ten-minute break, between Reading and Writing and Math.

  • Your device stays open on the desk. Do not shut it.
  • Keep your ID with you.
  • Leave your calculator on the desk.
  • Do not touch a phone or any other prohibited device. The device rules apply during the break as well as during the test.

Eat and drink if you brought something, then be back at your desk inside the ten minutes.

The two sections are scored separately

You get two section scores, one for Reading and Writing and one for Math, and they are worked out separately. Each section also picks its own second module from how its own first module went, so nothing that happens in Reading and Writing reaches Math.

When something goes wrong

None of the College Board pages this guide is built from says what happens when a device breaks or a connection drops, so this section is our advice and not a rule. The advice is the same every time: raise your hand and let the staff deal with it.

  • Bluebook will not open, or says another program is running. Show the proctor the message. Do not open other programs and do not turn a required setting back on.
  • You cannot sign in. Raise your hand rather than guessing at your password.
  • The Wi-Fi drops. Keep working if Bluebook lets you, leave the device open, and put your hand up the moment an error shows. Your phone is a prohibited device during the test, so it is not a hotspot you can use.
  • The device freezes, or the battery goes. Raise your hand. Do not restart it yourself, do not swap devices yourself, and do not carry it out of the room.
  • Your calculator stops working. Use Desmos in Bluebook. You cannot borrow another student's.

At the end, your answers submit on their own. If submission does not go through, your work stays saved on the device, and College Board allows it to be submitted by the end of the following day. Keep that device, leave Bluebook installed, and do what the staff and the app tell you.

When you have finished, wait to be dismissed. Walking out of the testing room once you are done, without staff dismissing you, is against the rules. Staff collect the scratch paper and hand back whatever they took at check-in.

Testing with accommodations

Approved accommodations are built into the test itself. Extended time arrives as extended time in the app. Breaks as needed arrive as a test you can pause.

They cannot be added, changed, or waived on test day. If something is missing or wrong, sort it out with College Board's Services for Students with Disabilities before the date.

Some accommodations need your own equipment: a refreshable braille display, screen reader software, or speech-to-text software. Wired headphones are allowed if you are approved for them. Ordinary headphones and earplugs stay prohibited for everyone else.

We suggest doing one full practice run in Bluebook with your accommodation turned on, well before the date, so that the day itself is not the first time you use it.
