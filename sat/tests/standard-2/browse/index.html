Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 1 of 27

A surveyor sketching contour lines in the field cannot wait for the office to check every reading before she draws something on the map. She draws each line from her rough field notes, and marks it ____: good enough to work from today, but certain to be redrawn once the leveling run back at the office confirms or corrects the elevation.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

The text glosses the word itself: the line is "good enough to work from today, but certain to be redrawn," a version adopted until the office confirms a better one. "Provisional" captures exactly that. "Arbitrary" would mean the line has no basis at all, but it is drawn from real field notes. "Permanent" is the opposite of "certain to be redrawn." And "untrustworthy" is ruled out by the same gloss: a line the surveyor can work from today is one she can rely on today. What is still open is the elevation, not whether the line can be used.

Before she throws a bowl, a potter wedges the clay: kneading out air pockets and working the body until it is evenly soft throughout. Only clay in that state is properly plastic. A body wedged too little cracks instead of holding a shape, so she tests it with her thumb before she ever touches the wheel.

As used in the text, what does the word "plastic" most nearly mean?

The passage defines the term through what wedged clay does: it "keeps the new shape rather than springing back," which is what "moldable" means. The second sentence admits that hand scaling is slower than the hammer it replaced, which is a concession. Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 10 of 27

A glacier can dam a valley and back its meltwater into a lake that grows for years behind the ice. The dam "reforms and fails again on no fixed schedule," which means the refill-and-flood cycle repeats rather than happening once. Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 11 of 27

A pollen core pulled from a lake bed at the edge of an old farming valley shows tree pollen rising sharply at one depth, replacing the grass and grain pollen that dominated the layers below. Researchers hypothesize that the shift marks the point when farms in the valley were abandoned and the fields grew back to woodland, rather than the point when farmers simply changed which crop they were managing on the same cleared land.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support the researchers' hypothesis?

The hypothesis is that the fields were left alone, not replanted with something else. In a core the layers above a given depth are the later ones, so scrub pollen appearing there says the fields stopped being worked at the depth where the tree pollen rises. A field kept in production is cleared and tended whatever crop is on it, so scrub never gets a footing there, while a field nobody comes back to fills with scrub first and woodland after. Lake depth is not addressed by either reading of the core. How easily a pollen type is identified is a fact about the laboratory, not about the valley. And a nutrient-rich layer at one depth could have many causes, and it picks neither abandonment nor a change of crop over the other.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 15 of 27

Of the five test pits the survey opened along the ridge, Pit 3 was ____.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

The blank completes "Pit 3 was," so it needs an adjective, and the adjective ranks Pit 3 against four other pits, which is what the superlative "the deepest" does. "The deeper of the five" puts the comparative in a frame that takes exactly two, as in "the deeper of the two." Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 16 of 27

Rarely ____ open once the shed's last tram of the night had been walked over it and locked away.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A fronted "rarely" requires subject-auxiliary inversion, and this clause carries no auxiliary of its own, so "did" must be supplied and placed before the subject: "did the inspection pit stand." Putting the subject first, with or without "did," leaves the clause uninverted. Placing "did stand" together and moving the subject to the end inverts the wrong pair of words, since the auxiliary must come before the subject, not the whole verb phrase.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 17 of 27

Between pressings the racks are scrubbed clean and stacked to dry; the cloths ____ separately, in a tub kept just for them.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A clause that follows a semicolon needs a finite verb of its own, since the semicolon joins two independent clauses. "Are washed" supplies one, in the passive, matching how the racks were treated in the first clause. "Washing" is a present participle and cannot serve as a clause's only verb. "To be washed" is an infinitive, which names an action without performing one. "Washed" alone is a bare past participle, which still needs an auxiliary such as "are" before it can carry the clause.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 18 of 27

The captain always sends for Olsen, ____ held the license longest of anyone on the river since the ferry switched to a winter schedule.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

The blank has to supply a subject for "held," and the stretch of time the "since" phrase opens runs from the ferry's switch up to the present, which "has held" fits. "Who's" contracts "who has" and puts the subject and that auxiliary in place at once. "Whose" is possessive and needs a noun to attach to, not a verb. "Whom" is the object case and cannot serve as the subject of "held." "Who have" gets the auxiliary right but not its number, since a relative pronoun takes the number of the noun it stands for, and "Olsen" is one person.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 19 of 27

Because the seed store refuses every plant the county has listed as invasive ____ running bamboo and giant hogweed, it can promise that nothing it sells will spread past a customer's fence.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

"Such as running bamboo and giant hogweed" adds two examples to a set the sentence has already described, so it is a supplement, and a supplement sitting inside a sentence is fenced with a comma at each end. The comma after "hogweed" is already in place, so the blank supplies the opening one. A semicolon needs an independent clause on its right, and "such as running bamboo and giant hogweed" has neither a subject nor a verb. A colon needs a complete sentence on its left, but the words before the blank open with "Because" and cannot stand alone. And a second comma, dropped in after "such as," cuts the phrase off from the examples it introduces and strands it between two pauses.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 21 of 27

The builder listened to the new pipe speak as if it ____ a voice of its own, rough at the attack and then suddenly clear.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

"As if" introduces a comparison contrary to fact, since a pipe does not literally have a voice, and English marks that with the subjunctive "were" regardless of the subject's number. "Was" is the ordinary indicative past, appropriate for a fact rather than a comparison. "Has" is present tense and does not match the past-tense frame the sentence sets up. "Would have" is a modal construction that implies a real, unfulfilled past condition rather than a present comparison.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 22 of 27

A healthy salt marsh gains height each year as tides bring in a new layer of mud, and for centuries that gain has kept pace with the slow rise of the sea. ____ the sea is now rising faster than most marshes can keep up with, and a marsh that cannot gain height fast enough drowns from beneath, one flood at a time.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

"However" marks the shift from centuries of a marsh keeping pace with the sea to a present in which the sea has outrun it. "For example" would present the second sentence as an instance of the first, which it is not. "Similarly" would claim the two sentences agree, when the second reverses the first. "In other words" would restate the first sentence rather than contradict it.

"Instead" marks a substitution. The obvious repair has just been ruled out, and turning the bell on a lathe is what the founder does in its place. A sentence that emphasizes a similarity must credit both methods with the same fact, and the notes supply exactly one shared fact: both arrive at the same percentage. Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 1 of 27

The compound itself is not new. Chemists have kept it on the shelf since 1962, and nothing about its properties surprises anyone now. What earns Aliyev's paper its place is the route she took to it, which is ____: five steps where the standard preparation takes eleven, and no one before her had run those five in that order. The product is old. The way to it is not.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

The last two sentences separate the two things: the product is old and the way to it is not. A route nobody had run before is novel, in the sense of new rather than the sense of made up. Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 2 of 27

A nurseryman who wants a bushy plant does not wait for one. He takes the leading shoot between finger and thumb and pinches out its tip. That single act will check the shoot, and the buds below it, which the tip had been holding down, break within a week. The plant grows on as fast as it ever did. It simply grows outward instead of up.

As used in the text, what does the word "check" most nearly mean?

The pinched shoot stops extending and the buds below it take over. That is restraint applied to one shoot, not to the plant, which the passage says grows on as fast as ever. Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 3 of 27

Straight from the tin the ink is stiffer than the press wants, and over a run of forty thousand sheets that stiffness will lift the coating off the paper. So the pressman works a clear extender into it, a spoonful at a time, until the ink leaves the knife in a soft ribbon. He is not diluting the ink to make it go further. One property of the ink is wrong, its stiffness, and the extender is worked in to bring that one property down. Nothing else about the ink changes. Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 6 of 27

Text 1

Two rural routes that once ran half empty now run as one. Whitlock, who costed the merger for the district, reports that folding the northern route into the southern one cut the district's spending on transport by 18 percent. The saving comes from one driver, one vehicle, and one set of insurance premiums where there had been two of each.

Text 2

Nwosu's team followed six districts through three years of merged routes. In the first year every district saved close to what it had projected. By the third year four of the six were spending more than before. A merged route covers nearly twice the daily distance of either route it replaced, and a bus driven that far reaches the end of its service life in about half the usual time. The replacement falls due in year three.

Based on the texts, how would Nwosu's team (Text 2) most likely characterize Whitlock's claim in Text 1 that folding the northern route into the southern one cut the district's spending on transport by 18 percent?

Text 2 does not deny the 18 percent. Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 7 of 27

In an ordinary wire rope the strands are twisted one way and the wires inside each strand are twisted the other. That opposition is what keeps a loaded rope from spinning, because the twist the strands want to release and the twist the wires want to release pull against each other and cancel. The same opposition gives the rope its cross-hatched look, which is how a rigger tells one lay from another. A rope with both twists running the same way is more flexible, and it will turn a load slowly all day.
