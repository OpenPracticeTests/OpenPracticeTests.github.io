Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 1 of 27

Instrument makers who build harpsichords face a difficulty the player never sees. A harpsichord's string is plucked rather than struck, so its tone cannot be softened by a gentler touch at the keyboard. Everything an audience will hear is settled before the instrument leaves the workshop, in the thickness of the soundboard and the length of each string. The maker's craft is therefore ____: one mismeasured part cannot be compensated for in performance.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

The text says a single mismeasured part cannot be fixed later, so the craft must be precise and unforgiving. "Exacting" carries exactly that sense. "Hurried" describes pace, not precision, and the passage never mentions speed. "Ornamental" would concern decoration, which is not at issue. "Provisional" means temporary or subject to revision, the opposite of what the final sentence establishes.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 2 of 27

Bronze that has lain in wet ground for centuries carries a corrosion that keeps eating the metal even after the object reaches a museum case. Conservators cannot undo the damage already done. They can, however, arrest it: by sealing the object away from moisture and chloride, they starve the reaction that drives the corrosion forward.

As used in the text, what does the word "arrest" most nearly mean?

The sentence contrasts what conservators cannot do (undo the damage) with what they can: stop the reaction from continuing. "Halt" fits that contrast. "Capture" is the everyday legal sense of the word, which the sentence does not use. "Detect" describes finding the corrosion, not stopping it. "Reverse" is precisely what the previous sentence rules out.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 6 of 27

Text 1

A beaver dam holds back water that would otherwise run straight into the river, so a valley full of dams releases a storm's rain over days rather than hours. Restoring beavers to the headwaters, Lindqvist argues, is the cheapest flood defense a downstream town can buy, and the only one that repairs itself.

Text 2

Ferreira's team gauged twelve dammed streams through a winter of storms. In the narrow valleys, the ponds behind the dams filled within a few hours of a storm's beginning, and every drop that fell afterward passed downstream unslowed. Only where the valley floor was wide enough for the water to spread sideways did the flood peak arrive measurably later.

Based on the texts, how would Ferreira's team (Text 2) most likely respond to Lindqvist's argument in Text 1?

Ferreira's team found that a pond stops storing water once it is full, which in a narrow valley happens within hours. The delay Lindqvist promises therefore survives only in wide valleys, so the response is to attach a condition to his claim rather than to accept or reject it outright. Agreeing that the dams delay the peak ignores the narrow-valley half of the result, and the maintenance cost is Lindqvist's point rather than anything Text 2 gauged. The ponds plainly did fill, within a few hours. And Text 2 has the water passing downstream unslowed, which is neither faster than a bare channel nor the same in every valley.

The second sentence proposes that the sliding is merely accidental, and the third sentence answers it with three observations ("seek out," "repeat," "abandon") showing the behavior is deliberate. Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 10 of 27

A tardigrade dried to a husk survives conditions that would rupture any ordinary cell, and revives once wetted. Its trick is a family of proteins that set into a glass as the water departs, holding every molecule where it stood. Freezing is a different insult: the water stays where it is and expands into crystals. It follows that the tardigrade's tolerance of drying ____

Which choice most logically completes the text?

The proteins work by replacing departing water. Freezing does not remove water; it leaves it in place as crystals. So the mechanism that handles drying has nothing to act on when the tardigrade freezes, and the conclusion must be that drying tolerance does not transfer. Claiming it explains freezing survival reverses the logic. The glass-forming proteins are the very mechanism under discussion. And heat is never contrasted with an ordinary cell.

Which finding, if true, would most directly support the researchers' hypothesis?

The hypothesis makes two claims at once: smoke detection happens at the antennal pits, and heat is not the cue. Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 15 of 27

Tidal mills once ground grain along this estuary ____ channel is now silted and empty.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Both halves of this sentence can stand alone, so they need a mark strong enough to separate two independent clauses. A semicolon does that. A comma alone produces a comma splice. A colon promises that what follows will explain or specify what came before, and a silted channel does not explain grain milling. Running the clauses together with no punctuation gives a run-on sentence.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 16 of 27

The expedition carried only what could not be improvised on the ice ____ medicine, and film for the camera.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

What precedes the blank is a complete sentence, and what follows is a list that specifies the thing that sentence named. A colon is the mark for introducing such a list. A period and a semicolon each demand a complete sentence on the right and get a list with no finished verb in it. No punctuation at all runs the list straight into the clause, where ice fuel reads as one thing until the rest of the list arrives and the reading collapses.

Each spring the colony rebuilds ____ nest from mud carried up from the riverbank.

A possessive pronoun is needed, and the antecedent, "colony," is a singular noun. "Its" is the singular possessive. Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 22 of 27

Peat forms only where the ground stays too wet for dead plants to rot away completely. Drain a bog and it stops making peat almost at once. ____ the peat already beneath the surface does not simply vanish. It oxidizes slowly, over decades, releasing carbon the whole time.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Draining stops peat from forming, but it does not remove the peat that is already there. The blank sits at that turn, so it needs a contrastive transition. "However" supplies it. "For instance" would make the sentence an example of drainage halting formation, which it is not. "Likewise" would claim the two facts agree. "Therefore" would present the surviving peat as a consequence of drainage rather than as a qualification of it.

Reading and Writing · Module 1 · Question 23 of 27

A cave painting cannot be dated directly, because its pigment is mineral and holds no carbon. The thin crust of calcite that grows over the painting does hold carbon, and it can only have begun forming after the paint was laid down. ____ a date obtained from the crust establishes a minimum age for the painting beneath it.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

The final sentence follows logically from the one before it: because the crust formed after the paint, its date must postdate the painting, which makes it a minimum age. That is a consequence, and "Consequently" marks one. "In contrast" and "Nevertheless" would both signal opposition where there is agreement. "Similarly" would claim the two sentences make parallel points rather than that one produces the other.

The colon tells the reader what the missing word means: an account built from indirect traces rather than from the subject herself. Circumstantial names evidence of exactly that kind. Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 2 of 27

For a century the bell tower's lean was blamed on a foundation settling into soft ground. Core samples taken beneath it have made that explanation ____. The clay under the tower is undisturbed, and the lean turns out to be written into the masonry itself: course by course, the builders were correcting for an error they had already made.

The last sentence rules the settling explanation out: the ground never moved, and the lean was built in. An explanation that cannot be held is untenable. Text 1

Reading and Writing · Module 2 · Question 10 of 27

Near the coast the ice flows faster and folds old layers back over young ones. A core drilled at the center of the sheet will therefore ____
