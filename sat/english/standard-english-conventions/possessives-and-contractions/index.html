Possessives and Contractions

  • Standard English Conventions
  • Form, Structure, and Sense
←PreviousAll Reading and Writing lessonsNext→

On an apostrophe item you decide whether the word is a contraction, a possessive, or a plain plural. Sound cannot separate vendors from vendor's, so two written tests settle it instead of your ear.

Key takeaways

  • An apostrophe marks a contraction or a possessive; a plain plural takes neither.
  • Expand every apostrophe form: it is or it has, who is or who has, let us.
  • Put the possessive marking after the complete owner, never inside it.
  • A plural ending in -s takes the apostrophe alone; children takes 's.
  • Two owners who share what they own take one marking, on the last name. Two who own separately take one each.
  • Personal possessives and whose carry ownership with no apostrophe.

What the SAT may ask

  • Choose between its and it's, or whose and who's.
  • Mark a singular owner, a plural owner, or neither.
  • Decide whether two named owners share what they own.
  • Place the marking on an irregular plural such as children.

The rule

An apostrophe does one of two jobs. It stands where letters were dropped, which makes a contraction, or it sits after the complete owner, which makes a possessive.

Possession here is grammar, not law. The marked noun need not own what follows, only be connected to it as its owner, the one it is for, or its measure: the children's museum is a museum for children, a day's delay a delay lasting a day. Ask what the sentence connects, not who could sell it. A noun that only says what kind of thing follows is doing none of those three jobs and takes no marking, which is the case the special-cases card below covers.

An of phrase already names the owner, so the noun after of stays bare: the gable of the old brewery, never the gable of the old brewery's.

Both jobs are open to an ordinary noun. The kiln's cooling can mean the kiln is cooling or the cooling that belongs to the kiln, so expand before you decide.

A plain plural takes no apostrophe. Neither do the personal possessives or whose: they are already possessive, so a mark on one of them signals a contraction or a mistake.

Form Marking Example
Plain singular none one student
Plain plural none twelve students
One owner 's the student's projects
A plural owner ending in -s apostrophe alone the students' projects
A plural owner without -s 's the children's projects
Two owners, together 's on the last name Ines and Basil's vans
Two owners, separately 's on both names Ines's and Basil's vans
A personal possessive, or whose none yours, whose

The last two rows turn on who shares what, not on how many things there are. Ines and Basil's vans is the fleet the two of them own together, however many vans that is; Ines's and Basil's vans is her van or vans beside his. Only the sentence can say which, so look for what it says about the sharing.

The owner's number and the owned thing's number are separate questions. The student's projects is one student with several projects.

Pronouns covers its against it's and whose against who's from the other side.

Why the rule exists

The apostrophe is a written distinction only, so an apostrophe item cannot be answered by what sounds natural.

Worked examples

1. Read the apostrophe as two words

The laundry room's oldest washer rocks ____ drum on the spin cycle. (its / it's) The tenant ____ basket sits by the door folds first. (whose / who's)

Task: decide whether each blank holds a contraction or a possessive.

Evidence: It's expands to it is or it has, and the washer rocks it is drum fails on both readings. Who's expands to who is or who has, and the tenant who is basket fails the same way.

Answer: its and whose, the possessive forms.

Closest trap: possessive-contraction swap, which is what choosing by sound produces here.

2. Mark after the complete owner

One dancer owns the practice mirror: the dancer's mirror. Twelve dancers share the barre: the dancers' barre.

Task: place the marking once you know how many owners there are.

Evidence: Write the owner out in full first. One owner is dancer, so the marking is 's. Twelve owners give dancers, a plural already ending in -s, so the apostrophe follows it and nothing is added.

Answer: the dancer's mirror, the dancers' barre.

Closest trap: plural-possessive confusion, which writes the bare plural dancers where an owner has to be marked.

3. Irregular plurals and shared ownership

The children's shelf at the village library, never childrens'. Ana and Rosa run three shops between them, and share every one: Ana and Rosa's shops.

Task: mark two owners that the drilled pattern gets wrong.

Evidence: Childrens is not a word, so there is no -s for an apostrophe to follow; the complete owner is children, which takes 's. For the shops, the sentence says the two share every one, so the pair acts as a single owner however many shops there are.

Answer: the children's shelf; Ana and Rosa's shops. Had each run her own, it would be Ana's and Rosa's shops, with the same plural noun after it.

Closest trap: counting the things owned instead of asking who shares them. The number of shops decides nothing here.

Special cases

Cases the two tests do not settle

A stretch of time in the possessive slot takes the marking though nothing changes hands: a day's delay, two years' work. Written as a hyphenated modifier it takes none: a six-week course.

A plural in front of a noun does not always own it. It takes the marking where the thing is theirs, is for them, or is measured by them (a teachers' lounge) and none where it only says what kind (a departures board, a savings account). Those are the same three jobs the rule above names. Ask which the sentence means before reaching for the apostrophe.

A possessive can stand with its noun left out, once the sentence has named that noun.

Common traps

Four ways an apostrophe choice goes wrong

Trap Correction
possessive-contraction swap Try both verbs: who's is who is or who has
plural-possessive confusion A plain plural owns nothing, and the plural-owner pattern fits neither a pronoun nor an irregular plural
misplaced owner mark Write the owner in full, then mark once, after the whole of it
wrong category Ask what the slot needs: an owner marked, or no mark at all

The method: Contraction, plural, or possession

  1. Ask what the word is doing: contracting, pluralizing, or possessing. The choices vary on the apostrophe, and a homophone that is a different word altogether is often among them.
  2. Expand every apostrophe form, and try more than one reading. Read it's as it is and as it has, who's as who is and who has, let's as let us, and a noun's 's too. If no expansion fits, the apostrophe is not a contraction.
  3. For possession, find the owner and say whether it is singular or plural. Mark after the whole of the owner. One owner takes 's; a plural in -s takes the apostrophe alone; a plural without it takes 's. Two names sharing what they own take one marking, on the last.
  4. Write the form and reread the sentence. No personal possessive takes an apostrophe, and neither does whose. My, your, her, its, our and their need the owned noun after them; mine, yours, hers, ours and theirs stand where it has already been said.

Practice 1

  • Standard English Conventions
  • Form, Structure, and Sense
  • Introductory

Once each ride, the antique carousel dispenses ____ brass ring to the rider on the outermost row of horses.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Trap: possessive-contraction swap. It's is only ever it is or it has, and neither expansion fits in front of brass ring.
Trap: plural-possessive confusion. This hangs the plural-owner apostrophe on a pronoun, and its' is not an English word under any reading.
Correct. The ring belongs to the carousel, and its is the possessive pronoun, which carries ownership without an apostrophe.
Trap: possessive-contraction swap. This is the contraction written out in full, and the carousel dispenses it is brass ring is not English.

The method, applied

Step 1: four spellings of one pronoun, so the axis is the apostrophe. Step 2: expand. The carousel dispenses it is brass ring fails, and it has fails with it, so no contraction is hiding here. Step 3: the owner is the carousel, and no noun owner sits in the blank. Step 4: its is a personal possessive and takes no apostrophe.

Practice 2

  • Standard English Conventions
  • Form, Structure, and Sense
  • Introductory

Before morning practice, while the lane ropes are being strung across the pool, the swimmers hang ____ towels on the hooks along the wall.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Trap: possessive-contraction swap. They're is only ever they are, and the swimmers hang they are towels is not English.
Correct. The towels belong to the swimmers, and their is the possessive form that sits before the noun it owns.
Trap: wrong category. There points to a place and marks no ownership; it survives a read-aloud only because it sounds like the possessive.
Trap: wrong category. Theirs stands where the owned noun has already been said, so it cannot sit directly in front of towels.

The method, applied

Step 1: a contraction, two possessive forms, and a place word, three of them alike aloud. Step 2: expand they're to they are, and the swimmers hang they are towels fails. Step 3: the owner is the swimmers, with no noun owner in the blank. Step 4: a noun follows the blank, so the sentence needs a determiner rather than the standalone theirs. That leaves their.

Practice 3

Two stalls stand side by side under the covered market's iron roof, one rented by the cheesemonger and one shared by the fruit sellers, and their counters are not the same age: the cheesemonger's was rebuilt last spring, but the ____ has not been touched in thirty years.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Trap: plural-possessive confusion. This marks one seller, and the sentence has already said the second counter is shared by the fruit sellers.
Trap: plural-possessive confusion. A plain plural owns nothing, and the blank names the sellers' counter rather than the sellers.
Trap: plural-possessive confusion. A plural already ending in -s takes the apostrophe alone, with no second s stacked on top of it.
Correct. The owners are the fruit sellers, a plural ending in -s, so the apostrophe follows the whole owner and nothing is added. The owned noun is left out because the sentence named counters a clause earlier.

The method, applied

Step 1: one noun in four spellings, varying only in the apostrophe. Step 2: expand. Fruit seller is has not been touched fails, and fruit seller has fails too, so the apostrophe marks ownership. Step 3: the sentence says the second counter is shared by the fruit sellers, so the owner is a plural already ending in -s, and the apostrophe follows the whole of it. The owned noun is missing because counters was named a clause earlier, which a possessive may do. Step 4: no personal possessive is on offer.

Practice 4

  • Standard English Conventions
  • Form, Structure, and Sense
  • Standard

At the chess club's Tuesday meeting, the two members at the top of the ladder replay ____ sharpest games on the wall-hung demonstration board while the rest of the room follows the pieces from the far wall.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. Each other is one two-word pronoun, so the marking goes after the whole of it, and there is no plural each others for it to follow.
Trap: plural-possessive confusion. This borrows the plural-owner pattern and has to invent the -s to do it, since each other has no plural.
Trap: plural-possessive confusion. The same invented -s, with the ownership marking dropped as well, so the games belong to nobody.
Trap: wrong category. This is the form that follows a verb, and it marks no ownership, though the blank stands directly in front of the thing owned.

The method, applied

Step 1: one two-word pronoun in four spellings, so the axis is where the marking goes. Step 2: expand. The two members replay each other is sharpest games fails, and each other has fails with it. Step 3: the games belong to the two members, each to the other, so the owner is each other and the marking follows the whole of it. There is no plural each others for it to sit behind. Step 4: a reciprocal pronoun is not a personal possessive or whose, so nothing there refuses the mark.

Practice 5

  • Standard English Conventions
  • Form, Structure, and Sense
  • Advanced

Just past the ticket desk, the ____ museum's most crowded room holds the hands-on water exhibit, a knee-high river that visitors pump, dam, and redirect all afternoon.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Trap: plural-possessive confusion. This treats children as if it needed an -s to become plural, and childrens is not a word.
Correct. The owner is children, a plural that never ends in -s, so it takes the marking a single owner takes: apostrophe plus s. A museum for children is a children's museum: the possessive marks the connection, not a deed.
Trap: plural-possessive confusion. This invents a regular plural for a word that refuses one, and drops the ownership marking as well.
Trap: plural-possessive confusion. A bare plural owns nothing, and the museum's owner still has to be marked.

The method, applied

Step 1: one owner in four spellings, so the question is whether and where the apostrophe lands. Step 2: expand. The children is museum's most crowded room fails, and the children has fails with it, so the apostrophe marks possession. The museum is for children rather than owned by them, and the possessive covers that just as well. Step 3: the complete owner is children, a plural that never takes an -s. The drilled pattern of an apostrophe after the -s has nothing to attach to, so this plural is marked the way a single owner is: children's. Step 4: no personal possessive is on offer. Reread, and do not flinch at two markings in a row: the children's museum's most crowded room chains one owner inside another.

Practice 6

  • Standard English Conventions
  • Form, Structure, and Sense
  • Advanced

On the first Friday of each month, ____ supper club fills the room above the pharmacy: one long table, one shared menu, and two cooks trading courses at a single stove.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Trap: misplaced owner mark. Marking both names gives each cook a club of her own, and the sentence names one table, one menu, one club.
Trap: wrong category. Two bare names make a subject rather than a possessor, so nothing here ties the cooks to the club.
Trap: misplaced owner mark. The apostrophe lands inside the owner, and the complete owner is the pair Mara and Odile.
Correct. The two cooks own one club together, so the pair is a single owner and the apostrophe follows its final word.

The method, applied

Step 1: the choices move one apostrophe around a two-name owner, so the axis is where the marking belongs. Step 2: expand. Mara and Odile is supper club fills the room fails, and Mara and Odile has fails with it. Step 3: the sentence insists on one table, one menu, and two cooks at a single stove, so the pair owns the club jointly and counts as one owner. The marking follows the complete owner, which is the whole pair, so only the final name carries it. Marking each name would give each cook her own club. Step 4: no personal possessive is on offer.
