Subject-Verb Agreement

  • Standard English Conventions
  • Form, Structure, and Sense
←PreviousAll Reading and Writing lessonsNext→

Decide which word the verb belongs to, then match its number. The noun nearest the blank may not be the subject, so find the subject before you pick a form.

Key takeaways

  • A verb agrees with its subject: the word or phrase answering who or what is this verb about?
  • Present tense: a third-person singular subject takes the -s form (rings, is, has). A plural subject takes the bare form (ring, have) and, for be, are.
  • A noun inside a prepositional phrase or a who/which/that clause cannot be the main verb's subject.
  • After a fraction or a portion word, the of phrase is what decides the number. Keep it.
  • After there, or when the verb comes before its subject, look for the subject after the blank.
  • Two subjects joined by and are plural, unless the pair names one thing; or and nor take the nearer subject.

What the SAT may ask

  • Choose the verb form across a prepositional phrase or a who/which/that clause.
  • Choose the verb form when the subject comes after the blank.
  • Choose the verb form for subjects joined by and, or, or nor.
  • Choose the verb form after a fraction or a portion word.

The rule

A verb agrees in number with its grammatical subject, the word that answers who or what is this verb about? For an action verb that is the doer. For a passive or linking verb it is the thing the sentence describes.

In the present tense a third-person singular subject takes the -s form: the bell rings. Every other subject takes the bare form, I ring and you ring included. Be is the exception that has forms of its own for each person (I am, you are, she is), and be, have and do keep their own pairs (is/are, was/were, has/have, does/do). Modals such as can do not change at all.

Prepositional phrases, appositives, phrases set off by commas, and who/which/that clauses can stand between the subject and the verb. None of them contains the main verb's subject. Cross them out and read what is left.

Version Sentence
Full sentence The list of names taped beside the phones needs an update.
Crossed out The list needs an update.

One phrase you must not cross out is the of phrase after a fraction or a portion word. There it is what decides the number: most of the water is, most of the samples are.

The subject can follow the verb, after there or after an opening phrase that puts the verb first. In behind the shed ____ two barrels, the subject is barrels.

Two subjects joined by and make a plural subject, unless the pair names one thing (bread and butter is). With either/or or neither/nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject: neither the coins nor the ledger ____ takes a singular verb.

Why the rule exists

The number ending ties a verb back to its subject across a long interruption. In the crate of oranges weighs, the -s says the crate weighs, not the oranges.

Worked examples

1. Singular takes -s, plural takes the bare form

The kettle whistles. The kettles whistle.

Task: see where the -s goes.

Evidence: one kettle, and the verb carries the -s. Several kettles, and the noun carries the -s while the verb is bare.

Answer: whistles with the singular subject, whistle with the plural one.

Closest trap: verb -s confusion, reading the -s on a verb as a plural marker. On a present-tense verb it marks the singular.

2. Cross out the phrases between subject and blank

The basket of apples on the porch ____ more than it looks. (weigh / weighs)

Task: choose weigh or weighs.

Evidence: what does the weighing? The basket. Of apples and on the porch are prepositional phrases, so neither can hold the subject. Cross them out and what is left is the basket ____ more than it looks.

Answer: basket is singular, so weighs.

Closest trap: attractor noun. The plural apples sits next to the blank, and a reader who agrees by ear picks weigh.

3. Find the subject after the verb

There ____ two ways to check an answer under time pressure. (is / are)

Task: choose is or are.

Evidence: there is a placeholder, not a subject. Ask what exists: two ways, which comes after the blank.

Answer: ways is plural, so are.

Closest trap: inversion missed. Nothing before the blank is a subject. An opening place phrase can put the verb first in the same way: in along the fence ____ the roses, the subject is roses.

4. Two verbs, two subjects

The freight scales that the depot ____ every autumn still ____ within a pound. (recalibrate / recalibrates, read / reads)

Task: choose a form for each blank.

Evidence: two clauses, so two subjects. The main verb belongs to scales; the verb inside the that clause belongs to the depot.

Answer: the depot recalibrates inside the clause, the scales read outside it.

Closest trap: attractor noun, on the main verb. Depot is a singular noun standing between the subject and read, and taking it gives the scales reads. (Autumn is nearer still, and singular too.) Inside the clause the nearest noun happens to be the subject, which is why the two verbs have to be settled separately rather than by one habit.

Special cases

Subjects whose number is not obvious

After a fraction or a portion word (half, most, some, the rest), the of-phrase decides the number. Half of the crop is singular and half of the seedlings is plural. Keep that of-phrase when you cross the others out.

A number of means several and is plural. The number of names one figure and is singular. A number of entries were late, the number of entries was small.

A gerund phrase, or a that or whether clause, standing as the subject is singular however many plurals it holds: replacing the floorboards takes a week, that the joists were cracked surprises nobody. A what clause is the exception and takes its number from what follows the verb: what we need is a plan, what we need are volunteers.

When each, everyone, or more than one is the subject, the verb is singular.

Common traps

Traps on agreement items

Trap Correction
attractor noun Cross out the phrases between the subject and the blank, keeping the of phrase after a fraction or portion word; agree with what is left.
verb -s confusion On a present-tense verb, -s marks the singular, not the plural.
inversion missed When the verb comes first, find the subject after the blank.
missing finite verb An -ing or to form alone leaves the sentence with no verb.

The method: Find the verb's subject

  1. Read the choices and name what varies. One verb in singular, plural and nonfinite forms means an agreement item.
  2. Find the verb's subject. Ask who or what the verb is about, and do not answer by sound. With or or nor joining two subjects it is the nearer one. Where the blank is a who/which/that clause's own verb, it answers to the noun that pronoun stands for.
  3. Set aside the interrupting words. Cross out prepositional phrases, appositives, comma-fenced phrases and relative clauses standing between the subject and the blank. Leave the of phrase after a fraction or a portion word: there it decides the number.
  4. Decide the number, then check the verb in the whole sentence. A third-person singular subject takes the -s form, is, has; a plural subject takes the bare form, have, and are for be. Reread with your choice in place: the sentence must still have a finite verb, and Verb Forms and Tense covers which forms are one.

Practice 1

Every evening at six, the reading room's brass lamps ____ on with a click that carries down the hall.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. The subject lamps is plural, and a plural subject takes the bare form: the lamps switch on.
Trap: verb -s confusion. Switches is the singular form; the -s that makes a noun plural makes a present-tense verb singular, and lamps is plural.
Trap: missing finite verb. An -ing form alone is not a finished verb, so the lamps switching on leaves the sentence with no main verb.
Trap: missing finite verb. An infinitive cannot be a sentence's main verb, so the lamps to switch on is a fragment.

The method, applied

Step 1: the choices are four forms of one verb, so find the subject. Step 2: what switches on? The lamps. Step 3: nothing stands between lamps and the blank. Step 4: lamps is plural, so the bare form switch; switching and to switch would leave the sentence without a verb.

Practice 2

  • Standard English Conventions
  • Form, Structure, and Sense
  • Introductory

Freshly polished for parade day, the town band's oldest tuba ____ at the front of the procession.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Trap: verb -s confusion. Gleam is the plural form. A singular noun goes without -s, but a present-tense verb takes -s in the singular: one tuba gleams.
Trap: missing finite verb. The band's oldest tuba gleaming describes the tuba but never makes a statement about it, so the sentence has no main verb.
Correct. The subject tuba is singular, and a singular subject takes the -s form: the tuba gleams.
Trap: missing finite verb. An infinitive cannot be the main verb, so the tuba to gleam leaves a fragment.

The method, applied

Step 1: four forms of one verb, so find the subject. Step 2: what gleams? The tuba. Step 3: the opening phrase freshly polished for parade day comes before the subject, not between it and the blank, so there is nothing to strip. Step 4: tuba is singular, so gleams; gleaming and to gleam leave no finished verb.

Practice 3

  • Form, Structure, and Sense

The set of tuning forks in the music room's oak cabinet ____ pure tones when struck.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Trap: attractor noun. Produce is the plural form, and the only plural noun near it is forks, which sits inside a prepositional phrase. The subject is the singular set.
Correct. Cross out of tuning forks and in the music room's oak cabinet and what is left is the set ____ pure tones. Set is singular, so produces.
Trap: attractor noun. Are is plural, so it agrees with the nearby forks rather than with the singular subject set.
Trap: attractor noun. Have is the plural auxiliary, agreeing with the nearby forks rather than with the singular subject set.

The method, applied

Step 1: the choices vary one verb's number and finish, so find the subject. Step 2: what produces the tones? The set; the forks are what it is made of. Step 3: cross out of tuning forks and in the music room's oak cabinet, both prepositional phrases. What is left is the set ____ pure tones when struck. Step 4: set is singular, so produces.

Practice 4

  • Standard English Conventions
  • Form, Structure, and Sense
  • Standard

The paper lanterns that the restaurant's caretaker wires to a single timer above the back courtyard ____ at dusk all at once.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Trap: attractor noun. Glows agrees with caretaker, timer or the courtyard standing against the blank, but all three sit inside the relative clause, and the subject is the plural lanterns.
Trap: attractor noun. Is is singular, so it agrees with the nearby timer rather than with the plural subject lanterns.
Trap: missing finite verb. Glowing alone leaves the paper lanterns that the caretaker wires to a single timer glowing as a noun phrase with no verb.
Correct. Cross out the relative clause and what is left is the paper lanterns ____ at dusk all at once. Lanterns is plural, so the bare form glow.

The method, applied

Step 1: the choices vary one verb's number and finish, so find the subject. Step 2: what glows? The lanterns; the caretaker only wires them. Step 3: cross out the relative clause that the restaurant's caretaker wires to a single timer above the back courtyard. A that clause describes the subject and cannot contain it. What is left is the paper lanterns ____ at dusk all at once. Step 4: lanterns is plural, so glow. The clause puts four singular nouns inside itself, and the one standing against the blank is courtyard.

Practice 5

  • Standard English Conventions
  • Form, Structure, and Sense
  • Advanced

Along the ridge above the tree line, where the trail crosses a slope of broken granite, ____ the cairns that volunteers restack each spring.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. The sentence is inverted, so the subject cairns comes after the verb. Cairns is plural, so the bare form stand.
Trap: inversion missed. Stands agrees with ridge, slope, or granite, but all three sit inside the opening modifier, and the subject cairns follows the blank.
Trap: inversion missed. Is is singular, so it agrees with the singular nouns before the blank rather than with the plural cairns after it.
Trap: inversion missed. Was is singular and agrees with a noun in the opening modifier rather than with the plural cairns that follows the blank.

The method, applied

Step 1: four forms of one verb, varying number and tense, so find the subject. Step 2: what stands? Not the ridge or the slope: the cairns. Step 3: the sentence is inverted. Everything before the blank, along the ridge above the tree line and where the trail crosses a slope of broken granite, is an opening modifier full of singular nouns, and the subject comes after the verb. What is left is ____ the cairns. Step 4: cairns is plural, so stand. Number settles all four choices here; each spring is not what rules the past out.

Practice 6

  • Standard English Conventions
  • Form, Structure, and Sense
  • Advanced

Neither the almanac's long-range tables nor the journal the growers keep ____ the date of the first frost reliably.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Trap: verb -s confusion. Predict is the plural form. The subject journal is singular, and a singular subject takes the -s form predicts.
Correct. With neither ... nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject. That is the singular journal, so predicts.
Trap: missing finite verb. Predicting is not a finished verb, so the whole neither ... nor subject is left with no verb.
Trap: attractor noun. Have is plural and agrees with tables or growers, but the subject is the nearer one journal, which is singular.

The method, applied

Step 1: four forms of one verb, so find the subject. Step 2: what predicts? A neither ... nor pair, and with nor the subject is the nearer one, the singular journal. Step 3: cross out the growers keep, a relative clause with its that dropped, which puts the plural growers next to the blank. The far half of the pair, tables, does not control the verb. What is left is neither the tables nor the journal ____ the date reliably. Step 4: journal is singular, so predicts.
