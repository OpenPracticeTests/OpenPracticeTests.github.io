Subject-Verb Agreement: practice session

Unlike the lesson, the difficulty of each question stays hidden until you open the solution, so you meet them the way test day hands them to you: with no label. There is no timer and no score. Question

  • Introductory

The mast's wind gauge and its humidity sensor ____ their readings to the same recorder every hour.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Singular, and neither instrument is acting alone: and has joined two subjects into one plural subject.
Correct. Two things joined by and make a plural subject, and a plural subject takes the bare form.
An -ing form cannot anchor a clause, so the sentence would never say anything about the two instruments.
Singular, so it agrees with either instrument on its own rather than with the pair and has made out of them.

Why this answer

The subject is two things joined by and, the wind gauge and the humidity sensor, so it is plural however singular each half is on its own. A plural subject takes the bare form send. Both other finite choices are singular, and the -ing form could not serve as the sentence's verb at all.

Question

  • Standard

Sorting the film archive's unlabeled cans ____ most of a winter, since every reel has to be run before it can be listed.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with cans, but the cans are what is being sorted; the subject is the sorting itself.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause, so the sentence never reaches a verb.
Correct. The subject is the whole activity, sorting the film archive's unlabeled cans, which is one thing.
Plural, so it answers to cans rather than to the single activity that heads the subject.

Why this answer

The subject is a gerund phrase, sorting the film archive's unlabeled cans, and an activity is one thing however many objects it takes in. So the singular takes is right; take and have taken both agree with the plural cans, which sit inside the subject rather than heading it.

Question

  • Introductory

The flying club's two aircraft ____ under canvas from October to April, since the hangar has no heating.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. Aircraft is spelled the same in the singular and the plural, so two settles the number, and two things take the bare form.
Reads aircraft as singular, which the word can be. Two has already ruled that out here.
An -ing form on its own finishes nothing, leaving the sentence without a main verb.
Singular, so it makes the same wrong bet on a noun whose own spelling gives no signal either way.

Why this answer

Aircraft is one of the nouns English spells the same way in the singular and the plural, so the noun itself tells you nothing: two settles the number, and the verb is the only other place it shows. Two aircraft, so the bare form stay.

Question

  • Introductory

Linguistics ____ far beyond the vocabulary lists a first-year student expects: the sounds of a language, its sentence patterns, and its history all fall inside the subject.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

The -s on the end of linguistics belongs to the name of the field, not to a plural.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause, so the sentence would state nothing about the field.
Plural, and it treats the field's name as though it named several things.
Correct. Linguistics here names one field of study, and a field name takes a singular verb.

Why this answer

Linguistics looks plural and is not: like mathematics and physics, it names one field of study, so it takes the singular extends. The plural forms answer to the -s on the end of the word rather than to what the word refers to. The -s is not a rule by itself, though: the same shape can name a set of properties instead of a field, and the hall's acoustics are poor is then plural.

Question

  • Standard

Everyone on the station's two lifeboat crews ____ the log by the door before leaving, whatever the hour.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. Everyone is grammatically singular however many people it covers, so the verb takes the -s form.
Agrees with crews, which sit inside a prepositional phrase saying only which people are meant.
An -ing form cannot serve as the sentence's main verb.
Plural, so it answers to crews rather than to the singular everyone.

Why this answer

Everyone is singular even though it takes in a roomful of people, and on the station's two lifeboat crews is a prepositional phrase that cannot hold the subject. Singular subject, so signs.

Question

  • Introductory

Unlike the county's newer gritting trucks, the yard's snowplow ____ a blade that still has to be cranked up and down by hand.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with trucks, which sit in the opening comparison rather than in the subject.
Plural, and it makes the same wrong bet on the trucks the plow is being compared against.
Correct. The subject is the singular snowplow, sitting directly before the blank, so the verb takes the -s form.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause.

Why this answer

The opening unlike phrase names what the snowplow is being compared against, not what the sentence is about, so the plural trucks gets no vote. The subject is snowplow, singular and right beside the blank, so carries.

Question

  • Standard

The mountain hut's warden, along with the two volunteers who walk up every Friday, ____ the water tank and the stove before the weekend crowd arrives.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with volunteers, but along with adds a companion to the sentence, not to the subject.
Correct. Along with the two volunteers is a supplementary phrase fenced by commas; the subject is still the singular warden.
Plural, and it makes the same wrong bet: a phrase fenced by commas cannot enlarge the subject.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause, so the sentence never says what the warden does.

Why this answer

Along with, like as well as and together with, adds information without adding to the subject: it is not a coordinator, and only coordination with and makes a subject plural. Fence the phrase off and the sentence reads the mountain hut's warden ____ the water tank and the stove, so the singular checks is right.

Question

  • Standard

The card index listing the walled garden's four hundred plants ____ in a drawer beside the potting bench.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with plants, the object inside the participial phrase. The phrase describes the index, and the index is what sits in the drawer.
Plural, so it answers to the four hundred plants rather than to the single index.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause. The sentence already has one, listing, and it is doing modifier work.
Correct. The subject is the singular card index; listing the walled garden's four hundred plants only describes it.

Why this answer

Listing the walled garden's four hundred plants is a participial phrase modifying card index, and a modifier never holds the main clause's subject. Cross it out and the sentence reads the card index ____ in a drawer, so the singular sits is right.

Question

  • Standard

The village's three standpipes, whenever the summer stream falls below the intake, ____ dry for a day or two until the level comes back.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with stream or intake, both of which sit inside the clause wedged between the subject and the blank.
Singular, and it makes the same wrong bet, on nouns that belong to a subordinate clause rather than to the subject.
Correct. The subject is the plural standpipes, and a plural subject takes the bare form.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause.

Why this answer

The interrupter here is a whole subordinate clause, whenever the summer stream falls below the intake, carrying a subject and a verb of its own, so neither of its nouns can be the subject of the main sentence. Strip it and the sentence reads the village's three standpipes ____ dry, so the bare run is right.

Question

  • Standard

Most of the mortar between the courtyard's paving stones ____ out in the first hard winter after the stones are laid.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. After a portion word the of-phrase decides the number, and mortar is a singular mass noun.
Plural, and the only plural noun here is stones, which sits inside a phrase saying where the mortar is.
Plural again, and it makes the same bet on the paving stones.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause.

Why this answer

Most has no number of its own, so this is the one place the cross-out habit misfires: the of-phrase after a fraction or a portion word is what settles the verb, and crossing it out throws away the evidence. Most of the mortar is singular because mortar is. Had the sentence said most of the paving stones, the same portion word would take a plural verb.

Question

  • Standard

The taps in the campsite's washhouse ____ a slow trickle even at the height of summer.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with washhouse, the noun beside the blank. It sits inside a prepositional phrase, which never holds the subject whatever its own number.
Singular, and it makes the same wrong bet on washhouse rather than on the taps.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause.
Correct. The subject is the plural taps; in the campsite's washhouse only says which taps are meant.

Why this answer

The nearby noun is singular this time, which is the direction that gets forgotten: a prepositional phrase cannot hold the subject whichever number its own noun carries, so the method does not change. Cross out in the campsite's washhouse and the sentence reads the taps ____ a slow trickle, so the plural give is right.

Question

  • Introductory

More than one visitor ____ the same question about the tapestry's missing corner every week, and the guides have learned to answer it before it is asked.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

More than one counts several people, but the noun it heads is the singular visitor.
Correct. More than one takes a singular verb, because the verb follows the singular noun rather than the count.
Plural, so it follows the sense of the phrase rather than its grammar.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause.

Why this answer

More than one visitor means several visitors and still takes a singular verb, because the noun it heads is singular and agreement runs off the noun. Read it as one visitor ____ and the question is settled: asks.

Question

  • Introductory

The hotel's night porter or the duty engineer ____ the boiler pressure at midnight, and whichever of the two is on shift initials the sheet.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. Or offers a choice between two singular subjects rather than adding them together, so the verb stays singular.
Treats or as though it were and. Only and joins two subjects into a plural one.
Plural, and it makes the same wrong bet: one person does this, not two.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause.

Why this answer

And adds two subjects together; or sets them side by side and lets one of them act. Two singular subjects joined by or therefore take a singular verb, so logs is right, and the second clause confirms that only one of the two is on duty.

Question

  • Advanced

The dovecote and granary on the far side of the farmstead's yard, rebuilt in the year after the fire that gutted the range behind them, ____ each other across a strip of worn cobbles.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with side, yard, fire, or range, the singular nouns the two interruptions supply, or with granary alone, which is only half of the subject.
Singular, and it makes the same wrong bet after a long interruption has hidden the first half of the subject.
Correct. The dovecote and granary is a compound subject and therefore plural, whatever is wedged between it and the verb.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause; the sentence's other verb form, rebuilt, is a modifier rather than a main verb.

Why this answer

And has already made the subject plural before either interruption begins, and neither a prepositional phrase nor a supplementary phrase fenced by commas can undo that. Strip on the far side of the farmstead's yard and rebuilt in the year after the fire that gutted the range behind them, and the sentence reads the dovecote and granary ____ each other across a strip of worn cobbles, so the bare face is right.

Question

  • Advanced

A small plaque inside the tower door thanks the four volunteer keepers of the town clock who, working in pairs at the top of the shaft, ____ it by hand every Sunday morning.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with clock, the noun sitting just before who, or with shaft, the one just before the blank. The clock is the thing being wound, and the sentence names it again as it.
Correct. Who stands for the four keepers, so the verb inside the relative clause is plural.
Singular, and it makes the same wrong bet on the noun nearest the relative pronoun.
An -ing form cannot anchor the relative clause, which needs a finite verb of its own.

Why this answer

The blank is the verb of a relative clause, and a relative clause's verb agrees with whatever who refers back to. That is the four volunteer keepers, not the singular clock parked between them and the pronoun, and not shaft inside the participial phrase that follows the pronoun; the clock is the object it, so it cannot also be doing the winding. Plural antecedent, so wind.

Question

  • Advanced

Whether the marsh's two feeder channels silt up in the same winter, as the older drainage maps assume, ____ on a single sluice a mile upstream.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with channels, the subject of the whether clause rather than of the sentence.
Plural, and it makes the same wrong bet, this time on maps, the plural noun the comment leaves sitting against the blank.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause.
Correct. The subject is the whole whether clause, and a clause standing as a subject is one thing.

Why this answer

The subject is not a noun at all: it is the clause whether the marsh's two feeder channels silt up in the same winter, and a clause serving as a subject counts as a single question, so it takes a singular verb. Two plurals push the other way, channels inside the clause and maps inside the comment fenced by commas, and neither is the subject of the sentence. Read it as the question ____ on a single sluice and the agreement is settled.

Question

  • Advanced

The daily total at the foot of the clinic's appointment book, not the individual cards the receptionists fill in at the desk, ____ the figure the health board sees at the end of the month.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. The verb answers to the positive subject, the daily total, which is singular.
Agrees with cards or with receptionists, both of them inside the supplementary phrase fenced by commas, which no more governs the verb than an appositive would.
Not a finite verb, so the sentence would be left with no main verb at all.
Plural, so it makes the same wrong bet on the two plurals the sentence has fenced off.

Why this answer

A not phrase set off by commas is a supplement: it corrects a possible misreading of the subject rather than joining it, so it leaves the number alone. (Negation itself is no bar to being a subject, as not everyone agrees shows; what matters here is the fencing.) Strip it, and the prepositional phrase before it, and the sentence reads the daily total ____ the figure, so the singular supplies is right.

Question

  • Advanced

The science rooms along the school's upper corridor each ____ a separate gas shut-off, so a leak at one bench does not have to close the rest.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Answers to each, which is not the subject here: it is a quantifier dropped in beside the verb.
Correct. The subject is the plural science rooms; each only distributes what follows across them.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause.
Singular too, so it makes the same wrong bet on each, or on corridor and school in the phrase before it.

Why this answer

Each of the rooms has is singular because each is the subject there. This sentence is built the other way round: the subject is the science rooms, two singular nouns sit between it and the blank, and each has floated down beside the verb, where it spreads the shut-off across the rooms without changing what the verb agrees with. Plural subject, so have.

Question

  • Advanced

The proceeds of the village harvest supper, less the cost of the hall and the printing, ____ to the chapel roof fund every year, and the treasurer reads the figure aloud at the door.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with supper, cost, or printing, the singular nouns between the subject and the blank, and treats proceeds as though it named one sum.
Singular, and it makes the same wrong bet on the noun nearest the blank.
Correct. Proceeds has no singular: it is a plural-only noun and takes a plural verb.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause.

Why this answer

Proceeds is one of the nouns English keeps only in the plural, like belongings and premises, so it takes a plural verb even though the money is one sum. Neither the prepositional phrase of the village harvest supper nor the deduction fenced by commas can supply the subject, and both are stocked with singular nouns; strip them and the sentence reads the proceeds ____ to the chapel roof fund, so the bare go is right.

Question

  • Advanced

The number of climbers who sign the summit register over a single July weekend ____ the total for the whole of the previous winter.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Plural, and the plural noun it answers to is climbers, which sits inside the relative clause saying who signs.
Plural again, and it makes the same bet on the climbers rather than on the figure the sentence is about.
An -ing form cannot anchor the clause.
Correct. The subject is the number, one figure, and one figure is singular.

Why this answer

Two phrases that look alike take opposite verbs. The number of names a single figure and is singular; a number of means several and is plural. This sentence compares one total against another, so the subject is the number, and exceeds is the form it takes. Swap in a number of climbers and the sentence would be about the climbers themselves and take exceed.
