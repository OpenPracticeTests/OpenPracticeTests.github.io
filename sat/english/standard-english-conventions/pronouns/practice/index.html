Pronouns: practice session

20 questions on Pronouns, in random order. Unlike the lesson, the difficulty of each question stays hidden until you open the solution, so you meet them the way test day hands them to you: with no label. There is no timer and no score. Answer a question, then press Show solution whenever you want to see how you did and why.

Question

  • Introductory

The community garden's tool shed and rain barrel got the same coat of green paint last spring, and ____ will both need a second one before the ground freezes.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Reaches for the nearer of the two things and leaves the other one out, which both on the far side of the blank forbids: only a plural subject can be described as doing something both of.
Expand it and the clause loses its verb: it is will both need a second one. An apostrophe never makes a pronoun agree.
Correct. Two singular nouns joined by and make one plural antecedent, and the sentence says both of them need the second coat, so the plural subject pronoun is the only form that fits.
A possessive determiner has to be followed by the thing owned, and what follows the blank is a verb. Nothing is owned here.

Why this answer

Name the axis: one pronoun in four forms, so the question is number first and form second. The antecedent is not the noun nearest the blank but everything the paint went on, the shed and the barrel together, and two singular nouns joined by and are plural. Both says the same thing from the other side of the blank. The slot is a subject, which rules out the possessive, and the contraction will not expand.

Question

  • Introductory

The evening lecture series at the art museum still runs on 35-millimeter slides rather than a laptop, and next fall ____ will meet on Thursdays so that the hall is free on Tuesdays.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. What meets on a day of the week is the series, one thing however many slides it uses, and runs has already treated it as one.
Agrees with the slides, the plural noun standing between the antecedent and the blank. Slides do not meet on Thursdays; a lecture series does.
The possessive determiner, which must be followed by the noun it owns. The blank is the subject of will meet.
Expand it and two verbs collide: they are will meet on Thursdays. The contraction is also plural, so it answers to the wrong noun twice over.

Why this answer

A noun ending in -s is not automatically plural, and series is the standing example: one series, several slides. Ask what meets on Thursdays and the answer is the series, so the pronoun is singular, and the sentence's own runs confirms it before you choose. The plural noun beside the blank is there to collect the by-ear vote.

Question

  • Introductory

Tomas Beltran took over the ferry cafe last spring, and on the morning shift the baker and ____ start slicing bread before the first boarding call.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

The object form sitting in a subject's chair. Strip the compound, letting the verb fall back to the singular a lone subject wants, and the frame is him starts slicing, which no sentence allows.
A reflexive has to reflect the subject of its own clause, and here the pronoun is half of that subject. There is nothing for it to point back at.
A possessive, which would need a noun to own. The blank names one of the people doing the slicing.
Correct. The pronoun is half of the compound subject of start, and a subject slot takes the subject form.

Why this answer

Case comes from the pronoun's own job, and the fastest way to see the job is to strip the compound and try the pronoun alone, letting the verb take the singular a lone subject wants: he starts slicing bread stands, him starts does not. The other noun sharing the slot changes nothing. A possessive would need something to own and a reflexive would need somebody else to reflect.

Question

  • Introductory

Dana Okafor works the hatchery's night shift alone, so she has taught ____, from a borrowed manual, to read an egg's shadow rather than wait for the incubator's turner to fail again.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A plain object pronoun inside a clause has to name somebody other than that clause's subject, and this sentence names no one else at all. Alone is there to make that absence deliberate rather than an oversight.
Correct. The teacher and the learner are the same person, and a reflexive is the form English uses when a clause's object is its own subject.
The subject form in an object slot. Strip the fenced phrase and the frame is taught she to read an egg's shadow, which fails as soon as it is read back.
A standalone possessive stands in for a thing that is owned, as in the lamp is hers. It cannot be the person being taught.

Why this answer

Read the choices and the axis is case, one pronoun in four forms. The clause's subject is she, which is Dana, and its object is whoever learned to candle. No second person is named anywhere in the sentence, so a plain object pronoun would have nobody to stand for, and an object that is its own clause's subject takes the reflexive.

Question

  • Introductory

The troop leader stacked the split logs by the cabin door on Friday and left a note about the wet kindling on the woodbox for the quartermaster and ____.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. The pronoun is half of the object of for, and a preposition takes the object form however many nouns share the slot.
Strip the compound and the phrase is a note for I, which is what the strip test exists to expose. The subject form sounds careful and is still the wrong case.
A reflexive needs its own clause's subject to reflect, and the subject here is the troop leader. Politeness is not a case.
A standalone possessive names a thing owned, not a person the note was left for.

Why this answer

The four forms differ only in case, so find the pronoun's job: it is one of two people the note was left for, which makes it the object of the preposition for. Strip the other half of the compound and the answer is audible at once. The reflexive and the standalone possessive are not candidates at all, since nothing in the clause corefers and nothing is owned.

Question

  • Introductory

The insulated coveralls that hang inside the wind farm's tower door zip from ankle to collar in one piece, but ____ will be inspected for tears again before the first climb of the season, because a climber cannot patch a sleeve halfway up the ladder.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Counts the garment instead of reading the noun. Coveralls is one of the nouns English keeps plural even for a single garment, and the zip earlier in the sentence has already treated it as plural.
Expand it and the verb doubles: they are will be inspected. The contraction cannot fill a slot whose verb is already waiting on the other side of it.
Correct. The antecedent is coveralls, which stays plural however many garments it names, so the pronoun standing for it is plural too.
A possessive determiner with nothing to own: what follows the blank is a verb phrase, not a noun.

Why this answer

Some nouns stay plural however few things they name: coveralls, pliers, binoculars. Number is a fact about the noun and not about the count, which is why one garment that zips from ankle to collar still takes a plural verb and a plural pronoun. The blank is a subject slot, so neither the possessive nor the contraction is in the running.

Question

  • Standard

At the end of a landing, either the paper quota log or the two dockside tablets must have ____ entries signed by the skipper before the boat ties up.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with the log, the half of the pair that sits farther from the pronoun. With either ... or the nearer half is the one that counts.
Expand it and the phrase is it is entries, which owns nothing and finishes nothing.
The standalone possessive, which stands in place of an owned noun (the entries are theirs) and never in front of one.
Correct. An either ... or pair does not add its halves together; the pronoun agrees with the half beside it, and that half is the two tablets.

Why this answer

And adds, or chooses. A subject built with either ... or hands both the verb and the pronoun to whichever half stands nearer, so the plural tablets governs here and the singular log is the decoy. The remaining two choices are ruled out before number is considered at all: one is a contraction that will not expand, and one is a possessive that cannot stand in front of the noun it owns.

Question

  • Standard

Both of the chimneys on the lock keeper's cottage were relined last spring, but ____ had cracked again at the shoulder by August.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Carries over the habit that a counting word in front of an of-phrase is singular. That is true of each of, every one of and neither of, which take the members one at a time; both of takes the two together and is plural. The singular cottage sitting between the antecedent and the blank collects the stray vote.
Correct. Both is plural however singular the things it counts, so the pronoun standing for it is plural too, and the sentence's own were relined has already said so.
Expand it and the clause doubles its verb: it is had cracked again.
A possessive determiner with nothing to own. What follows the blank is a verb phrase.

Why this answer

Counting words in front of an of-phrase are not all the same number, and this is the one that goes the other way. Each of, every one of and neither of pick the members off one at a time and stay singular; both of takes the two together and is plural. The sentence has already committed to that with were relined, and the singular cottage, inside the phrase that modifies chimneys, is there to collect a stray vote.

Question

  • Standard

The planetarium's gift shop, a narrow room lined with star globes and folded sky charts, closes for inventory every January, and ____ will reopen the week the spring shows begin.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with the globes and the charts, which are furniture inside the appositive. An appositive renames the antecedent; it does not replace it or lend it a number.
Correct. What closes in January and opens again in spring is the shop, the singular noun the commas are wrapped around, and the singular closes has already treated it as one.
The possessive determiner, which needs a noun to own. The blank is the subject of will reopen.
Expand it and the clause carries two verbs: it is will reopen the week.

Why this answer

The commas fence a description of the shop, not a new subject, so lift the whole of it out and the sentence reads the gift shop closes for inventory every January, and ____ will reopen. The plural nouns are inside the fence, which is exactly why they were put there. Singular antecedent, subject slot, so the plain subject pronoun.

Question

  • Standard

The orchestra's librarian hands the marked string parts to ____ arrives first at the rehearsal room, since the bowings have to be copied before the downbeat.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. The pronoun is the subject of arrives, and the whole clause, not the pronoun alone, is what the preposition to takes as its object.
Takes its case from the preposition standing to the left of the clause. Case comes from the pronoun's job inside its own clause, and this pronoun does the arriving.
Wrong on both counts: the wrong case, and a relative pronoun where the sentence needs the form meaning anyone who.
The right case in the wrong shape. Whoever means anyone who, and that is what the sentence wants: the parts go to any early arriver rather than to a particular person. A relative who would have to attach to a noun already named, and no such noun is here.

Why this answer

A preposition governs the phrase that follows it, and here what follows is a whole clause. Look inside that clause for the pronoun's job: it performs arrives, so the case is subject, and the to on its left decides nothing. Then choose the shape. Whoever reads as anyone who, which is what the sentence means, and a relative who would need a noun in front of it to attach to. (Absence of a noun is not by itself the test: the librarian asked who had arrived first has no such noun and takes who, because that clause is a question rather than a name for a person.)

Question

  • Standard

The pottery night class meets after the studio has closed, so when Rosa Delgado stays late to finish a bowl, the instructor and the studio monitor walk ____ through the kiln's stacking order.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

A reflexive has to match the subject of its own clause, and this clause's subject is two people. Neither of them is who got walked through the order.
The subject form in an object slot: walk she through the order fails the moment it is read back.
A standalone possessive names something owned, as in the bowl is hers. The blank names the person being walked through the stacking order.
Correct. The pronoun is the object of walk, and it stands for Rosa Delgado rather than for either member of the clause's subject, so the plain object form is the one with grammar behind it.

Why this answer

The reflexive is the polite-sounding pick and the sentence bars it outright: a reflexive points back at the subject of its own clause, and that subject is the instructor and the monitor together. The person being walked through the stacking order is named in the clause before, so the blank wants a plain object pronoun, which is also what the verb in front of it demands.

Question

  • Standard

Amos Whitfield had already built four of the benches in the hospice garden, so the committee expected ____ to shape the last two from the elm that came down in the storm.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Reads the pronoun as the subject of to shape and stops there. It is that subject, and the subject of an infinitive is the one subject English puts in the object form.
A possessive determiner needing a noun to own, where the sentence needs the person who will do the shaping.
Correct. The pronoun is the object of expected and the subject of the infinitive that follows, and both jobs call for the object form.
A reflexive needs its own clause's subject to reflect, and that subject is the committee.

Why this answer

In this construction a pronoun doing a subject's work still takes the object form. The committee expected him to shape the benches is the pattern, and expected he is what happens when the case is read off the infinitive instead of off the verb governing it. The page's rule statement carries the rule; the possessive owns nothing here, and the reflexive has nobody in its own clause to point back at.

Question

  • Standard

The volunteer millers set out their hand tools beside the tide mill's great wheel at every open house, but ____ turns only while the ebb runs, so a late visitor sees nothing move.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. What turns is the wheel, singular, the millers already have their own pronoun earlier in the sentence, and the singular verb turns confirms the number.
Takes the millers, who are plural and who have already been given their earlier in the sentence. A plural subject cannot carry turns either, so the sentence rejects this twice over.
Expand it and the clause carries two verbs: it is turns only while the ebb runs.
A possessive determiner needs a noun to own, and what follows the blank is a verb phrase.

Why this answer

Two antecedents sit in this sentence, one plural and one singular, and each keeps its own pronoun: the millers have their hand tools, and the wheel gets the blank. Which one the blank takes is settled by the verb after it, which is singular and which describes what a wheel does rather than what a miller does. A pronoun's number comes from the noun it stands for, never from the last pronoun on the page.

Question

  • Advanced

The mason ____ the historical society's trustees say rebuilt the meetinghouse chimney in 1954 left no invoice behind, so the repair is dated only by a scrap of newspaper found in the flue.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Reads the pronoun as the object of say, which is what the interpolation is built to suggest. Cover the trustees say and the pronoun is left performing rebuilt.
A relative possessive has to own the noun after it, and the noun after it here is the trustees, who belong to nobody in this sentence.
Points at things, and a mason is a person, so this clause cannot open with it whatever the case turns out to be.
Correct. The trustees say is an interruption with no grip on the pronoun; the pronoun is the subject of rebuilt, so it takes the subject form.

Why this answer

Two clauses are laid over each other here. Lift out the reporting clause the trustees say and what remains is the mason ____ rebuilt the meetinghouse chimney, where the pronoun plainly does the rebuilding. Run the case test on that: he rebuilt the chimney, a subject answer, so the subject form. The object form is attractive only because the pronoun sits in front of a verb that takes objects.

Question

  • Advanced

The race director's files list the runners who withdrew that spring but never make clear ____ came up from the lottery's waiting list to fill the gaps, so the finish list is the only evidence left.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

The object form, chosen because the blank sits immediately after a verb. Position is not case: this pronoun belongs to the clause on its right, where it does the coming up.
The fused form, which stands in for a noun and opens a clause at the same time. Read it as what it means and the sentence collapses: never make clear anyone who came up is not what the files fail to do.
Correct. Somebody came up from the waiting list, and that somebody is the pronoun, so it is the subject of came and takes the subject form.
Both errors at once: the fused form where a plain one belongs, and the object case where the pronoun is a subject.

Why this answer

An embedded question is a clause, and a clause has its own subject. The pull toward the object form comes from position alone, since the blank sits directly after a verb. Look inside the clause instead and it reads ____ came up from the waiting list, which has no subject until the pronoun supplies one. The case test agrees: he came up from the list, a subject answer. The four choices are one pronoun crossed two ways, plain against fused and subject against object, and only one cell is right.

Question

  • Advanced

The mirror cells behind the two big telescopes each rest on three steel pads, and ____ actuators are recalibrated whenever a cold front has moved through overnight.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Takes the floating each as the controller. An each that has slid past the subject to sit beside the verb spreads the action out one by one; it does not make the subject singular, and rest proves the subject stayed plural. No singular noun in this sentence could own an actuator either.
Correct. The antecedent is cells, still plural with each standing where it does, so the possessive that stands for it is plural too.
Expand it and the phrase owns nothing: it is actuators are recalibrated.
The right number in the wrong shape. A standalone possessive replaces the noun owned rather than standing in front of it.

Why this answer

Position decides this one. Each of the cells would head the subject and make everything singular, but here each has drifted to the right of the subject and sits beside the verb, where it distributes the resting without touching the number of the noun. The plural rest is the free confirmation, and the pronoun follows the same plural noun the verb does.

Question

  • Advanced

The battery box that powers the river gauge below the two highway bridges has been swapped out twice this year, and ____ latch is already rusted through again.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Agrees with the bridges, which sit inside a prepositional phrase inside a relative clause. Two layers deep is still not the antecedent.
A relative possessive opens a clause, and the sentence has already opened and closed the only relative clause it wants.
Correct. The latch belongs to the box, the singular thing that has been swapped out twice, so the singular possessive determiner is what stands in front of it.
Expand it: it is latch is already rusted through, two verbs and no owner.

Why this answer

Strip in layers. Cross out below the two highway bridges, then that powers the river gauge, and the sentence collapses to the battery box has been swapped out twice this year, and ____ latch is already rusted through. Nothing plural survives the stripping, which is the point of stacking a prepositional phrase inside a relative clause: the farther a noun is from the head, the more it looks like the one you want.

Question

  • Advanced

The choir tour placed its singers with eleven host parents, four ____ live within walking distance of the concert hall, so those four can walk to the late rehearsal after supper.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Correct. A supplementary clause has to be attached to the sentence by a relative pronoun, and after a preposition that pronoun takes the object form.
A personal pronoun attaches nothing. It leaves two complete sentences joined by a comma, which is a splice however true both halves are.
The right kind of pronoun in the wrong case: a preposition takes the object form, and of is a preposition wherever it stands.
Points at things, and the eleven are people. The person-pronoun is the only candidate here.

Why this answer

Ask first what the comma is doing. It is opening a supplementary clause about the host parents, and a clause of that kind is joined to its sentence by a relative pronoun rather than sitting beside it with a personal one. That settles the kind; the preposition of settles the case; and the fact that the eleven are people settles which relative pronoun it is.

Question

  • Advanced

Because volunteers fence ____ against foxes every April, the sand spit has held a steady tern nest count, while the count on the two mainland beaches, which no one has ever fenced, has fallen by half.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Reaches downstream for the mainland beaches, the rival antecedent, which the sentence itself says no one has ever fenced. The volunteers cannot be it either, since an object pronoun does not point back at the subject of its own clause.
Expand it and the clause makes no sense: volunteers fence it is against foxes.
A possessive determiner with nothing to own; the blank is what the volunteers fence, not something owned by them.
Correct. The pronoun stands for the sand spit, which the main clause names a few words later, and the spit is one place, so the singular object pronoun fills the slot.

Why this answer

A pronoun usually follows its antecedent, and a fronted clause is where it does not: the because clause runs first and the noun it depends on arrives with the main clause after it. The only other candidate is ruled out in words rather than by taste, since the sentence says outright that no one has ever fenced the mainland beaches. Read the main clause first, then fill the blank.

Question

  • Advanced

The coaches have not named a spring equipment manager yet, and although three of them have said they would step in if nobody else did, whoever finally volunteers will find that ____ inherited a ledger three seasons out of date.

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

Singular they stands for one person and still takes the plural verb form, exactly as singular you does: you have, never you has.
Correct. Whoever finally volunteers is one person whose name nobody knows yet, which is where singular they belongs, and singular they takes have.
The pronoun stands for whoever finally volunteers, a person, and it does not stand for a person. The only singular thing in the sentence is the ledger, and the ledger cannot be what inherits it.
The object form in a subject slot. The pronoun does the inheriting, so it takes the subject form.

Why this answer

Two things have to be held apart. The they earlier in the sentence stands for the three coaches and is plural; the blank stands for whoever finally volunteers, which is one person, not yet named. Singular they is the standard form for exactly that, and the form it takes is the plural one: they have, never they has. The pronoun is also the subject of inherited, which rules out the object form, and it stands for a person, which rules out it.
