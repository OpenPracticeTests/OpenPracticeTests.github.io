Transitions

  • Expression of Ideas
  • Transitions
←PreviousAll Reading and Writing lessonsNext→

A Transitions item blanks out a connector and asks which one belongs. Every choice is a real transition, and none of them is a vocabulary word, so do not pick by subject or by tone alone. Decide what the second idea does to the first, then read the choices.

Key takeaways

  • Name the relationship before you read the choices.
  • Six families cover this question type: continue, contrast, cause, specify, sequence, restate.
  • Contrast has four kinds: plain opposition, concession, substitution, correction.
  • A shared subject is not agreement, and a topic change is not disagreement.
  • The blank can sit inside a sentence, and reach back further than the sentence before it.
  • When two choices share a family, check direction, then kind, then precision.

What the SAT may ask

  • Complete the text with the most logical transition.
  • Choose between two transitions from the same family.
  • Point a cause word in the right direction.
  • Connect a new paragraph to the one before it.

The rule

Name the relationship before you read the choices. Find the two ideas the blank joins, which are usually the sentence before it and the one carrying it, then say what the second does to the first.

Family The second sentence Words
continue adds more of the same also, in addition
contrast sets something against it however, even so, instead
cause follows from it, or explains it therefore, as a result
specify narrows to an instance or member for example, in particular
sequence places it in time or order first, finally
restate repeats it in other words that is, in short

Continue has a narrower kind: similarity. Likewise and in the same way say the second idea runs the same way as the first, so take one only where a reader can see what the two have in common. In addition asks less: it only puts one fact beside another.

Contrast comes in four kinds. A concession keeps its position after giving a point away. A substitution replaces an option the text ruled out. A correction follows a reported belief, or an impression the text has just created. Take plain opposition when none of those is there.

Correcting and pressing harder are different jobs. The bridge was thought to be Victorian. In fact it was built in 1740. That puts a reported belief right, so it is a correction.

The bridge was built in 1740. Indeed, its piles were felled the winter before. That agrees with the sentence before it and drives it home, so it continues. Neither word is fixed to one job. Ask what a sentence does to the claim before it, not which word sits in the gap.

Restate has no mechanical test. Ask whether the next sentence carries you further or repeats what you have, sometimes several sentences at once, in fewer words.

Why the rule exists

A transition asserts a relationship. Put the wrong one in and the text now claims a cause, a turn or an instance it never had.

Choose the move

The question asks forFirst move
The next sentence hands you what you already have in fewer wordsRestate, even if it uses new words
Two choices left in one familyCheck direction, then kind, then precision
Two contrast words leftLook for a rejected option, reported belief, held position, or an impression just created

Worked examples

1. Name the relationship, then match a choice

The town's oldest streetlights burn a gas flame behind glass, and a lamplighter walks the route at dusk with a pole to light each one. Each gas lamp costs the town forty times what an electric one costs to run for a year. ____ the council has kept eleven of them lit on the high street.

A. Therefore, B. Nevertheless, C. For example, D. Likewise,

Task: name the relationship.

Evidence: before the blank, a gas lamp costs forty times what an electric one costs. After it, the council keeps eleven of them burning.

Answer: the second idea goes against the first, so the family is contrast. Only Nevertheless is a contrast word.

Closest trap: Therefore would make the cost the council's reason for keeping the lamps, and the printed cost is a reason to drop them.

2. Test the relationship, not how it reads

A funicular's two cars hang on opposite ends of one cable, so neither can move without sending the other the same distance the other way. ____ the car at the top is loaded with water before it is released, and its weight carries the empty car up.

Candidate 1: Meanwhile,

Candidate 2: As a result,

Task: choose between two candidates that both read smoothly in the gap.

Evidence: one cable ties the two cars together, so whatever the loaded car does to the cable is what the empty car feels.

Answer: the second idea follows from the first, so the family is cause. The water lifts the bottom car only because the cable makes the two one machine.

Closest trap: Meanwhile puts the two at the same time and leaves the mechanism unnamed.

3. A blank inside the sentence

A sheet of paper laid flat will not hold a coin. Fold the same sheet into a fan of narrow pleats and it will carry a stack of them. A cardboard box, ____, takes its stiffness from the fluted layer glued between its two flat faces, not from any thickness of its own.

A. likewise, B. in addition,

Task: the blank sits inside a sentence, so find the two ideas it joins. They are the pleated paper and the box, and the paper takes two sentences to set up.

Evidence: both get their strength from a shape rather than from more material.

Answer: likewise. The box runs the same way as the paper, and the reader can see what the two have in common.

Closest trap: in addition would only set the box beside the paper as a second fact, and the point is that the two work alike.

Common traps

Wrong family, wrong direction, wrong precision

Trap Correction
wrong category Name the relationship first; a topic change is not a turn
wrong direction Say which sentence produces which
near-synonym imprecision Ask what each word promises next

The method: Name it before you read it

  1. Read the ideas on either side of the blank and say what the second does to the first, in plain words. Cover the choices while you do it. A blank can sit inside a sentence, and the idea it reaches back to can be more than one sentence away.
  2. Name the family if a name fits: continue, contrast, cause, specify, sequence, restate. If no name comes to you, keep your own words for the relationship and go on. The prediction is there to help you sort, not to be graded.
  3. Try the choices against what you said. Usually one matches. Do not ask which word sounds smoothest in the gap.
  4. If two still fit, check direction, then kind, then precision. Direction: which idea produces which. Kind: a held position, a rejected option, a reported belief, or an impression the text has just created. Precision: what each word promises next. Then read the finished passage back.

Practice

Work each item with the method, untimed, until the method is automatic. Practice 1

  • Expression of Ideas
  • Transitions
  • Introductory

Wet concrete does not harden by drying out. Its cement and its water lock together into a mesh of crystals, and the mesh keeps growing only for as long as there is water left for it to grow in. ____ a crew that pours a slab in July covers it with damp sheets for a week, and a slab left to bake in the sun ends up weaker than one kept wet.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct. Covering the slab follows from the mesh needing water to stay, so the blank marks a cause producing its consequence.
Trap: wrong category. The damp sheets do not push back against the mechanism; they are what a crew does because of it.
Trap: wrong category. Covering a slab is a response to the reaction, not an instance of the reaction happening.
Trap: wrong category. The damp sheets are not one more fact about the mesh; they are what the mesh's need for water forces a crew to do.

The method, applied

Step 1: the mesh grows only while water is there, and crews keep slabs damp. Step 2: the last sentence is what people do because the one before it is true, so the family is cause. Step 3: the contrast, specify and continue words all fail on family. Step 4: nothing stands beside For this reason, so read it back into the gap.

Practice 2

  • Expression of Ideas
  • Transitions
  • Introductory

A swimmer throws off waves the whole length of a race, and waves slow everybody down. In a shallow pool they run down, strike the bottom, and come back up into the lanes as chop that the rest of the field then has to fight through. ____ in a pool three meters deep the same waves spread out on the way down and are spent long before they could return.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Trap: wrong category. The deep pool is not caused by the shallow one; it is the case the text turns to instead of it.
Trap: wrong category. The sentence before the blank is about shallow pools, and a deep pool is not an instance of a shallow one.
Correct. The two pools are set against each other on one dimension, what becomes of a swimmer's waves, and this is the only choice that names a difference.
Trap: wrong category. The second sentence does not add a fact about the shallow pool; it swaps the pool for the opposite case.

The method, applied

Step 1: shallow pool, the waves bounce back as chop; deep pool, they spread and die. Step 2: the second sentence sets the other case against the first, so the family is contrast. Step 3: only one contrast word is offered, and the cause, specify and continue words fail on family. Step 4: read it back in and the two pools stand opposed.

Practice 3

  • Expression of Ideas
  • Transitions
  • Standard

A loaf left out overnight turns firm and crumbly, and the obvious explanation is that it dried. Drying is real, and it is not the main event. Most of the firmness is the starch inside the crumb slowly recrystallizing, gathering itself back into the tight ordered packing it held before the loaf was ever baked. Warming the crumb breaks that packing up again. ____ a stale loaf put into a warm oven for ten minutes comes out soft, without a drop of water being added to it.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Trap: wrong direction. Right family, reversed arrow: it offers the reheated loaf as the reason warming breaks the packing up, when it is the other way about.
Correct. The text says warming breaks the packing up, so a warmed loaf going soft with no water added is what that sentence produces.
Trap: wrong category. The paragraph set the drying explanation aside in its second sentence, so no standing claim is left for However to turn against. A loaf going soft with no water added bears that out, and bearing out is not turning.
Trap: wrong category. The last sentence is not a parallel case running alongside the mechanism; it is what follows from it.

The method, applied

Step 1: staling is starch recrystallizing, and warming breaks that packing up. Step 2: the last sentence is what the sentence before it produces, so the family is cause. Step 3: the continue word fails on family, and so does However, because the drying explanation it would turn against was set aside back in the second sentence. Step 4: two causal choices remain, so check direction. That is because makes the oven the reason for the chemistry, and the arrow runs the other way.

Practice 4

  • Expression of Ideas
  • Transitions
  • Standard

A hearing visitor to a school for deaf students in another country will often expect to follow at least a little of the signing, on the theory that a sign shows you what it means. ____ sign languages are no more universal than spoken ones. Each grew up among its own signers, so the sign for something as ordinary as a tree may picture a trunk and branches in one country and be a shape drawn in the air in the next, and the vocabulary a visitor was counting on reading turns out to be the part that varies most.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Trap: wrong category. A visitor's expectation cannot produce the fact that sign languages differ, and the second sentence denies the first rather than following from it.
Trap: wrong category. An illustrating word needs the sentence after it to bear out the one before. This one denies it. The real example, a tree signed two ways, arrives a sentence later.
Trap: wrong category. A continue word says the second sentence runs alongside the first, agreeing, and this one contradicts it outright.
Correct. The first sentence reports what a visitor expects and labels it a theory; the second says the expectation is false, which is a correction.

The method, applied

Step 1: a visitor expects to follow the signing, on the theory that a sign shows you what it means. After the blank, sign languages are no more universal than spoken ones. Step 2: that denies the expectation, so the family is contrast and the kind is correction, since the belief is reported rather than asserted. Step 3: only one contrast word is offered, and the cause and continue words need agreement. Step 4: the real example, the tree, arrives one sentence too late.

Practice 5

  • Expression of Ideas
  • Transitions
  • Advanced

Three things decide whether a sign can be read from a moving car: how brightly it is lit, how far apart its letters sit, and how tall its lowercase letters stand once you set aside the ascenders and descenders, a measure designers call x-height. ____ x-height is the one that is settled before the sign is drawn. Brightness is a lamp and spacing is a number in the layout, and both are set on the sign itself; x-height comes with whichever typeface was chosen, so a face carrying a shallow one hands a driver less letter at every point size the frame will hold.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Trap: near-synonym imprecision. An illustrating word promises a case of the claim just made. This sentence gives no case; it lifts one of the three out and says what sets it apart.
Trap: wrong category. Singling out one of three listed factors does not disagree with having listed them, so there is no claim here to push against.
Correct. The first sentence names three factors and the next picks one out as the one already fixed by the time the sign is drawn, which is what this phrase marks.
Trap: wrong category. Listing three factors is not a reason why one of them should already be settled, and the text supplies that reason afterward.

The method, applied

Step 1: three named factors, then one of them singled out. Step 2: the second sentence narrows to a member of the group the first listed, so the family is specify. Step 3: the contrast word fails, since nothing is disagreed with, and the cause word fails, since naming three factors does not bring about the fixing of one. Two specify words are left. Step 4: precision. The sentence does not show the three-factor claim in action; it lifts one factor out and says what separates it from the other two. That is singling out, which In particular marks, not illustrating, which For example promises.

Practice 6

  • Expression of Ideas
  • Transitions
  • Advanced

An organist cannot tune an organ before a concert the way a violinist tunes a violin, and an organ's pitch moves with the season. That is a fact about the instrument rather than a fault in it. The pitch of a flue pipe is set by the speed of sound in the column of air standing inside it, and sound moves faster through warm air, so a warm pipe sings sharp and a cold one sings flat. The metal has almost nothing to do with it. ____ a church that loses five degrees overnight takes every flue pipe down by the same interval, and an organist who arrives to a cold instrument and draws only flue stops finds them flat, in tune with one another, and perfectly playable.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct. If pitch tracks the air rather than the metal, a temperature falling across the whole church moves every pipe together and leaves the intervals between them untouched.
Trap: wrong category. A concession needs a position under pressure, and the playable cold organ is the claim's own output rather than a cost the text absorbs.
Trap: wrong category. The sentence before the blank says the metal barely matters, and the sentence after it carries that fact out to its end. A surprise is not a turn.
Trap: wrong direction. It offers the cold church as the reason the metal does not matter, when the metal not mattering is why cooling moves the whole instrument together.

The method, applied

Step 1: pitch is set by the air in the pipe rather than the metal, and a church that cools takes every pipe down together. Step 2: the last sentence is what the mechanism predicts, so the family is cause. Step 3: both contrast words fail. A pitch that moves with the season sounds like trouble and the ending sounds like relief, so the paragraph reads as a reversal, but the text called that movement a fact rather than a fault, so nothing here puts a position under pressure and nothing turns against it. Step 4: two causal choices remain, so check direction. That is because makes the cold church the reason the metal does not matter, and the arrow runs the other way.
