Transitions: practice session

20 questions on Transitions, in random order. Unlike the lesson, the difficulty of each question stays hidden until you open the solution, so you meet them the way test day hands them to you: with no label. There is no timer and no score. Answer a question, then press Show solution whenever you want to see how you did and why.

Question

  • Introductory

The letters stamped on a pencil describe a recipe rather than a hardness anyone could measure by hand. The core is graphite mixed with clay and fired, and the proportions decide everything about the line it leaves. ____ the H grades carry the most clay of any, and their line is pale enough that a draftsman can lay one across another without the two smearing into one.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

An adding word claims the H grades are a further fact standing beside the recipe. They are the recipe shown in one part of the range.
Correct. The sentence before the blank states the rule in general; the sentence after it shows that rule in one grade.
Which letters were given which proportion is a convention somebody settled rather than something the recipe produces, so the fact this sentence leads with follows from nothing above it.
This word needs a stretch of time with something going on inside it, and the passage names none. The H grades are the recipe shown in one part of the range.

Why this answer

Cover the choices and say what the second sentence does to the first. It takes a general claim about clay and graphite and shows it in one grade, which makes the family specify. The other three die on family alone: an instance of a rule is not one more fact standing beside it, which letter got which recipe follows from nothing, and the passage names no stretch of time for anything to go on inside.

Question

  • Introductory

A glazier does not so much cut sheet glass as persuade it to break where he wants it to. He runs a small steel wheel across the surface once, under even pressure, drawing a shallow scratch from edge to edge. ____ he lays the sheet over a thin batten and presses down on both sides at once, and the glass parts along the scratch.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

The pressing is not an instance of the scoring. It is the other half of the job, and it happens after.
A summarizing word claims the sentence hands you the one before it and takes you no further. This one takes you the whole rest of the way, to the batten and the break.
The two actions are not done alike. One draws a line with a wheel and the other applies force on either side of it.
Correct. The scratch has to exist before the sheet is pressed, and this is the only choice that puts the second action after the first.

Why this answer

The two sentences are consecutive steps, and the order is the point: press first and there is nothing for the break to follow. That makes the family sequence, and After that is the only choice that puts one action later than the other. The three rivals call the pressing an instance of the scoring, a summing-up of it, and a thing done in the same way, and the text gives no ground for any of the three.

Question

  • Introductory

Willow rods spend days in a soaking tank before a basketmaker bends any of them. A soaked rod is supple enough to turn a tight corner without splitting along its length. ____ its bark comes away in one pull, where the bark of a dry rod clings and has to be scraped.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct. Bending without splitting and shedding its bark are two separate things a soaked rod does, and this is the only choice that puts one alongside the other.
Bark coming away in one pull is not a case of a rod being supple. Suppleness is about how far the rod bends before it splits; the bark is a separate thing the soaking leaves behind.
A summarizing word claims the sentence carries you no further than the one before it. This one moves off suppleness onto the bark, which is a different property.
A substituting word wants an option the text has ruled out and something brought on in its place. Nothing here has been ruled out.

Why this answer

Both sentences report something true of a soaked rod, one beside the other, so the family is continue and In addition is the choice. The illustrating word fails because shedding bark is not a case of bending without splitting, the summarizing word because the bark is a further property rather than the same one again, and the substituting word because nothing has been set aside.

Question

  • Introductory

Before a game the ice is not left smooth. A technician walks the sheet swinging a can of water, and the spray freezes into a field of small bumps the players call pebble. A stone rides on the tips of those bumps, so it meets a fraction of the ice a smooth sheet would give it. ____ a gentle push carries it the whole length of the rink.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

The long slide is not a second fact running alongside the small contact area. It is what the small contact area produces.
Nothing is being singled out here. The long slide is not one case of the small contact area; it is what the small contact area produces.
Correct. Less ice touched means less to hold the stone back, so the distance follows directly from the sentence before the blank.
There is nothing to turn against. The long slide is what the pebble is for, not a limit on it.

Why this answer

The sentence before the blank says the stone touches very little ice; the sentence after it says a light push sends the stone the length of the rink. One produces the other, so the family is cause, and Therefore is the only choice that says one produced the other. The three rivals claim a second fact of the same kind, one case singled out of a group, and an opposition, and the text carries none of the three.

Question

  • Introductory

A bandmaster setting out the chairs before a rehearsal is serving two masters, and they do not want the same thing. Players hold a beat by ear, so the ones who must lock together want to sit within earshot of each other. ____ an audience hears a band as a wall of sound with the tune on top, which wants the cornets forward and the basses along the back.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

The first sentence says outright that the two do not want the same thing, so a word claiming the second matches the first is denied by the text.
Correct. The players want to be close and the audience wants them spread out, which is one demand set against the other.
What an audience hears does not follow from what the players need. Neither demand produces the other; they simply pull in opposite directions.
A restating word claims the sentence goes no further than the one before it. This one leaves the players entirely and takes up the audience.

Why this answer

The opening sentence says outright that the two demands do not agree, and the two sentences that follow are those demands, one each. That is plain opposition, so the family is contrast. The other three would each need the second demand to agree with the first, to follow from it, or to be it again in fewer words.

Question

  • Introductory

The rules a town meeting runs on are not there to keep anybody quiet. They exist so that the loudest voice in the hall does not become the longest one. A resident who signs the sheet gets three minutes, and the chair cuts her off at three. ____ a selectman who wants the floor signs the same sheet and gets the same three minutes.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

These are two standing rules rather than two moments, so there is no later for this word to name.
This word wants the second case to run opposite to the first. It runs identically, which is the whole point of the sentence.
A summarizing word claims the sentence carries you no further. This one carries you to a second kind of speaker the text had not reached.
Correct. The same rule holding for the next kind of speaker is what this word is for.

Why this answer

The sentence before the blank states the rule for one speaker and the sentence after it states the same rule for another, so the family is continue and the parallel is the whole of what the word has to say, so Likewise is the choice. The three rivals want a later moment, a case that comes out the other way, and a sentence that adds nobody new, and the text gives two standing rules that match.

Question

  • Advanced

The size of a bicycle's wheels tells a rider almost nothing. What matters is gear inches: the teeth on the chainring divided by the teeth on the sprocket, multiplied by the diameter of the wheel. A folding bike with a twenty-inch wheel and a sixty-tooth chainring driving a fifteen-tooth sprocket comes to eighty; so does a tourer with a twenty-eight-inch wheel and a forty-tooth chainring driving a fourteen-tooth sprocket. ____ a shop that sells the folding machine as a town bike and the tourer as a hill bike is selling a difference the gearing does not contain.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct. The two machines meeting at one number is what makes the shop's distinction empty, so the last sentence is the consequence of the one before it.
A restating word claims the sentence takes you no further than the arithmetic did. This one leaves the arithmetic and makes a claim about how the two bikes are sold, which nothing above it mentions.
Nothing turns. The shop's mistake is what the equal numbers lead to, not a case that cuts against them.
The last sentence is not a third case laid beside the two computed ones. It is what those two computations come to.

Why this answer

Two bikes reach the same gear inches, and the sentence after the blank says what follows for anyone selling them apart, so the family is cause. The layer of work is that an equality invites a restating word: read the last sentence and it has left the numbers behind for a claim about a shop, which is further along rather than the same ground again.

Question

  • Standard

A cryptic clue is built in two halves, and the solver's first job is to find the seam. One half is a plain definition of the answer, of the kind an ordinary crossword would give. The other is a set of instructions for assembling that same answer letter by letter, dressed so that they read as sensible English in their own right. ____ the solver is handed the answer twice, once in the open and once in code, and a solution that satisfies only one half is not a solution at all.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

The last sentence is not an instance of the sentence before it. It is both halves of the clue named together.
It is not a third thing standing alongside the two halves. It is what having those two halves amounts to.
Correct. Twice over, once openly and once in code, is the two-halves description of the clue said again in fewer words.
There is no interval here and nothing is happening during it. The text describes how a clue is built, not what a solver does minute by minute.

Why this answer

The sentence after the blank gathers the definition half and the instruction half and says what they amount to, which is the family the sorting step misfiles most often: it shares the whole subject of the sentences before it and reads like one more fact about them. Ask instead whether it takes you further or hands you what you already have in plainer words.

Question

  • Standard

A saddle stitch is worked by hand, two needles on one thread, each needle passing through the hole the other has just left, so every hole is sewn twice. On most of a bag that is more than the seam will ever need: a straight run under no load would hold with half the work. ____ a saddler sews the whole seam that way, because which hole will one day be cut is not knowable in advance, and a stitch locked at every hole loses one hole to a cut where a chainstitch loses the row.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A summarizing word claims the sentence carries you no further. This one brings in the cut, which is the reason and which nothing above it mentions.
Sewing the whole seam is not a second practice resembling the first. It is the same practice, extended past the part that needs it.
The saddler's sequence ended two sentences ago, and the last sentence is not a further step in it.
Correct. The text grants that most of the seam does not need the second pass, then has the saddler make it everywhere anyway, which is a concession.

Why this answer

The sentence before the blank gives a real reason to do less, and the sentence after it does the work everywhere regardless, which is the conceding kind of contrast and what Nevertheless marks. The three rivals want a sentence that carries you no further, a second practice resembling the first, and a further step in a sequence that ended two sentences ago.

Question

  • Standard

Two stamps can share a design, a color and a value and still not be the same stamp. Collectors tell one printing from another by laying the stamp on a gauge and counting how many perforation holes fall within two centimeters, a number fixed by whichever comb the press carried that year. The gauge answers questions no amount of looking will. ____ a design reissued a decade after its first run, on a comb of a different pitch, is indistinguishable at arm's length and gauges a full hole apart.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A similarity word needs two things a reader can watch behave alike. There is one claim here and one case of it, which is not a pair.
Correct. The last sentence takes the general claim about what the gauge settles and names the case that shows it.
A restating word claims the sentence goes no further. This one goes as far as a particular reissue, a particular decade and a particular comb.
The contrast in this sentence is inside it, between how the two printings look and how they measure. Nothing is being set against the sentence before the blank.

Why this answer

The sentence before the blank claims the gauge decides what the eye cannot, and the sentence after it is that claim in one case, so the family is specify. The work is in the last sentence's own shape: it holds a contrast of its own, looks identical against gauges a hole apart, and that internal opposition is what pulls a reader toward a contrast word for a relationship that is nothing of the kind. The other two rivals want a second case running the same way as the first, and a sentence that goes no further than the one before it, and the reissue goes as far as a decade and a comb.

Question

  • Advanced

A pattern is written in stitches and a body is measured in inches, and the two meet only if the knitter's stitches come out the size the designer's did. A pattern names its gauge before it names anything else, and a careful knitter works a small square before casting on. A square giving twenty-two stitches across four inches where the pattern asked for twenty was knitted tighter than the designer knitted. ____ that knitter has to abandon the needle size printed at the top of the pattern, which is the one instruction in it that was only ever a guess about somebody else's hands.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct. Tighter stitches are smaller stitches, so a bigger needle is what the count forces, and the last sentence is what the one before it produces.
Throwing away a printed instruction sounds like a reversal, and nothing is reversed here: it is the pattern's own gauge that sends the knitter to a different needle.
Changing needles is not an instance of a square coming out tight. It is what a knitter does about one.
A summarizing word claims the sentence carries you no further. This one moves off the square altogether and onto what the knitter must now do.

Why this answer

The measured square produces the decision about needles, so the family is cause and Hence is the choice. The layer of work is that the consequence is a printed instruction being discarded, which reads as a turn against the pattern when it is the pattern being obeyed. The other two rivals want the needle change to be a case of the square coming out tight, and to be the square's own sentence again in fewer words.

Question

  • Standard

An understudy's week has two halves. She learns the lead in full and rehearses it on a schedule of its own, in an empty house, with the stage manager reading in the other roles. ____ she watches the show she is covering from the back of the house most nights, with a notebook open on her knee.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A night spent sitting at the back sounds like the opposite of working, which is the whole pull of this choice. The text is calling it the second half of the week.
No sentence above the blank produces this one. The text sets the two halves of the week side by side and never makes either of them bring on the other.
Correct. Two halves of the same week, the second standing beside the first rather than coming out of it.
A summarizing word claims the sentence stops where the one before it stopped. This one goes on to a second duty the rehearsing sentence never reached.

Why this answer

The opening sentence counts two halves and the two that follow are those halves, neither of them produced by the other, so the family is continue and Moreover is the choice. The pull is toward a contrast, because a night at the back of the house reads as the opposite of the rehearsing described before it, and a turn is easy to take when the relationship has not been named first. The other two rivals want the rehearsing to have produced the watching, and want the sentence to stop where the rehearsing sentence stopped.

Question

  • Standard

A feature arrived at a cinema on six or seven reels, and a projectionist ran it on two machines in turn. Near the end of a reel a small dot appears in the corner of the frame; another follows about seven seconds later. The first dot is not the changeover. It tells the projectionist to start the motor on the second machine, which has stood threaded and still since the house lights went down. ____ the first machine keeps running and the audience sees nothing. The second dot is the one that throws the picture across.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A restating word claims the sentence takes you no further. This one adds what the audience is seeing, which nothing above it reports.
Correct. The seven seconds between the two dots are an interval, and this is what is happening in the auditorium while they run.
Two machines turning at once feels like a tension, and the text removed it two sentences before the blank: The first dot is not the changeover. Nothing here departs from what you were told to expect.
Nothing is being singled out. The first machine carrying on is not one case of the second machine's motor starting.

Why this answer

The text has just named a stretch of time, the seven seconds between the dots, and the sentence after the blank says what is going on during it, so the family is sequence and Meanwhile is the choice. The trap is that a second machine starting while the first still runs reads as opposition, when running together for seven seconds is the whole design and the text has said so. The other two rivals want the sentence to hand back what you already had, and to single out a case of the motor starting.

Question

  • Advanced

The shelf beside a self-checkout is a scale, and after every scan it checks that the weight on it climbs by what the item just scanned should weigh. A shopper who scans a tin and drops it back in the trolley trips it. So does one who lifts a bag of apples onto the shelf without scanning them. So does one who sets down a handbag while hunting for a card, and so does one whose own bag was never declared at the start. ____ the machine is not watching for dishonesty at all. It is watching for a disagreement between what has been scanned and what is on the shelf, and it cannot tell an innocent disagreement from any other kind.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

The last two sentences are not a fifth case in the list. They are what the four cases come to.
The four cases are not a run of events in order, so there is no moment after them for this word to name.
The flat denial sounds like a reversal, and a contrast word needs two things that come out differently. There is one machine here and one account of it; no sentence above ever claimed it was watching for dishonesty.
Correct. Four particular cases are gathered up and named as one thing, which is the same account in fewer words.

Why this answer

Four cases are listed and the two sentences after the blank say what they have in common, which is those four again at a coarser grain, so the family is restate and In short is the choice. They use words the text has not used, and a restating word is allowed to: the test is whether it carries you further, not whether it is built from old words. The three rivals want a fifth case running the same way as the four, a moment after them, and a turn against them. The keyed sentence opens on a flat denial, and a denial reads like a turn.

Question

  • Standard

An arrow does not leave a bow straight. The string drives the back of the shaft forward before the point has moved, so the shaft buckles sideways, springs back, and is still swinging when it passes the riser; spine is the name for how much it gives under that shove. An archer who calls a shaft stiff is never saying anything about the shaft alone. ____ one aluminum shaft can be right for a fifty-pound bow, bending just enough to slide past the riser, and too stiff for a thirty-pound bow, whose gentler push never bends it far enough and leaves it coming off the string pointing wide.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct. The claim above the blank is that stiffness is a match rather than a property, and one shaft on both sides of the word is that claim in a case.
A restating word claims the sentence carries you no further. This one brings in two bows, two draw weights and a shaft that behaves differently on each.
A similarity word needs two things behaving alike. The last sentence is the claim happening once, not a second thing that runs the way the claim does.
This word puts the illustration earlier than the claim it illustrates, and the text gives no order between the two. The shaft leaving the bow is a sequence; the claim and its case are not.

Why this answer

The sentence before the blank makes a general claim, that stiffness is relational, and the sentence after it is that claim in one shaft, so the family is specify and For instance is the choice. The three rivals want the sentence to carry you no further, to lay a second thing running the way the claim does, and to come earlier in time than it. The interference is that the illustration has two bows in it and a comparison of its own, so a reader who goes to the choices first sorts on the numbers rather than on the relationship the blank marks.

Question

  • Advanced

Feather shuttlecocks are sold in tubes marked with a number, and the trade calls that number the speed. Cold air is dense and holds a shuttle back, so a cold hall is issued a higher grade, built to carry further, and a warm hall a lower one. The word does the damage: ordering seventy-eights in January sounds like a club choosing a quicker game. ____ the grade is a correction and not a preference, and the whole apparatus exists so that the same stroke from the same player finishes in the same place in January as in July.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

An illustrating word needs the sentence after it to bear out the one before. This one puts right the impression the sentence before it describes.
It does not agree with the sentence before it. It denies the impression that sentence says the word creates.
Correct. The sentence before the blank reports what the word speed makes the practice sound like, and this one says what is really going on.
January and July sit inside the last sentence rather than between the two sentences, so the blank has no later moment to mark.

Why this answer

The sentence before the blank reports the impression the word speed creates, and the sentence after it denies that impression and says what is so, which is the correcting kind of contrast. An impression the text has just created is one of the two things the page says a correction can follow. In fact is not fixed to correcting; here it is the sentence that puts something right. The three rivals want the sentence to bear out the one before it, to agree with it, and to come after it in time, and it does none of the three.

Question

  • Standard

A control on an orienteering course is a nylon marker hung on a fence corner or a boulder, and a runner is meant to visit twenty of them, alone and out of sight of everyone. Posting a marshal at each one is not an option: the club that lays a course on a Sunday morning has fifty runners and eleven volunteers, and the volunteers are wanted at the start and the finish. ____ every control carries a small pin punch whose pattern of holes belongs to that control and no other, and the card a runner hands in at the finish is read box by box before a time is allowed to stand.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

A summarizing word claims the sentence goes no further than the staffing problem. This one leaves that problem behind and describes the system that answers it.
Correct. The sentence before the blank rules an option out and this one brings on what takes its place, which is what this word is for.
A punch is not an instance of a staffing problem.
This word marks the last of a run of steps. The sentences the blank joins are a staffing problem and the system that answers it, which is no run of steps.

Why this answer

The sentence before the blank rejects an option outright and the sentence after it supplies the replacement, which is the substituting kind of contrast and what Instead marks. The three rivals want the sentence to stop where the staffing problem stopped, to be a case of that problem, and to be the last of a series of steps, and no series runs between the two sentences the blank joins.

Question

  • Advanced

Clay carries very little of its own weight, and a modeled figure much above knee height will sag off its own legs before the modeling is finished. So a modeler builds over an armature: aluminum wire twisted around a steel back-iron, wrapped at intervals with more wire so the clay has something to grip. ____ the wire holds whatever the hands leave on it, so a pose survives the night between sessions, which matters more to somebody working from life than the sagging ever does. A limb held up by clay alone is a limb that has moved by morning.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

Correct. Holding a pose overnight is a second service the same wire performs, standing beside the first rather than coming out of it.
The clause ranking one service above the other reads like a turn, and nothing is set against anything: both sentences say why a modeler wants an armature.
Holding a pose is not an instance of wire twisted around a back-iron. It is a second thing that arrangement does.
The sentence before the blank fixes no moment, so this word has nothing to point back to. The two sentences give two reasons for an armature, not two points in time.

Why this answer

Both sentences give a reason a modeler wants an armature and neither reason produces the other, so the family is continue and What is more is the choice. The steer is inside the keyed sentence: it ranks this benefit above the first, and a ranking sounds like a turn. The three rivals want something set against the armature, a case of the wire twisted around the back-iron, and an earlier moment to point back to.

Question

  • Advanced

A dead-end water main has nowhere for its water to go, so the chlorine in it fades and rust and silt settle along the bottom of the pipe. A crew clears one by working outward from the reservoir end, opening a single hydrant at a time so that the water arriving behind them always comes from a length they have already cleaned. Each hydrant is eased open rather than thrown wide, because a column of water brought up short knocks the pipe about, and is then left running hard, since only a fast current will carry the loosened rust out instead of letting it settle again. ____ the crew draws a sample at the last hydrant on the line and holds the run open until its chlorine reading matches the reading at the plant.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

The sentence names what the whole job was for, which is why it reads like a summing-up. It is another thing the crew does, and it carries the run past every step above it.
A substituting word wants an option dropped and a replacement brought on in its place. The text does reject throwing a hydrant wide, but what it puts in place of that is easing the same hydrant open, not drawing a sample at the last one.
Nothing turns. The sampling finishes the procedure the sentences before it have been laying out.
Correct. The text is walking through a procedure in order, and this is its last step.

Why this answer

The text runs a procedure step by step and the sentence after the blank is the step that ends it, so the family is sequence and Finally is the choice. The difficulty is that this particular step states what the whole run was for, so it wears the clothes of a summary while doing the work of an action. The three rivals want a summing-up, an option the text ruled out, and a turn, and every sentence from the second on sets out a step the crew works through in order.

Question

  • Advanced

A time control written as fifteen plus ten does not give a player fifteen minutes and a bit. The ten is an increment: the clock adds ten seconds each time a player completes a move and presses the clock, and it adds nothing at any other time. ____ the seconds a player collects are earned by finishing moves and never by thinking about them. A clock down to four seconds will hand ten back, over and over, to anybody willing to keep moving, and nothing at all to a player who wants to sit and think.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?

The sentence is built out of a denial, which sounds like an opposition. It is the rule above it turned around, not a case that comes out differently.
Earning seconds by finishing and not by thinking is not one case of the adding rule. It is that rule stated from the other side.
Correct. Ten seconds added on a completed move is the same rule said again, this time as the thing the clock will not add for.
The two sentences are one rule twice over, not two moments with something happening between them.

Why this answer

The sentence after the blank carries no fact the rule above it did not already carry; it states the same rule from the other side, as the thing the clock will not add for, so the family is restate and That is is the choice. The interference is that a rule stated as a prohibition reads as an opposition, and the word never is doing that work in the middle of it. The other two rivals want a case of the adding rule and an interval with something happening inside it.
